# Geomorphology and Earth’s Interior

## Introduction to Geomorphology

Geomorphology is the scientific study of landforms, their origin, evolution, and the processes that shape the Earth’s surface. For UPSC, understanding the internal structure of the Earth, the forces that create and modify landforms (endogenic and exogenic), and the major landform types is foundational for both physical geography of India and the world.

## Interior of the Earth

Direct observation of the Earth’s interior is limited to a few kilometres (deepest mines and boreholes). Knowledge comes mainly from indirect evidence: seismic waves, density calculations, meteorite composition, and volcanic materials.

### Sources of Information

- **Seismic Waves**: Primary (P) waves are compressional and travel through solids and liquids. Secondary (S) waves are shear waves and travel only through solids. The behaviour of these waves (refraction, reflection, disappearance) reveals internal boundaries.
- **Density and Gravity**: Average density of Earth is about 5.5 g/cm³; surface rocks are lighter (~2.7 g/cm³), implying denser material inside.
- **Volcanic Eruptions and Magma**: Provide samples of material from the upper mantle and crust.
- **Meteorites**: Assumed to represent early solar system materials similar to Earth’s building blocks.

### Structure of the Earth

The Earth is divided into three main concentric layers:

**1. Crust**
- Outermost solid layer.
- Continental crust: thicker (average 30–40 km, up to 70 km under mountains), composition rich in silica and aluminium (sial), density ~2.7 g/cm³.
- Oceanic crust: thinner (5–10 km), composition rich in silica and magnesium (sima), denser (~3.0 g/cm³).
- The boundary between crust and mantle is the Mohorovičić discontinuity (Moho).

**2. Mantle**
- Extends from Moho to about 2,900 km depth.
- Upper mantle includes the asthenosphere (partially molten, plastic layer that allows plate movement) and the lithospheric mantle.
- Lower mantle is more rigid.
- Composition: mainly silicate minerals rich in iron and magnesium.
- The mantle accounts for the bulk of Earth’s volume.

**3. Core**
- Outer core: liquid, extends from ~2,900 km to ~5,150 km. Composition mainly iron and nickel. The liquid outer core is responsible for Earth’s magnetic field through the dynamo effect.
- Inner core: solid, from ~5,150 km to the centre (6,371 km). Also iron-nickel, solid due to immense pressure despite high temperature.
- The boundary between mantle and core is the Gutenberg discontinuity. The boundary between outer and inner core is the Lehmann discontinuity.

### Temperature, Pressure and Density

Temperature increases with depth (geothermal gradient). At the core, temperatures are estimated at 5,000–6,000°C or higher. Pressure and density also increase dramatically towards the centre.

## Endogenic and Exogenic Forces

Landforms are the result of the interaction between internal (endogenic) and external (exogenic) forces.

### Endogenic Forces

These originate from within the Earth and are primarily driven by heat from radioactive decay and residual heat from formation.

- **Diastrophic Forces**: Slow movements.
  - Epeirogenic: vertical movements causing uplift or subsidence of large areas (continent-building).
  - Orogenic: horizontal movements causing folding and mountain building (e.g., Himalayas).
- **Sudden Forces**: Earthquakes and volcanism.

Endogenic forces create the initial irregularities of the Earth’s surface – mountains, plateaus, rift valleys, etc.

### Exogenic Forces

These operate on the surface and are driven mainly by solar energy and gravity.

- **Weathering**: In-situ breakdown of rocks (physical, chemical, biological).
- **Erosion and Transportation**: Removal and movement of weathered material by agents – running water (fluvial), wind (aeolian), glaciers (glacial), waves (coastal), groundwater (karst).
- **Deposition**: Laying down of transported material, creating depositional landforms.

Exogenic processes generally work to level the surface (gradation), wearing down highlands and filling lowlands.

## Plate Tectonics – Brief Framework

The lithosphere is broken into large plates that move over the asthenosphere. Plate boundaries are of three types:

- **Divergent**: Plates move apart (mid-ocean ridges, rift valleys). New crust is formed.
- **Convergent**: Plates collide. Oceanic-continental (subduction, volcanic arcs), oceanic-oceanic (island arcs), continental-continental (fold mountains like Himalayas).
- **Transform**: Plates slide past each other (e.g., San Andreas Fault). Earthquakes common, little volcanism.

Plate tectonics explains the distribution of earthquakes, volcanoes, mountain ranges, and the configuration of continents and oceans.

## Major Landform Types – Conceptual Overview

**Mountains**:
- Fold mountains (Himalayas, Alps) – formed by compression.
- Block mountains (Vosges, Black Forest) – formed by faulting.
- Volcanic mountains (Mt. Fuji, Mt. Kilimanjaro).
- Residual mountains – remnants after erosion.

**Plateaus**: Elevated flat or undulating surfaces. Can be intermontane (Tibet), piedmont, or continental. Often rich in minerals.

**Plains**: Formed mainly by deposition (alluvial plains of North India) or erosion. Most suitable for dense human settlement and agriculture.

**Other features**: Rift valleys, volcanic landforms (shield volcanoes, composite cones, calderas), coastal landforms (cliffs, beaches, spits, lagoons), glacial landforms (cirques, moraines, fjords), arid landforms (sand dunes, playas, pediments).

## Geomorphic Processes in the Indian Context – Key Points

- The Himalayas are young fold mountains still rising due to the ongoing collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates.
- The Peninsular Plateau is an ancient stable block (part of Gondwana) with residual hills, rift valleys (Narmada, Tapi), and Deccan Trap volcanic province.
- The Indo-Gangetic Plain is a vast alluvial plain formed by deposition in the foreland basin south of the Himalayas.
- Coastal plains of India show both depositional (east coast, wider) and erosional/narrow features (west coast in parts).
- Western disturbances, monsoon, and glacial processes in the Himalayas continue to shape the northern landscape.

## Importance for UPSC

Questions often test:
- Structure of the Earth and discontinuities
- Difference between endogenic and exogenic processes
- Types of mountains and their formation
- Application to Indian physiography (why Himalayas are prone to earthquakes, nature of Peninsular rivers vs Himalayan rivers, etc.)
- Basic plate tectonics in the context of the Indian plate’s northward movement

A clear conceptual grasp of these foundations allows accurate interpretation of maps, diagrams, and applied questions on natural hazards, resource distribution, and regional geography.
