# State Executive: Governor and Chief Minister

## The Governor: Constitutional Position

The Governor is the constitutional head of a State. Article 153 provides that there shall be a Governor for each State. The same person can be appointed as Governor for two or more States. The Governor is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal. Unlike the President, who is elected, the Governor is a nominated executive head.

The office of Governor is not an employment under the Central Government. It is an independent constitutional office. The Governor is not a servant of the Union Government, though he is appointed by the President and holds office during the pleasure of the President.

## Appointment, Tenure and Qualifications

There is no prescribed procedure or eligibility criteria in the Constitution beyond citizenship of India and completion of 35 years of age. By convention, the Governor is appointed after consultation with the Chief Minister of the concerned State, though this convention has not always been followed. Usually a person from outside the State is preferred, and active politicians of the ruling party at the Centre are often appointed.

The Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President. There is no fixed term, though the normal term is five years. He may resign by writing to the President. The President may transfer a Governor from one State to another. There is no impeachment process for the Governor. He can be removed by the President at any time.

## Powers and Functions of the Governor

The powers of the Governor can be classified into executive, legislative, financial and judicial powers. In addition, the Governor has certain discretionary powers which distinguish the office from that of the President.

### Executive Powers

All executive action of the State is taken in the name of the Governor. He appoints the Chief Minister and, on the advice of the Chief Minister, other ministers. He allocates portfolios among ministers. He can ask the Chief Minister to furnish information relating to the administration of the State and proposals for legislation. He can require the Chief Minister to submit for the consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by a minister but which has not been considered by the Council.

The Governor appoints the Advocate General, the State Election Commissioner, the Chairman and members of the State Public Service Commission, and the Vice-Chancellors of State universities in many cases. He is the chancellor of most State universities.

### Legislative Powers

The Governor is a part of the State legislature. He summons, prorogues and dissolves the Legislative Assembly. He addresses the legislature at the commencement of the first session after each general election and at the beginning of the first session of each year. He can send messages to the legislature.

When a bill is passed by the State legislature it is presented to the Governor for assent. The Governor may give assent, withhold assent, return the bill for reconsideration (if it is not a money bill), or reserve the bill for the consideration of the President. Reservation is mandatory in certain cases, such as bills that endanger the position of the High Court, and discretionary in others.

The Governor can promulgate ordinances when the legislature is not in session. An ordinance has the same force as a law passed by the legislature but must be approved by the legislature within six weeks of reassembly.

### Financial Powers

A money bill can be introduced in the State legislature only on the recommendation of the Governor. The Governor causes the annual financial statement (budget) to be laid before the legislature. No demand for grant can be made except on his recommendation. He can make advances out of the Contingency Fund of the State to meet unforeseen expenditure.

### Judicial Powers

The Governor has the power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites and remissions of punishment or to suspend, remit or commute the sentence of any person convicted of any offence against a law relating to a matter to which the executive power of the State extends. This power is co-extensive with the executive power of the State and does not extend to sentences by court martial or to death sentences in the same manner as the President’s power under Article 72.

### Discretionary Powers

Unlike the President, the Governor has certain areas where he can act in his discretion. These include:

- Reservation of a bill for the consideration of the President
- Recommendation for the imposition of President’s Rule under Article 356
- Appointment of the Chief Minister when no party has a clear majority
- Dismissal of a ministry that has lost the confidence of the Assembly but refuses to resign
- Dissolution of the Assembly when the ministry has lost majority

The Constitution also provides for special responsibilities of the Governors of certain States such as Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nagaland, Manipur, Sikkim and Arunachal Pradesh in relation to particular regions or tribal areas.

## The Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers

The real executive authority in a State is exercised by the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister. Article 163 provides that there shall be a Council of Ministers with the Chief Minister at the head to aid and advise the Governor in the exercise of his functions, except in so far as he is by or under the Constitution required to exercise his functions or any of them in his discretion.

### Appointment of the Chief Minister

The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor. Normally the leader of the majority party in the Legislative Assembly is appointed. When no party has a clear majority the Governor has to exercise discretion and may invite the leader of the single largest party or a person who can demonstrate majority support through letters or a floor test.

Other ministers are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister. The total number of ministers, including the Chief Minister, in a State shall not exceed 15 per cent of the total number of members of the Legislative Assembly of that State. This limit was introduced by the 91st Amendment Act, 2003. For smaller Assemblies the minimum number is 12.

### Powers and Position of the Chief Minister

The Chief Minister is the head of the government in the State. He recommends persons for appointment as ministers and allocates portfolios. He can ask a minister to resign or advise the Governor to dismiss a minister. He presides over meetings of the Council of Ministers and coordinates the work of different departments. He is the principal channel of communication between the Governor and the Council of Ministers. He advises the Governor on the summoning and prorogation of the Assembly and on its dissolution.

The Chief Minister is the leader of the party in power and therefore exercises great political influence. He represents the State in interactions with the Central Government and other States.

### Collective Responsibility

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly of the State. This means that the ministry stays in office only as long as it enjoys the confidence of the majority in the Assembly. If a no-confidence motion is passed, the entire ministry has to resign. Individual responsibility of ministers also exists; a minister can be removed by the Chief Minister.

## Relationship between Governor and Chief Minister

In normal circumstances the Governor acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers. The relationship is modelled on the British parliamentary system. However, the existence of discretionary powers and the fact that the Governor is appointed by the Centre have sometimes led to tensions, especially when different parties are in power at the Centre and in the State. Judicial decisions have clarified that the Governor’s discretion is limited and must be exercised in accordance with constitutional principles and conventions.

## Key Points for Memory

- Governor: constitutional head of State, appointed by President, holds office during pleasure
- Qualifications: citizen of India, 35 years of age
- Powers: executive, legislative, financial, judicial; plus discretionary powers
- Chief Minister: real executive head, appointed by Governor, leader of majority party
- Council of Ministers collectively responsible to Legislative Assembly
- Limit on size of ministry: 15 per cent of Assembly strength (minimum 12)
- Governor can reserve bills for President, recommend President’s Rule
- Ordinance making power of Governor similar to President
- Chief Minister is the link between Governor and Council of Ministers

The State executive operates on the parliamentary model. The Governor is the formal head while the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers exercise real power, subject to the confidence of the Legislative Assembly and the overall framework of the Constitution.
