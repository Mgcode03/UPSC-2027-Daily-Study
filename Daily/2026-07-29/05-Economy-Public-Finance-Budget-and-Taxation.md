# Public Finance: Budget and Taxation

## Meaning of Public Finance

Public finance deals with the income and expenditure of public authorities. In the context of India it primarily concerns the finances of the Central Government, State Governments and local bodies. The main components are public revenue, public expenditure, public debt and financial administration. The annual budget is the principal instrument through which the government presents its estimates of revenue and expenditure and outlines its fiscal policy.

## The Union Budget

The Union Budget is the annual financial statement of the Central Government. Article 112 of the Constitution requires the President to cause to be laid before both Houses of Parliament a statement of the estimated receipts and expenditure of the Government of India for that year. The budget is presented by the Finance Minister, usually on the first of February, so that it can be passed before the beginning of the new financial year on 1 April.

### Components of the Budget

The budget contains:

- Estimates of revenue and capital receipts
- Ways and means to raise the revenue
- Details of expenditure
- A review of economic conditions and fiscal policy

Receipts are divided into revenue receipts and capital receipts. Revenue receipts include tax revenue and non-tax revenue (interest, dividends, fees, etc.). Capital receipts include recoveries of loans, borrowings and other liabilities, and receipts from disinvestment.

Expenditure is classified into revenue expenditure and capital expenditure. Revenue expenditure does not create assets (salaries, interest payments, subsidies, pensions). Capital expenditure leads to creation of assets or reduction of liabilities (loans to States, investment in shares, infrastructure spending).

The budget also shows the revenue deficit, fiscal deficit and primary deficit.

- Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure – revenue receipts
- Fiscal deficit = total expenditure – (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts)
- Primary deficit = fiscal deficit – interest payments

Fiscal deficit indicates the total borrowing requirement of the government.

### Budget Process

The preparation of the budget begins several months in advance. Ministries submit estimates. The Finance Ministry consolidates them. After presentation, the budget is discussed in Parliament. Demands for grants are voted by the Lok Sabha. The Finance Bill (containing tax proposals) and the Appropriation Bill (authorising expenditure) are passed. After the President’s assent the budget becomes operative.

## Taxation in India

Taxes are compulsory contributions imposed by public authorities to meet public expenditure. Taxes are classified into direct and indirect taxes.

### Direct Taxes

Direct taxes are those whose burden cannot be shifted. The most important direct taxes in India are:

- Personal Income Tax: levied on the income of individuals, Hindu Undivided Families, and certain other entities. The tax is progressive, with higher rates for higher income slabs. Agricultural income is exempt from Central income tax.

- Corporation Tax: levied on the profits of companies. It is a major source of revenue for the Centre.

- Capital Gains Tax: levied on profits from the sale of capital assets.

- Securities Transaction Tax and other smaller direct taxes.

The Income Tax Act and related rules govern the assessment and collection of these taxes. The Central Board of Direct Taxes administers direct taxes.

### Indirect Taxes

Indirect taxes are those whose burden can be shifted to another person. The major reform in indirect taxation was the introduction of the Goods and Services Tax (GST) on 1 July 2017.

GST is a comprehensive destination-based tax on the supply of goods and services. It subsumed a large number of Central and State taxes such as Central Excise, Service Tax, VAT, Entry Tax, Octroi and others. GST has four main components:

- Central GST (CGST)
- State GST (SGST) or Union Territory GST (UTGST)
- Integrated GST (IGST) for inter-State supplies
- GST Compensation Cess on certain luxury and demerit goods

GST is administered through a dual structure with the GST Council as the apex decision-making body. The Council is chaired by the Union Finance Minister and includes State Finance Ministers. Decisions are taken by a three-fourths majority, with the Centre having one-third weight and the States collectively two-thirds.

Other residual indirect taxes include customs duties on imports and exports (though most export duties have been abolished) and some State taxes outside GST such as stamp duty and excise on alcohol.

## Principles of Taxation

A good tax system is guided by certain principles:

- Equity: ability to pay (progressive taxation) and benefit principle
- Certainty: clear rules regarding amount, time and manner of payment
- Convenience: easy for the taxpayer to pay
- Economy: low cost of collection
- Productivity: adequate yield
- Elasticity: ability to expand or contract with economic conditions
- Simplicity and transparency

India’s tax system has moved towards greater reliance on direct taxes and a simplified indirect tax structure through GST, though challenges of compliance, evasion and the tax base remain.

## Fiscal Policy and FRBM

Fiscal policy refers to the use of government revenue and expenditure to influence the economy. The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act, 2003 aims to ensure inter-generational equity in fiscal management, long-term macroeconomic stability, and transparency. It sets targets for reduction of fiscal and revenue deficits. Amendments and escape clauses have been used during periods of economic stress such as the global financial crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Key Points for Memory

- Budget: annual financial statement under Article 112
- Revenue receipts vs capital receipts; revenue expenditure vs capital expenditure
- Fiscal deficit = total expenditure – (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts)
- Direct taxes: income tax, corporation tax (burden not shifted)
- Indirect taxes: GST (destination-based, dual structure)
- GST Council: Centre one-third weight, States two-thirds; three-fourths majority
- GST launched 1 July 2017; subsumed most indirect taxes
- FRBM Act: targets for fiscal discipline
- Progressive taxation: higher rates on higher incomes
- Agricultural income exempt from Central income tax

Public finance is central to the functioning of the government and to macroeconomic management. Understanding the budget, deficits and the tax structure is essential for analysing government policy and economic developments in India.
