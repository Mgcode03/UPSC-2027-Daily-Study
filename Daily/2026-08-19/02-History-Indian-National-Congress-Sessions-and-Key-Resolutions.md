# Indian National Congress Sessions and Key Resolutions

## Foundation and Early Years of the Indian National Congress

The Indian National Congress (INC) was founded on 28 December 1885 at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College in Bombay. The initiative came from Allan Octavian Hume, a retired British civil servant, who believed that a platform for educated Indians to express their aspirations would prevent unrest. The first session was presided over by Womesh Chunder Bonnerjee and attended by 72 delegates.

The early Congress (1885–1905) is often described as the Moderate phase. Leaders such as Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjea, and Dinshaw Wacha dominated. Their methods were constitutional: petitions, prayers, protests, and public meetings. They demanded greater Indian representation in legislatures and civil services, reduction of military expenditure, and economic reforms.

## Important Early Sessions and Resolutions

### 1885 – Bombay (W.C. Bonnerjee)
The first session laid down the objectives: promotion of friendly relations among political workers, development of national unity, recording of the opinions of educated Indians, and determination of lines of future action. Demands included expansion of the Legislative Councils and holding of civil service examinations simultaneously in India and England.

### 1886 – Calcutta (Dadabhai Naoroji)
Naoroji’s presidential address emphasised the economic drain theory. The session saw increased participation and the beginning of the demand for representative government.

### 1896 – Calcutta (Rahimtulla M. Sayani)
The Congress condemned the oppressive forest laws and the salt tax. It also discussed the famine and the need for agricultural reforms.

### 1905 – Benares (Gopal Krishna Gokhale)
Held against the backdrop of the Partition of Bengal. Gokhale’s address was a masterly exposition of Moderate politics. The session supported the Swadeshi movement but remained cautious about boycott.

## The Surat Split and the Extremist Phase (1905–1918)

The Partition of Bengal (1905) radicalised a section of Congress leadership. Leaders like Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai, and Aurobindo Ghosh advocated more assertive methods — Swadeshi, boycott, national education, and passive resistance. They came to be known as Extremists or Nationalists.

### 1906 – Calcutta (Dadabhai Naoroji)
The session adopted the goal of “Swaraj” (self-government) for the first time. Naoroji declared Swaraj as the aim of the Congress. The four-fold programme of Swadeshi, boycott, national education, and self-government was endorsed.

### 1907 – Surat (Rash Behari Ghosh)
The session witnessed a dramatic split between Moderates and Extremists. Tilak’s supporters clashed with the Moderate leadership over the election of the President and the resolution on Swaraj. The Extremists were expelled. The split weakened the national movement temporarily.

### 1916 – Lucknow (Ambica Charan Mazumdar)
This session is memorable for two major developments:
1. Reunion of Moderates and Extremists.
2. Lucknow Pact between Congress and the Muslim League. The Pact accepted separate electorates and proposed a scheme of reforms with greater Indian participation.

The Lucknow Pact marked a rare moment of Hindu-Muslim political unity.

## Gandhian Era and Mass Movements (1919–1947)

Mahatma Gandhi transformed the Congress into a mass organisation. Sessions during this period reflected the changing strategies of the freedom struggle.

### 1919 – Amritsar (Motilal Nehru)
Held in the aftermath of the Jallianwala Bagh massacre. The session condemned the atrocity and the Rowlatt Act. Gandhi’s leadership was consolidating.

### 1920 – Calcutta (Special Session) and Nagpur (Regular Session)
The Calcutta special session (September 1920) endorsed Gandhi’s Non-Cooperation programme. The Nagpur session (December 1920) under C. Vijayaraghavachariar ratified it fully. The Congress creed was changed from “colonial self-government” to “attainment of Swaraj by peaceful and legitimate means.” The organisational structure was overhauled: Provincial Congress Committees were reorganised on a linguistic basis, and a Working Committee of 15 members was created.

### 1924 – Belgaum (Mahatma Gandhi)
The only session presided over by Gandhi. He emphasised constructive work — khadi, Hindu-Muslim unity, removal of untouchability, and prohibition.

### 1927 – Madras (M.A. Ansari)
The session resolved that the goal of the Congress was complete independence (Purna Swaraj). It also decided to boycott the Simon Commission.

### 1928 – Calcutta (Motilal Nehru)
The Nehru Report (drafted by Motilal Nehru) was discussed. It proposed dominion status as the immediate goal. Younger leaders led by Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose demanded complete independence. The session gave the British government a one-year ultimatum to accept dominion status, failing which the Congress would launch a mass movement for complete independence.

### 1929 – Lahore (Jawaharlal Nehru)
A landmark session. The Congress declared Purna Swaraj (complete independence) as its goal. 26 January 1930 was fixed as the first Independence Day. The session authorised the Working Committee to launch Civil Disobedience. The tricolour flag was hoisted by Jawaharlal Nehru on the banks of the Ravi.

### 1931 – Karachi (Vallabhbhai Patel)
Held after the Gandhi-Irwin Pact. The session is famous for the resolution on Fundamental Rights and the National Economic Programme. It guaranteed basic civil liberties, equality, and a set of economic rights including living wages, limited hours of work, and state ownership of key industries. This resolution became a precursor to the Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles in the Constitution.

### 1934 – Bombay (Rajendra Prasad)
The Congress was reorganised after the Civil Disobedience Movement. The session adopted a new constitution of the Congress.

### 1936 – Lucknow (Jawaharlal Nehru) and Faizpur (Jawaharlal Nehru)
Nehru’s presidential addresses emphasised socialism and the need for agrarian reforms. The Faizpur session (1936) was the first to be held in a rural area, highlighting the Congress’s growing contact with the peasantry.

### 1938 – Haripura (Subhas Chandra Bose)
Bose was elected President. The session prioritised national planning and industrialisation. The National Planning Committee was set up under Nehru.

### 1939 – Tripuri (Subhas Chandra Bose)
Bose was re-elected against Gandhi’s preferred candidate Pattabhi Sitaramayya. Differences over the future course of the movement and Bose’s desire for an immediate mass struggle led to his resignation. Rajendra Prasad took over as President. The Forward Bloc was later formed by Bose.

### 1940 – Ramgarh (Abul Kalam Azad)
The session prepared for the Individual Satyagraha. Azad remained President till 1946, providing continuity during the critical war years.

### 1946 – Meerut (J.B. Kripalani)
The last session before independence. The Congress accepted the Cabinet Mission Plan with reservations. The session reflected the complex negotiations leading to independence and partition.

## Key Themes Across Congress Sessions

### Evolution of the Goal
- 1885–1905: Colonial self-government within the Empire.
- 1906: Swaraj (self-government).
- 1920: Swaraj by peaceful and legitimate means.
- 1929: Purna Swaraj (complete independence).

### Methods of Struggle
- Moderate phase: Constitutional agitation.
- Extremist phase: Swadeshi, boycott, passive resistance.
- Gandhian phase: Non-cooperation, civil disobedience, constructive programme, Quit India.

### Organisational Changes
- 1920 Nagpur session: Linguistic reorganisation of provinces, creation of Working Committee, mass membership.
- Entry of peasants, workers, women, and students transformed the Congress from an elite body into a mass movement.

### Economic and Social Resolutions
- Karachi Resolution (1931): Fundamental Rights and Economic Programme.
- Emphasis on khadi, village industries, removal of untouchability, Hindu-Muslim unity, and later, planning and industrialisation.

## Significant Resolutions Worth Remembering

- 1906 Calcutta: Declaration of Swaraj as the goal.
- 1916 Lucknow: Reunion and Lucknow Pact.
- 1920 Nagpur: Non-Cooperation and organisational overhaul.
- 1929 Lahore: Purna Swaraj resolution; 26 January as Independence Day.
- 1931 Karachi: Resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme.
- 1938 Haripura: National Planning Committee.

## Role of Presidents

Congress Presidents were not merely ceremonial. In the pre-independence period, the President shaped the political direction. Notable Presidents included:

- Dadabhai Naoroji (1886, 1893, 1906)
- Gopal Krishna Gokhale (1905)
- Bal Gangadhar Tilak (influenced but never President)
- Motilal Nehru (1919, 1928)
- Mahatma Gandhi (1924 – only once)
- Jawaharlal Nehru (1929, 1936, 1937, 1946)
- Subhas Chandra Bose (1938, 1939)
- Abul Kalam Azad (1940–1946)

## Legacy of the Congress Sessions

The annual sessions served as the parliament of the nationalist movement. They formulated demands, debated strategies, resolved internal differences, and communicated the national will to the British and the Indian public. Resolutions on rights, equality, planning, and social reform influenced the Constitution of independent India. The sessions also trained a generation of leaders in democratic debate, compromise, and mass mobilisation.

The history of Congress sessions is therefore not merely a chronicle of meetings; it is the story of the evolution of Indian nationalism from elite petitioning to mass struggle, from dominion status to complete independence, and from political freedom to the vision of a just social and economic order.

## Key Facts for Quick Revision

- First session: 1885, Bombay, W.C. Bonnerjee.
- First mention of Swaraj: 1906, Calcutta, Dadabhai Naoroji.
- Surat Split: 1907.
- Lucknow Pact and reunion: 1916.
- Non-Cooperation endorsed: 1920, Nagpur.
- Purna Swaraj: 1929, Lahore, Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Fundamental Rights Resolution: 1931, Karachi.
- Only session presided by Gandhi: 1924, Belgaum.
- First rural session: 1936, Faizpur.
- Bose’s re-election and resignation: 1939, Tripuri.

This chronological and thematic understanding of Congress sessions provides a clear framework for analysing the phases of the Indian national movement.
