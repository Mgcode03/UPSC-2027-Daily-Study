# CSAT Foundations: Percentages, Ratios and Data Interpretation

## Part A: Percentages

### Basic Concept

A percentage is a fraction with denominator 100. The symbol % means “per hundred.”

- x% = x/100
- To convert a fraction to a percentage: multiply by 100.
- To convert a percentage to a fraction: divide by 100 and simplify.

### Percentage Change

Percentage Increase = (Increase / Original Value) × 100
Percentage Decrease = (Decrease / Original Value) × 100

If a value increases by x% and then decreases by x%, the net change is not zero. The final value is less than the original by (x²/100)% of the original.

**Formula for successive percentage changes**:
If a number is increased by a% and then by b%, the net percentage change is:
a + b + (ab)/100

If one is an increase and the other a decrease, take the decrease as negative.

### Percentage Points vs Percentage

An increase from 20% to 30% is an increase of 10 percentage points, but a 50% increase in the percentage itself ((30–20)/20 × 100).

### Applications in CSAT

- Population growth and decline
- Price increases and discounts
- Marks, scores, and pass percentages
- Profit and loss (profit percentage = (Profit/Cost Price) × 100)
- Data interpretation involving percentage shares and growth rates

### Useful Shortcuts

- x% of y = y% of x
- 10% of a number = number / 10
- 5% = half of 10%; 1% = number / 100
- To find what percentage A is of B: (A/B) × 100
- If A is x% more than B, then B is [x/(100+x)] × 100% less than A

## Part B: Ratios and Proportions

### Ratio

A ratio is a comparison of two quantities of the same kind by division. The ratio of a to b is a : b or a/b.

- Ratios should be expressed in simplest form.
- If a : b = m : n, then a/b = m/n, and a = km, b = kn for some positive k.

### Proportion

When two ratios are equal, they form a proportion: a : b = c : d or a/b = c/d.

- Product of extremes = Product of means (a × d = b × c)

### Types of Ratios Relevant for CSAT

- **Duplicate ratio**: of a : b is a² : b²
- **Triplicate ratio**: of a : b is a³ : b³
- **Sub-duplicate ratio**: of a : b is √a : √b
- **Compound ratio**: of (a : b) and (c : d) is ac : bd

### Direct and Inverse Proportion

- **Direct Proportion**: If x increases, y increases in the same ratio. x/y = constant, or x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂
- **Inverse Proportion**: If x increases, y decreases. x × y = constant, or x₁y₁ = x₂y₂

### Partnership and Ratio

In business partnership, if investments (or capital × time) are in the ratio a : b : c, then profits are shared in the same ratio (assuming same rate of return).

### Applications

- Sharing of profits, costs, or resources
- Comparison of quantities
- Mixture and alligation problems (basic level)
- Scaling of quantities in data interpretation

## Part C: Data Interpretation Foundations

Data Interpretation (DI) tests the ability to extract, analyse, and interpret information presented in tables, bar graphs, line graphs, pie charts, and combinations of these.

### Types of Data Presentation

1. **Tables**: Data arranged in rows and columns. Require careful reading of units, totals, and footnotes.
2. **Bar Graphs**: Length of bars represents values. Can be simple, grouped, or stacked.
3. **Line Graphs**: Show trends over time. Slope indicates rate of change.
4. **Pie Charts**: Show composition of a whole. Angles or percentages represent shares. Full circle = 360° or 100%.
5. **Mixed / Combination Graphs**: Two or more types together; require careful linking of information.

### Key Skills for DI

- **Reading carefully**: Note units (absolute numbers, percentages, indices), time periods, and legends.
- **Calculating percentages and ratios**: Growth rates, shares, comparisons.
- **Approximation**: Many questions can be solved with intelligent approximation rather than exact calculation.
- **Avoiding common traps**: Misreading scales, confusing percentage points with percentages, ignoring the base for percentage calculations.

### Common Question Types

- What is the value of a particular item?
- What is the percentage share of X in the total?
- What is the percentage increase/decrease from one period to another?
- Which item showed the highest/lowest growth?
- What is the ratio of A to B?
- Based on the data, which of the following conclusions is correct?

### Approach to Solving DI Sets

1. Spend a few seconds understanding the structure of the data (axes, units, categories).
2. Read the questions before doing detailed calculations; sometimes only one or two values are needed.
3. Use approximation where options are sufficiently spaced.
4. For pie charts, remember that 3.6° = 1% (since 360° = 100%).
5. Cross-check calculations if time permits, especially for percentage changes.

### Percentage and Ratio in DI

Most DI questions ultimately reduce to:

- Calculating a percentage of a total
- Finding percentage change between two values
- Comparing ratios
- Finding a missing value given a percentage or ratio

Mastery of percentages and ratios therefore directly improves DI performance.

## Worked Conceptual Examples (Illustrative)

**Example 1 (Percentage)**:
A number is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. Net change = 20 + (–20) + (20)(–20)/100 = –4%. The number becomes 96% of the original.

**Example 2 (Ratio)**:
If A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5, then A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15 (making B common as 12).

**Example 3 (DI mindset)**:
If a pie chart shows that Sector X is 72°, its percentage share is (72/360) × 100 = 20%. If the total is 5,000, the value of Sector X is 1,000.

## Practice-Oriented Observations for CSAT

- Speed and accuracy both matter. Develop the habit of approximate calculation.
- Learn to convert between fractions, decimals, and percentages fluently (e.g., 1/6 ≈ 16.67%, 1/7 ≈ 14.3%, 1/8 = 12.5%).
- In ratio questions, always check whether the quantities are comparable and whether the ratio is in simplest form.
- In DI, do not calculate more than necessary. Scan the questions first.
- Maintain neat rough work so that intermediate steps can be reused across questions in a set.

## Key Formulas and Points for Quick Revision

- Percentage change = (Change / Original) × 100
- Successive changes: a + b + ab/100
- x% of y = y% of x
- a : b = c : d ⇒ ad = bc
- Direct proportion: x/y = k; Inverse: xy = k
- Pie chart: 1% = 3.6°
- Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100; Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100

A strong command of percentages, ratios, and the ability to interpret data presented in various forms is essential for the CSAT paper. These topics are foundational and appear repeatedly in different guises across the quantitative and data interpretation sections.
