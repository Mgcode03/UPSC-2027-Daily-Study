# Geomorphic Processes and Landforms

## Introduction to Geomorphology

Geomorphology is the study of landforms and the processes that create and modify them. The Earth’s surface is continuously shaped by endogenic (internal) and exogenic (external) forces. Understanding these processes is essential for explaining the diversity of physical landscapes, their evolution, and their significance for human activity.

## Endogenic Processes

Endogenic processes originate from within the Earth and are driven by internal heat and tectonic forces.

### Diastrophism

Diastrophism refers to large-scale deformation of the Earth’s crust.

- Orogenic processes: mountain-building through folding, faulting, and thrusting. Associated with convergent plate boundaries.
- Epeirogenic processes: broad, vertical movements of large crustal blocks leading to uplift or subsidence of continents without intense folding.

### Volcanism

Volcanism involves the movement of magma to the surface. Types of volcanic landforms:

- Extrusive: lava plateaus, shield volcanoes, composite cones, cinder cones, calderas.
- Intrusive (plutonic): batholiths, laccoliths, sills, dykes, phacoliths.

Volcanic activity creates new crust and contributes to the formation of island arcs, mid-ocean ridges, and continental flood basalts.

### Earthquakes

Sudden release of energy along faults produces seismic waves. Earthquakes reshape landscapes through ground rupture, landslides, and changes in elevation. They are concentrated along plate boundaries.

## Exogenic Processes

Exogenic processes operate at or near the Earth’s surface and are driven primarily by solar energy and gravity.

### Weathering

Weathering is the in-situ breakdown of rocks.

- Physical (mechanical) weathering: freeze-thaw, thermal expansion, salt crystal growth, abrasion, pressure release (unloading).
- Chemical weathering: solution, hydration, hydrolysis, oxidation, carbonation, reduction.
- Biological weathering: action of roots, burrowing organisms, organic acids.

Weathering produces regolith and prepares material for erosion and transport.

### Mass Movement (Mass Wasting)

Downslope movement of rock and soil under gravity.

- Slow: creep, solifluction.
- Rapid: rockfalls, landslides, debris flows, mudflows, avalanches.

Factors influencing mass movement include slope angle, water content, vegetation, and triggering events such as earthquakes or heavy rainfall.

### Erosion, Transportation, and Deposition

Agents of erosion:

1. Running water (fluvial)
2. Groundwater
3. Glaciers (glacial)
4. Wind (aeolian)
5. Waves and currents (coastal)
6. Sea ice and other specialised agents

Each agent produces characteristic erosional and depositional landforms.

## Fluvial Landforms

### Erosional Landforms

- V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons
- Waterfalls and rapids
- Potholes, plunge pools
- Interlocking spurs
- River terraces (when uplift or base-level change occurs)

### Depositional Landforms

- Alluvial fans and cones
- Floodplains and natural levees
- Meanders, ox-bow lakes
- Deltas (arcuate, bird’s foot, estuarine)
- Braided channels

The long profile of a river typically shows a concave-up shape from source to mouth, with graded reaches adjusting to base level.

## Glacial Landforms

### Erosional

- Cirques (corries), arêtes, pyramidal peaks
- U-shaped valleys, hanging valleys
- Roche moutonnées, striations, glacial troughs
- Fjords (drowned glacial valleys)

### Depositional

- Moraines (lateral, medial, terminal, ground)
- Drumlins
- Eskers, kames, kettles
- Outwash plains (sandurs)
- Erratics

Glacial processes are dominant in high latitudes and high altitudes. Past glaciations have left extensive landform assemblages in many mid-latitude regions.

## Aeolian Landforms

Dominant in arid and semi-arid regions.

### Erosional

- Deflation hollows
- Yardangs
- Ventifacts
- Desert pavement

### Depositional

- Sand dunes: barchans, transverse, longitudinal (seifs), star dunes, parabolic dunes
- Loess deposits (wind-blown silt)

Wind action is selective; fine particles are transported long distances while coarser material remains.

## Coastal Landforms

### Erosional

- Cliffs, wave-cut platforms
- Sea caves, arches, stacks, stumps
- Headlands and bays

### Depositional

- Beaches, spits, bars, tombolos
- Barrier islands
- Coastal dunes
- Mudflats and salt marshes (in sheltered areas)

Coastal processes are influenced by wave energy, tidal range, sediment supply, and relative sea-level change.

## Karst Landforms

Developed in soluble rocks (limestone, dolomite, gypsum) through chemical solution and related processes.

- Surface: sinkholes (dolines), uvalas, poljes, limestone pavements (clints and grikes)
- Underground: caves, stalactites, stalagmites, columns, underground rivers

Karst landscapes are characterised by disappearing streams, dry valleys, and complex drainage.

## Landscape Evolution Concepts

Classical models (Davis, Penck, King) described cycles of erosion leading to peneplains or other end-forms. Modern geomorphology emphasises process-response systems, climatic geomorphology, tectonic geomorphology, and the role of thresholds and complex responses. Landscapes are viewed as systems in dynamic equilibrium or adjusting to changing boundary conditions (climate, tectonics, base level, human activity).

## Significance for India

India exhibits a wide range of geomorphic features:

- Himalayan orogenic belt with active tectonics and glacial landforms
- Peninsular plateau with residual hills, laterite caps, and ancient drainage
- Indo-Gangetic plain formed by fluvial deposition
- Thar Desert with aeolian landforms
- Coastal plains and deltas of major rivers
- Karst features in parts of the Himalaya, Western Ghats, and central India

Understanding geomorphic processes helps explain regional physiography, soil formation, drainage patterns, natural hazards (landslides, floods, coastal erosion), and resource distribution.

## Key Concepts Summary

- Endogenic forces build; exogenic forces wear down.
- Weathering prepares; erosion removes; deposition builds new forms.
- Each agent produces distinctive landform assemblages.
- Landforms record the history of process and environmental change.
- Human activities increasingly modify geomorphic systems.

A systematic understanding of processes and resulting landforms is fundamental to physical geography and to interpreting the map of India and the world.
