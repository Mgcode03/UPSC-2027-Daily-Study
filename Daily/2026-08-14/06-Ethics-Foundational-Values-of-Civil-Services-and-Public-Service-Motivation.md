# Foundational Values of Civil Services and Public Service Motivation

## Concept of Foundational Values

Foundational values are the core ethical principles that guide the conduct of civil servants. They form the moral compass for decision-making, behaviour, and institutional culture in public administration. These values are derived from the Constitution, the nature of public office as a trust, and the expectations of a democratic society.

In the Indian context, foundational values have been articulated in various official documents, training curricula, and the recommendations of committees on ethics in governance. They are not merely aspirational statements but practical standards against which conduct is evaluated.

## Core Foundational Values

### 1. Integrity

Integrity means consistency between principles, words, and actions. It involves honesty, incorruptibility, and refusal to compromise ethical standards for personal gain or external pressure. A civil servant with integrity places public interest above private interest and resists temptations of corruption, favouritism, or misuse of authority.

Integrity also includes intellectual honesty—acknowledging limitations of knowledge, presenting facts accurately, and avoiding manipulation of information.

### 2. Impartiality and Non-partisanship

Civil servants serve the government of the day while remaining politically neutral. Impartiality requires treating all citizens equally without bias based on political affiliation, social identity, or personal preference. Non-partisanship means not using official position to advance the interests of any political party.

This value sustains the permanent character of the civil service across changes of government and protects the legitimacy of administration.

### 3. Objectivity

Objectivity demands that decisions be based on evidence, established criteria, and rational analysis rather than emotion, prejudice, or extraneous considerations. It requires collecting relevant facts, weighing options fairly, and documenting the basis of decisions.

Objectivity supports fairness and makes decisions defensible under scrutiny.

### 4. Dedication to Public Service

Public service is a vocation oriented towards the welfare of citizens and the larger public good. Dedication implies commitment beyond minimal duty, willingness to take responsibility, and prioritising citizen-centric outcomes over procedural convenience or personal comfort.

This value is closely linked to the concept of public service motivation—the intrinsic desire to serve society and contribute to meaningful public outcomes.

### 5. Empathy and Compassion

Empathy involves understanding the situations and feelings of others, particularly the vulnerable and marginalised. Compassion translates understanding into supportive action within the framework of rules and resources.

These values humanise administration and prevent mechanical application of rules that can produce harsh or unjust outcomes.

### 6. Tolerance and Responsiveness to Diversity

Indian society is characterised by immense diversity of language, religion, caste, region, and culture. Civil servants must demonstrate tolerance, respect for difference, and the ability to work effectively across social boundaries. Responsiveness requires listening to diverse voices and adapting delivery to varied needs while upholding constitutional equality.

### 7. Accountability and Transparency

Accountability means being answerable for decisions and actions to higher authorities, institutions, and ultimately the public. Transparency involves openness in processes and availability of information, subject to legitimate confidentiality requirements.

These values strengthen trust and enable democratic control over administration.

### 8. Courage of Conviction and Holding Fast to Ethical Principles

Civil servants sometimes face pressure to act against ethical or legal standards. Courage of conviction is the willingness to uphold what is right even at personal or professional cost. It includes the readiness to record dissent where necessary and to resist unlawful or improper directions through established channels.

## Public Service Motivation

Public Service Motivation (PSM) refers to an individual’s predisposition to respond to motives grounded primarily or uniquely in public institutions and organisations. Dimensions commonly identified include:

- Attraction to public policy making
- Commitment to public interest and civic duty
- Compassion
- Self-sacrifice

High PSM is associated with greater effort, ethical behaviour, and satisfaction derived from contributing to society. Cultivating and sustaining PSM requires supportive organisational cultures, meaningful work design, recognition of ethical conduct, and leadership that models public service values.

## Relationship with Constitutional Values

Foundational values of civil services are rooted in constitutional principles:

- Justice (social, economic, political)
- Liberty, equality, and fraternity
- Dignity of the individual
- Unity and integrity of the nation
- Secularism and democracy

Article 309 and related provisions, service rules, and conduct rules operationalise these expectations. The code of conduct for civil servants emphasises integrity, devotion to duty, and political neutrality.

## Challenges to Upholding Foundational Values

- Political and administrative pressure
- Corruption and opportunities for rent-seeking
- Conflicting demands from multiple stakeholders
- Resource constraints and implementation gaps
- Personal ambition or fear of consequences
- Organisational cultures that tolerate or reward unethical behaviour
- Ambiguity in rules or competing priorities

Addressing these challenges requires individual ethical competence, institutional mechanisms (vigilance, grievance redress, performance systems that reward integrity), and a broader societal culture that values public service ethics.

## Practical Implications

In daily work, foundational values guide:

- How information is handled and advice is tendered
- How discretion is exercised
- How citizens are treated at the interface of administration
- How conflicts of interest are managed
- How hierarchical instructions are evaluated for legality and propriety
- How resources are allocated and programmes implemented

Case studies and ethical dilemmas in public administration typically test the application of these values under conditions of uncertainty, competing claims, and pressure.

## Key Points for Retention

- Foundational values provide the ethical foundation for civil service conduct.
- Core values include integrity, impartiality, objectivity, dedication to public service, empathy, tolerance, accountability, and courage of conviction.
- Public Service Motivation explains the intrinsic drive to serve the public good.
- These values are constitutionally grounded and operationalised through rules and institutional culture.
- Upholding them requires both individual commitment and supportive systems.

Mastery of these concepts is central to Ethics paper answers and to understanding the normative expectations of public administration in India.
