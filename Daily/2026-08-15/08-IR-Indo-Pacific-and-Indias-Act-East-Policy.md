# Indo-Pacific Concept and India’s Act East Policy

## The Indo-Pacific as a Strategic Construct

The Indo-Pacific refers to the maritime space stretching from the eastern coast of Africa through the Indian Ocean and the western and central Pacific Ocean, encompassing the littoral states of these oceans. It has emerged as a key geopolitical and geo-economic concept in the twenty-first century, reflecting the shift of economic and strategic weight towards Asia and the increasing connectivity between the Indian and Pacific Oceans.

The concept emphasises the interconnectedness of the two oceans, the importance of sea lanes of communication for global trade and energy flows, and the need for a stable, rules-based order in the region. Different countries define the geographical scope slightly differently, but the core idea of linkage between the Indian and Pacific theatres is shared.

## Significance of the Indo-Pacific

- A large share of global trade, including energy supplies, passes through Indo-Pacific sea lanes, particularly critical chokepoints such as the Strait of Malacca, the South China Sea and the Strait of Hormuz.
- The region contains major economies and is a driver of global growth.
- Strategic competition, especially between major powers, is intense in parts of the region.
- Non-traditional security challenges—piracy, illegal fishing, natural disasters, climate change impacts and maritime pollution—require cooperative responses.
- The region is central to global supply chains and technological competition.

## India’s Vision of the Indo-Pacific

India views the Indo-Pacific as a free, open, inclusive and rules-based region. Key elements of India’s approach include:

- Respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity.
- Freedom of navigation and overflight.
- Peaceful resolution of disputes in accordance with international law, particularly UNCLOS.
- Openness to cooperation with all countries that share these principles.
- Emphasis on ASEAN centrality in the regional architecture.
- Focus on practical cooperation in areas such as maritime security, connectivity, sustainable development and capacity building.

India has articulated this vision in various forums and has engaged with partners through bilateral, minilateral and multilateral mechanisms.

## Act East Policy

India’s Act East Policy, evolving from the earlier Look East Policy, is a diplomatic initiative to strengthen economic, strategic and cultural relations with the Asia-Pacific region, with particular emphasis on Southeast Asia and East Asia. It reflects the recognition of the growing importance of these regions for India’s economic growth, security and global role.

Key dimensions include:

- Economic: Trade, investment, connectivity projects and integration with regional value chains.
- Strategic and security: Defence dialogues, joint exercises, maritime cooperation and coordination on regional security issues.
- Connectivity: Land, sea and air connectivity, including projects linking India’s Northeast with Southeast Asia.
- Cultural and people-to-people: Shared historical and cultural ties, tourism, education and diaspora linkages.
- Institutional: Engagement with ASEAN, East Asia Summit, ASEAN Regional Forum and other mechanisms.

The policy also aligns with India’s broader Indo-Pacific approach by deepening partnerships that support a stable and multipolar regional order.

## Key Partnerships and Mechanisms

India engages with ASEAN as a central partner. Relations with individual Southeast Asian countries have expanded in defence, trade and connectivity. Partnerships with Japan, Australia, the United States and other like-minded countries have grown, including through the Quad (Quadrilateral Security Dialogue), which focuses on practical cooperation in areas such as maritime domain awareness, humanitarian assistance, quality infrastructure and emerging technologies.

India also participates in other regional forums and maintains dialogues with China and Russia as part of a multi-aligned approach.

## Challenges and Opportunities

Challenges include managing differences with major powers, ensuring that connectivity projects are sustainable and beneficial, addressing capacity constraints, and navigating the complex security dynamics of the region. Opportunities lie in economic integration, contribution to regional public goods such as maritime security and disaster response, and the projection of India as a credible and responsible stakeholder in the Indo-Pacific.

## Key Concepts

- Indo-Pacific as a free, open, inclusive and rules-based region.
- ASEAN centrality.
- Freedom of navigation and adherence to international law.
- Act East Policy as the operational framework for engagement with Southeast and East Asia.
- Multi-aligned partnerships including the Quad and ASEAN-led mechanisms.
- Connectivity, maritime security and economic integration as priority areas.

## Summary

The Indo-Pacific has become a central theatre of global geopolitics and geo-economics. India’s vision emphasises openness, inclusivity and a rules-based order, with ASEAN at the core of the regional architecture. The Act East Policy provides the practical framework for deepening India’s engagement with the eastern part of this vast region across economic, strategic and cultural domains. Understanding these concepts is essential for analysing India’s foreign policy and its role in the evolving regional order.
