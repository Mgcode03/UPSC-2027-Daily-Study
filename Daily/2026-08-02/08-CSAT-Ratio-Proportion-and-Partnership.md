# CSAT: Ratio, Proportion and Partnership

## Introduction

Ratio and proportion are fundamental concepts in quantitative aptitude. They appear frequently in CSAT and in various forms of data interpretation and problem solving. Partnership problems are a direct application of ratio concepts to business and investment situations. Mastery of these topics requires conceptual clarity and regular practice with different problem types.

## Ratio

### Definition and Notation

A ratio is a comparison of two or more quantities of the same kind by division. If a and b are two quantities of the same kind, the ratio of a to b is a : b or a/b.

- The quantities being compared are called the terms of the ratio. In a : b, a is the first term (or antecedent) and b is the second term (or consequent).
- Ratios can be expressed in simplest form by dividing both terms by their greatest common divisor.
- A ratio has no unit; it is a pure number. The quantities themselves must be in the same units before a ratio is formed.

### Key Properties

1. If a : b = c : d, then a/b = c/d, and ad = bc (cross product property).
2. If a : b = b : c, then b is the geometric mean of a and c, and b squared equals a times c.
3. Ratios can be multiplied or divided by the same non-zero number without changing the ratio’s value in terms of comparison, but the terms change.
4. To combine ratios or to find a common ratio when multiple ratios are given, a common multiple method or the concept of equivalent ratios is used.

### Types of Ratios

- **Part-to-part ratio**: Compares one part to another part (for example, boys to girls in a class).
- **Part-to-whole ratio**: Compares a part to the whole (for example, boys to total students).
- **Ratio of more than two quantities**: a : b : c means a/b = first to second, b/c = second to third, and so on. To work with such ratios, it is often useful to express all quantities in terms of a common multiplier.

### Duplicate and Triplicate Ratios

- Duplicate ratio of a : b is a squared : b squared.
- Triplicate ratio of a : b is a cubed : b cubed.
- Sub-duplicate ratio of a : b is square root of a : square root of b.
- Sub-triplicate ratio of a : b is cube root of a : cube root of b.

These appear occasionally in problems and should be recognised.

### Important Techniques

**Dividing a quantity in a given ratio:**

If a quantity Q is to be divided in the ratio a : b, then

- First part = [a / (a + b)] times Q
- Second part = [b / (a + b)] times Q

For three parts a : b : c,

- First part = [a / (a + b + c)] times Q, and similarly for others.

**Finding the original quantities when the difference or sum is given:**

If the ratio is a : b and the difference of the quantities is D, then

- First quantity = [a / (a - b)] times D (when a > b)
- Second quantity = [b / (a - b)] times D

Similar relations hold when the sum is given.

**Ratio of ratios and continued ratios:**

When a : b and b : c are given, the combined ratio a : b : c can be found by making the common term (b) equal in both ratios through appropriate multiplication.

## Proportion

### Definition

Proportion is the equality of two ratios. If a : b = c : d, then a, b, c, d are said to be in proportion. This is written as a : b :: c : d or a/b = c/d.

- a and d are called the extremes.
- b and c are called the means.
- In a proportion, the product of the extremes equals the product of the means: a times d = b times c.

### Types of Proportion

1. **Direct proportion**: Two quantities are in direct proportion if an increase in one leads to a proportional increase in the other (and similarly for decrease). If x and y are in direct proportion, x/y is constant, or x = k y.

2. **Inverse proportion**: Two quantities are in inverse proportion if an increase in one leads to a proportional decrease in the other. If x and y are in inverse proportion, x times y is constant, or x = k / y.

3. **Continued proportion**: Three quantities a, b, c are in continued proportion if a : b = b : c. Then b is the geometric mean of a and c.

### Direct and Inverse Proportion – Problem Solving

Many word problems involve direct or inverse proportion, or a combination of both (compound proportion).

**Method of proportion (unitary or fractional method):**

- Identify which quantities are directly proportional and which are inversely proportional to the required quantity.
- Write the known values and the unknown.
- Form an equation using the principle that the product of quantities that are inversely related and the appropriate arrangement of directly related quantities remains consistent.

**Example structure for compound proportion:**

If A is proportional to B and inversely proportional to C, then A = k times (B / C). Given one set of values, k can be found and used to find the unknown.

### Mean Proportional and Third Proportional

- Mean proportional between a and b is the square root of (a times b).
- Third proportional to a and b is the quantity c such that a : b = b : c, so c = (b squared) / a.
- Fourth proportional to a, b, c is the quantity d such that a : b = c : d, so d = (b times c) / a.

## Partnership

### Basic Concept

Partnership is a business arrangement in which two or more persons share the profits (or losses) of a business. The partners contribute capital (and sometimes labour or expertise), and profits are shared according to an agreed ratio, which is often based on the capital contributed and the time for which it is invested.

### Simple Partnership

When capitals are invested for the same period, profits are divided in the ratio of the capitals invested.

If A invests Cap_A and B invests Cap_B for the same time, then

Profit of A : Profit of B = Cap_A : Cap_B

### Compound Partnership (Capital and Time both vary)

When capitals are invested for different periods, the profit share is proportional to the product of capital and time.

Profit of A : Profit of B = (Cap_A times Time_A) : (Cap_B times Time_B)

This is because capital is assumed to earn a return proportional to both the amount and the duration of investment.

### Working and Sleeping Partners

- A working partner actively manages the business and may receive a salary or a higher share of profit in lieu of salary.
- A sleeping (or dormant) partner contributes capital but does not take part in management.

In problems, if a working partner is given a salary or a commission before profit distribution, that amount is first deducted from the total profit, and the remaining profit is distributed in the capital-time ratio. Alternatively, the agreement may specify a fixed ratio that already accounts for the working partner’s extra contribution.

### Key Problem Types in Partnership

1. Finding the share of profit given capitals and time.
2. Finding the capital given profit share and time.
3. Finding the time given capital and profit share.
4. Problems involving change of capital during the year (additional investment or withdrawal).
5. Problems involving a working partner’s salary or commission.
6. Problems where the ratio of profits is given and capitals or times are to be found.

### Technique for Change of Capital

If a partner invests different amounts for different periods within the year, the equivalent capital for the full year (or a common period) is calculated as the sum of (capital times fraction of the year for which it was invested). The profit is then shared in the ratio of these equivalent capitals.

## Useful Algebraic Relations

- If A : B = m : n, then A = m k and B = n k for some positive k.
- If A : B = m : n and B : C = p : q, then A : B : C = (m p) : (n p) : (n q), after making the B terms equal.
- In proportion a : b = c : d, (a + b) / (c + d) equals a/c only in special cases; do not assume additivity of ratios without care.
- For three quantities in continued proportion a, b, c: b squared = a c.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Comparing quantities without converting to the same units.
2. Confusing part-to-part ratio with part-to-whole ratio.
3. Forgetting to multiply capital by time in partnership problems when periods differ.
4. Incorrectly handling the working partner’s salary (whether it is deducted before or after profit sharing depends on the problem statement).
5. Assuming direct proportion when the relationship is inverse, or vice versa.
6. Errors in simplifying ratios or in cross-multiplication.

## Practice-Oriented Conceptual Checklist

- Can you divide a given amount in a given ratio of two or three parts?
- Can you find the original amounts when the ratio and the sum (or difference) are given?
- Can you combine two ratios sharing a common term into a three-term ratio?
- Can you identify direct and inverse proportion in a word problem and set up the correct equation?
- Can you compute profit shares when capitals and times both differ?
- Can you adjust for a partner’s salary or for mid-year changes in capital?

## Key Formulas at a Glance

- Ratio a : b in simplest form: divide by GCD(a, b).
- Quantity Q in ratio a : b: parts are [a/(a+b)]Q and [b/(a+b)]Q.
- Proportion: a/b = c/d implies a d = b c.
- Mean proportional between a and b: square root of (a b).
- Partnership (same time): Profit ratio = Capital ratio.
- Partnership (different time): Profit ratio = (Capital times Time) ratio.

Ratio, proportion, and partnership form a connected set of ideas. Strong command over the basic definitions and the capital-time product rule in partnership will cover the large majority of CSAT questions in this area. Regular practice with varied problem statements builds both speed and accuracy.
