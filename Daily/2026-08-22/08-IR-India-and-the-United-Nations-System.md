# India and the United Nations System

## Introduction

The United Nations (UN) is the central organisation of the contemporary multilateral system. Founded in 1945 in the aftermath of the Second World War, its primary purposes, as stated in the UN Charter, are to maintain international peace and security, develop friendly relations among nations, achieve international cooperation in solving economic, social, cultural, and humanitarian problems, and harmonise the actions of nations. India was a founding member of the United Nations and has remained deeply engaged with the organisation and its specialised agencies ever since.

## Structure of the United Nations System (Conceptual Overview)

The UN system comprises principal organs and a family of specialised agencies, funds, and programmes.

### Principal Organs

1. **General Assembly**: The main deliberative body in which all member states have equal representation (one country, one vote). It discusses and makes recommendations on a wide range of issues; its resolutions are generally non-binding but carry political weight.
2. **Security Council**: Primary responsibility for the maintenance of international peace and security. It has 15 members: 5 permanent (China, France, Russia, United Kingdom, United States) with veto power, and 10 non-permanent elected for two-year terms. Its decisions can be binding.
3. **Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)**: Coordinates the economic and social work of the UN and the specialised agencies.
4. **International Court of Justice (ICJ)**: The principal judicial organ; settles legal disputes between states and gives advisory opinions.
5. **Secretariat**: Headed by the Secretary-General; carries out the day-to-day work of the organisation.
6. **Trusteeship Council**: Largely inactive after the completion of the trusteeship system.

### Specialised Agencies and Related Bodies

These include the World Health Organization (WHO), United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO), International Labour Organization (ILO), Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), World Bank Group, International Monetary Fund (IMF), United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF), United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), and many others. Each has its own membership, governance, and focus area.

## India’s Engagement with the United Nations

### Founding and Early Years
India participated in the San Francisco Conference of 1945 and signed the UN Charter. As a newly independent nation (from 1947), India viewed the UN as an important platform for articulating the concerns of Asian and African countries, opposing colonialism and racial discrimination, and promoting peaceful coexistence.

### Peacekeeping
India has been one of the largest and most consistent contributors to UN peacekeeping operations. Indian personnel have served in numerous missions across continents. This contribution is both a demonstration of commitment to collective security and a source of experience and international profile for the Indian armed forces and police.

### Decolonisation and Anti-Apartheid
India played an active role in the UN’s efforts toward decolonisation and was a leading voice against apartheid in South Africa. It used the General Assembly and other forums to mobilise international opinion on these issues.

### Disarmament and Non-Proliferation
India has consistently advocated nuclear disarmament and general and complete disarmament under effective international control. It has participated in Conference on Disarmament discussions while maintaining its own strategic autonomy and nuclear doctrine based on credible minimum deterrence and no first use.

### Development and South-South Cooperation
India engages actively with the development agenda of the UN, including the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). It emphasises the importance of development, poverty eradication, and the special needs of developing countries. India also contributes to capacity-building and technical cooperation with other developing countries under the framework of South-South cooperation.

### Human Rights and Social Issues
India participates in the Human Rights Council and other bodies, balancing the promotion of human rights with concerns about selectivity, politicisation, and the need to respect national sovereignty and cultural diversity.

### Reform of the United Nations
A central theme of India’s contemporary engagement is the call for reform of the UN system, particularly the Security Council. India argues that the Council’s permanent membership reflects the power realities of 1945 and not those of the twenty-first century. As one of the largest countries by population, a major economy, a consistent troop contributor, and a representative of the developing world, India seeks permanent membership in an expanded Security Council. It is part of the G4 grouping (with Brazil, Germany, and Japan) that coordinates on reform. India also supports broader reforms to make the UN more representative, transparent, efficient, and responsive to contemporary challenges (terrorism, climate change, pandemics, cyber security, etc.).

## Key Issues in India’s UN Diplomacy

- **Terrorism**: India has pushed for comprehensive conventions against international terrorism and for sanctions against specific terrorist entities and individuals. It emphasises that terrorism in all forms and manifestations must be condemned without double standards.
- **Climate change**: India participates in the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) processes, advocating the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities, and highlighting its own significant efforts in renewable energy and climate action.
- **Global health and pandemics**: Engagement with WHO and support for equitable access to vaccines and health technologies.
- **Sustainable development and financing for development**: Advocacy for adequate resources, technology transfer, and a supportive international economic environment for developing countries.
- **Peacekeeping reform**: Support for better resources, clearer mandates, and protection of peacekeepers, while maintaining the principle of consent of parties and non-use of force except in self-defence and defence of the mandate.

## Challenges and Critiques

The UN system faces persistent challenges: the veto power and the difficulty of Security Council reform; resource constraints; bureaucratic inefficiencies; geopolitical divisions that paralyse decision-making; and questions about the effectiveness of some specialised agencies. India’s own experience includes both successful multilateral diplomacy and frustrations when its priorities (such as Council reform or strong anti-terrorism measures) encounter resistance.

## Key Points for Memorisation

- UN founded 1945; India founding member.
- Principal organs: General Assembly, Security Council (5 permanent + 10 non-permanent), ECOSOC, ICJ, Secretariat.
- India major troop contributor to peacekeeping; active on decolonisation, anti-apartheid, disarmament, development.
- Central contemporary demand: permanent membership in reformed Security Council (G4 coordination).
- Key issue areas: terrorism, climate change, SDGs, peacekeeping reform, human rights.
- Guiding principles of India’s approach: multilateralism, sovereignty, equity, development priority, strategic autonomy.

## Conceptual Clarity Notes

India’s relationship with the United Nations illustrates the broader foreign-policy orientation of a large developing democracy that values multilateral institutions while remaining realistic about power politics. The UN provides legitimacy, a platform for norm entrepreneurship, and practical cooperation on transnational problems. At the same time, the structural inequalities of the system (especially the Security Council) limit its ability to reflect contemporary realities. India’s engagement is therefore both supportive of the UN’s purposes and reformist in intent. Understanding this dual stance – commitment plus demand for change – is essential to grasping India’s multilateral diplomacy.
