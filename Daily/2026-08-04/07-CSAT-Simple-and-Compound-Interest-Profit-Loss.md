# CSAT Foundations: Simple Interest, Compound Interest, Profit and Loss

## Simple Interest

Simple Interest is the interest calculated only on the original principal amount throughout the entire period of the loan or investment. The formula is:

**Simple Interest (SI) = (P × R × T) / 100**

where  
P = Principal (original sum)  
R = Rate of interest per annum (in per cent)  
T = Time period in years

**Amount (A) = Principal + Simple Interest = P + (P × R × T)/100 = P (1 + RT/100)**

### Key Points

- Interest is the same for every year if the rate and principal remain constant.
- If time is given in months, convert it into years by dividing by 12.
- If rate is given for a period other than a year, adjust accordingly.

### Illustrative Concepts

If a sum doubles itself in T years under simple interest, then  
P + SI = 2P ⇒ SI = P ⇒ (P × R × T)/100 = P ⇒ R T = 100 ⇒ T = 100/R  or R = 100/T.

If a sum becomes n times itself in T years under simple interest, then  
R = [(n – 1)/T] × 100.

## Compound Interest

Compound Interest is the interest calculated on the principal plus the accumulated interest of previous periods. Interest is added to the principal at regular intervals (annually, half-yearly, quarterly, etc.), and subsequent interest is calculated on the new principal.

**When interest is compounded annually:**  
Amount A = P (1 + R/100)^T  
Compound Interest CI = A – P = P [(1 + R/100)^T – 1]

**When interest is compounded half-yearly:**  
Rate for each half-year = R/2  
Number of half-years = 2T  
A = P (1 + R/200)^(2T)

**When interest is compounded quarterly:**  
Rate for each quarter = R/4  
Number of quarters = 4T  
A = P (1 + R/400)^(4T)

**When interest is compounded monthly:**  
A = P (1 + R/1200)^(12T)

### Difference between Simple Interest and Compound Interest

For two years, the difference between CI and SI is given by:  
CI – SI = P (R/100)^2

For three years the difference is larger and involves an additional term.

### Effective Rate of Interest

When interest is compounded more than once a year, the effective annual rate is higher than the nominal rate.  
Effective rate = [(1 + R/(100n))^n – 1] × 100  where n is the number of compounding periods in a year.

## Profit, Loss and Discount

### Basic Terminology

- **Cost Price (CP)**: The price at which an article is purchased.
- **Selling Price (SP)**: The price at which an article is sold.
- **Profit (or Gain)**: When SP > CP, Profit = SP – CP.
- **Loss**: When CP > SP, Loss = CP – SP.
- **Profit Percentage** = (Profit / CP) × 100
- **Loss Percentage** = (Loss / CP) × 100

Note that both profit and loss percentages are always calculated on the Cost Price unless otherwise stated.

### Formulas

SP = CP × (100 + Profit%)/100  
SP = CP × (100 – Loss%)/100  
CP = SP × 100/(100 + Profit%)  
CP = SP × 100/(100 – Loss%)

If there are two successive profits of a% and b%, the overall profit percentage is  
a + b + (a b)/100.

If there is a profit of a% and a loss of b%, the overall effect is  
a – b – (a b)/100.

### Discount

**Marked Price (MP)** or List Price is the price printed on the article.  
**Discount** is the reduction offered on the Marked Price.  
**Discount Percentage** = (Discount / MP) × 100  
SP = MP × (100 – Discount%)/100

When successive discounts of a% and b% are offered, the equivalent single discount is  
a + b – (a b)/100.

### Relationship between CP, SP, MP and Discount

If an article is sold at a profit of P% after allowing a discount of D% on the Marked Price, then  
CP / MP = (100 – D)/(100 + P)

### Important Conceptual Points

- Profit or loss is always calculated on the cost price unless the question specifically states otherwise.
- Discount is always calculated on the marked price.
- If a trader claims to sell at cost price but uses false weights, he still makes a profit. The profit percentage can be calculated from the ratio of true weight to false weight.
- In questions involving successive transactions (purchase and sale by different parties), calculate the profit or loss of each party separately with respect to their own cost price.

## Application Orientation for CSAT

CSAT questions test conceptual clarity and speed. Typical question types include:

- Finding SI or CI for a given principal, rate and time
- Finding principal or rate or time when amount or interest is given
- Comparing SI and CI
- Finding effective rate under different compounding frequencies
- Profit or loss percentage from given CP and SP
- Finding SP or CP when profit/loss percentage is given
- Successive discounts and equivalent single discount
- Questions involving false weights or dishonest dealing
- Mixture of profit/loss with discount and marked price

### Memory Aids

- SI is linear; CI is exponential.
- More frequent compounding leads to higher effective interest.
- Profit % and Loss % are always on CP; Discount % is always on MP.
- Successive percentage changes: a + b + (ab)/100 for two successive increases or discounts.

Mastery of these elementary formulas and the ability to apply them quickly under examination conditions forms an essential part of CSAT quantitative aptitude preparation.
