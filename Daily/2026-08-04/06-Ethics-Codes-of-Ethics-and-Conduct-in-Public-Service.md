# Codes of Ethics and Codes of Conduct in Public Service

## Distinction between Code of Ethics and Code of Conduct

A **Code of Ethics** is a set of fundamental principles and values that guide the behaviour and decision-making of public servants. It is aspirational in nature and focuses on the spirit of public service. It answers the question: “What ought I to do?” in terms of broader moral principles such as integrity, impartiality, justice, compassion and accountability.

A **Code of Conduct** is a more specific set of rules and standards that prescribe or prohibit particular types of behaviour. It is regulatory and enforceable. It answers the question: “What must I do or not do?” in concrete situations such as acceptance of gifts, conflict of interest, political activity, use of official information, and financial propriety.

In practice the two often overlap, and many documents combine elements of both. The ethical foundation provides the rationale for the specific rules of conduct.

## Need for Ethical Codes in Public Administration

Public servants exercise significant power and discretion. They handle public resources, make decisions affecting the lives and rights of citizens, and operate in situations where personal interest may conflict with public interest. A clear ethical framework:

- Provides guidance in situations of moral ambiguity
- Builds public trust and legitimacy of institutions
- Promotes consistency and fairness in decision-making
- Protects public servants from undue pressure and political interference
- Serves as a basis for accountability and disciplinary action
- Strengthens organisational culture and professional identity

## Core Values of Public Service

Across democratic systems certain values are universally recognised as essential to public service:

- **Integrity**: Honesty, consistency between words and actions, and refusal to compromise ethical standards for personal gain.
- **Impartiality and Objectivity**: Decisions based on merit, evidence and law rather than personal bias, favouritism or political affiliation.
- **Commitment to Public Interest**: Placing the public good above private or sectional interests.
- **Accountability**: Answerability for decisions and actions to the appropriate authorities and, ultimately, to the people.
- **Transparency**: Openness in decision-making and willingness to provide information subject to legitimate constraints.
- **Compassion and Empathy**: Sensitivity to the needs and circumstances of citizens, especially the vulnerable.
- **Professionalism**: Competence, diligence and continuous improvement.
- **Respect for the Law and Constitution**: Upholding the constitutional values and the rule of law.

## Indian Framework

### Constitutional Foundations

The Constitution of India embodies several ethical principles. The Preamble sets out the goals of justice, liberty, equality and fraternity. Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles provide the normative framework. Article 311 provides safeguards to civil servants against arbitrary dismissal. The oaths of office prescribed in the Third Schedule emphasise faithful and conscientious discharge of duties without fear or favour.

### Conduct Rules

The Central Civil Services (Conduct) Rules, 1964 and the All India Services (Conduct) Rules, 1968 lay down specific standards of behaviour for civil servants. Key provisions relate to:

- Maintenance of integrity and devotion to duty
- Prohibition of taking part in politics or elections
- Restrictions on criticism of government
- Rules regarding acceptance of gifts, hospitality and dowry
- Prohibition of private trade or employment
- Restrictions on investment, lending and borrowing
- Observance of rules regarding property returns
- Prohibition of sexual harassment
- Observance of the Official Secrets Act and rules on communication of official information

Similar conduct rules exist at the state level.

### Recommendations of Committees and Commissions

Several committees have examined the ethical dimension of public service:

- The Committee on Prevention of Corruption (Santhanam Committee, 1962) emphasised the need for integrity and recommended institutional mechanisms.
- The Second Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC) in its report on “Ethics in Governance” (2007) made comprehensive recommendations. It suggested a Code of Ethics for civil servants, a Code of Conduct for ministers, strengthening of the locust standi of the Central Vigilance Commission, and measures to promote transparency and accountability. The ARC emphasised that ethics should be an integral part of the training and evaluation of civil servants.
- The Nolan Committee in the United Kingdom (1995) articulated the Seven Principles of Public Life: Selflessness, Integrity, Objectivity, Accountability, Openness, Honesty and Leadership. These principles have influenced thinking on public service ethics globally, including in India.

### Code of Ethics for Civil Servants – Emerging Framework

While a comprehensive statutory Code of Ethics has not yet been enacted, the spirit of such a code is reflected in various documents and training curricula. The expected standards include:

- Upholding the Constitution and the laws
- Maintaining political neutrality
- Acting with integrity and honesty
- Ensuring accountability and transparency
- Promoting the public interest
- Treating all citizens with courtesy, respect and impartiality
- Avoiding conflict of interest and the appearance of conflict of interest
- Using public resources efficiently and honestly
- Maintaining confidentiality of information where required
- Continuous professional development

## Conflict of Interest

Conflict of interest arises when a public official’s private interests could improperly influence the performance of official duties. It may be actual, potential or apparent. Managing conflict of interest requires:

- Disclosure of interests
- Recusal from decision-making where conflict exists
- Divestment or blind trusts in certain cases
- Clear rules on post-retirement employment
- Transparent procedures for procurement and appointments

## Ethical Dilemmas in Public Service

Public servants frequently face situations where competing values or loyalties create dilemmas:

- Loyalty to the minister versus loyalty to the public interest or the law
- Efficiency versus equity
- Transparency versus confidentiality
- Rule-following versus humanitarian considerations in exceptional cases
- Short-term political pressures versus long-term institutional integrity

A well-internalised ethical framework, combined with institutional support and a culture that encourages ethical consultation, helps in resolving such dilemmas.

## Building an Ethical Organisation

Codes alone are not sufficient. An ethical public service requires:

- Leadership that models ethical behaviour
- Training and sensitisation programmes
- Mechanisms for ethical advice and counselling
- Protection for whistle-blowers
- Fair and swift disciplinary processes
- Recognition and reward of ethical conduct
- Reduction of systemic incentives for corruption and rent-seeking

## Key Points for Retention

- Code of Ethics is value-based and aspirational; Code of Conduct is rule-based and enforceable.
- Core public service values include integrity, impartiality, accountability, transparency and commitment to public interest.
- Conduct Rules of 1964 and 1968 provide the regulatory framework in India.
- Second ARC report on Ethics in Governance remains a key reference.
- Conflict of interest management and resolution of ethical dilemmas are practical challenges that require both personal integrity and institutional support.

A sound understanding of these concepts is essential for answering Ethics paper questions on public service values, codes, and case studies involving ethical decision-making.
