# Environmental Impact Assessment and Pollution Control

## Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

Environmental Impact Assessment is a planning and management tool for predicting the likely environmental consequences of a proposed project or development activity before a decision is taken to implement it. The objective is to identify, predict, evaluate and mitigate the biophysical, social and other relevant effects of development proposals prior to major decisions being taken.

### Evolution of EIA in India

EIA was introduced in India on a limited scale in the late 1970s. The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 provided the legal basis for environmental clearance. The EIA Notification of 1994 made environmental clearance mandatory for certain categories of projects. The Notification was comprehensively revised in 2006 and has been amended several times thereafter.

### Key Features of the EIA Process

1. **Screening**: Determines whether a project requires environmental clearance and at what level (Category A or Category B).
2. **Scoping**: Identifies the key issues and impacts that need to be addressed in the EIA study. Terms of Reference are finalised.
3. **Impact Analysis / Prediction**: Detailed study of baseline environmental conditions and prediction of impacts of the proposed project on air, water, land, flora, fauna, socio-economic conditions, etc.
4. **Mitigation Measures**: Formulation of measures to avoid, reduce or compensate for adverse impacts. An Environmental Management Plan (EMP) is prepared.
5. **Public Consultation**: Involves consultation with local affected persons and others who have a plausible stake. Public hearing is conducted at the project site.
6. **Appraisal**: Expert Appraisal Committees examine the EIA report, EMP and outcome of public consultation and make recommendations.
7. **Decision Making**: The regulatory authority (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change for Category A; State Environment Impact Assessment Authority for Category B) grants or rejects environmental clearance with conditions.
8. **Post-Clearance Monitoring**: Compliance with the conditions of clearance is monitored.

### Categories of Projects

- **Category A**: Projects with potentially significant impacts; require clearance from the Central Government.
- **Category B**: Projects with less significant impacts; require clearance from the State Level Authority. Category B is further divided into B1 (require EIA) and B2 (do not require EIA).

### Importance of EIA

- Integrates environmental considerations into development planning.
- Helps in selecting environmentally sound alternatives.
- Provides a structured opportunity for public participation.
- Reduces the risk of environmental disasters and long-term costs.
- Facilitates sustainable development.

## Pollution: Types, Sources and Effects

### Air Pollution

Air pollution is the presence of contaminants in the atmosphere in quantities and for durations that can be injurious to human health, plant or animal life, or property, or that interfere with the comfortable enjoyment of life.

**Major Pollutants**:
- Particulate matter (PM10 and PM2.5)
- Sulphur dioxide (SO₂)
- Nitrogen oxides (NOx)
- Carbon monoxide (CO)
- Ozone (O₃) at ground level
- Lead and other heavy metals
- Volatile organic compounds

**Sources**: Vehicular emissions, industrial emissions, thermal power plants, biomass burning, construction activities, dust.

**Effects**: Respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, reduced visibility, damage to vegetation and materials, contribution to climate change and acid rain.

### Water Pollution

Water pollution is the contamination of water bodies by harmful substances that make the water unfit for desired uses.

**Sources**:
- Domestic sewage
- Industrial effluents
- Agricultural runoff containing fertilisers and pesticides
- Oil spills
- Solid waste dumping

**Effects**: Spread of water-borne diseases, eutrophication, destruction of aquatic ecosystems, contamination of groundwater, reduced availability of potable water.

### Soil Pollution

Soil pollution results from the presence of toxic chemicals, salts, disease-causing agents or radioactive substances in the soil that adversely affect plant growth and animal health.

**Sources**: Excessive use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides, industrial waste, municipal solid waste, mining activities, oil spills.

**Effects**: Reduced soil fertility, contamination of food chain, groundwater pollution, loss of soil biodiversity.

### Noise Pollution

Noise pollution is unwanted or excessive sound that can have adverse effects on human health and environmental quality.

**Sources**: Traffic, industrial machinery, construction, loudspeakers, aircraft.

**Effects**: Hearing impairment, stress, sleep disturbance, reduced productivity, interference with communication.

## Legal and Institutional Framework for Pollution Control

### Major Legislations

- **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974**: Provides for the prevention and control of water pollution and the establishment of Central and State Pollution Control Boards.
- **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981**: Provides for the prevention, control and abatement of air pollution.
- **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**: An umbrella legislation that empowers the Central Government to take measures to protect and improve the environment and to prevent, control and abate environmental pollution. Rules for hazardous waste, biomedical waste, municipal solid waste, plastic waste, e-waste, etc., have been framed under this Act.
- **Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000**.
- **Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules**.
- **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016**.
- **Plastic Waste Management Rules**.
- **Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules**.
- **E-Waste Management Rules**.
- **Construction and Demolition Waste Management Rules**.

### Institutional Mechanism

- **Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)**: Nodal ministry at the Centre.
- **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)**: Technical arm of the MoEFCC; coordinates the activities of State Boards, provides technical assistance, and lays down standards.
- **State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs)** and Pollution Control Committees in Union Territories: Implement the pollution control laws at the state level, grant consents, and monitor compliance.
- **National Green Tribunal (NGT)**: Established under the NGT Act, 2010 for effective and expeditious disposal of cases relating to environmental protection and conservation of forests and other natural resources.

### National Ambient Air Quality Standards and Water Quality Criteria

The CPCB has notified National Ambient Air Quality Standards for twelve pollutants. Water quality criteria have been specified for different designated best uses (drinking water source, outdoor bathing, wildlife propagation, irrigation, industrial cooling, etc.).

## Key Concepts for Retention

- EIA is a decision-making tool that integrates environmental concerns into development projects.
- Public consultation is a mandatory component of the EIA process for most projects.
- Pollution control is governed by a comprehensive set of laws under the framework of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.
- CPCB and SPCBs form the institutional backbone of pollution regulation.
- The National Green Tribunal provides a specialised judicial forum for environmental disputes.

A clear understanding of EIA procedures and the pollution control framework is necessary for both Prelims and Mains questions on environmental governance and sustainable development.
