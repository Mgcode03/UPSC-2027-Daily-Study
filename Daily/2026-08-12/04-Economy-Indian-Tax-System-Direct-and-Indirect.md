# Indian Tax System: Direct and Indirect Taxes – Conceptual Framework

## Basic Classification of Taxes

Taxes are compulsory levies imposed by the government to finance public expenditure and achieve economic and social objectives. In India, taxes are broadly classified into:

- **Direct Taxes**: Levied directly on the income or wealth of persons and companies. The burden cannot be shifted to another person. Examples: Income Tax, Corporation Tax, Capital Gains Tax, Wealth Tax (now abolished).
- **Indirect Taxes**: Levied on goods and services. The burden can be shifted from the person who pays the tax to the government (e.g., manufacturer or seller) to the final consumer. Examples: Goods and Services Tax (GST), Customs Duty, Excise Duty (on a few items outside GST).

## Constitutional Division of Taxing Powers

The Constitution of India distributes taxation powers between the Union and the States through the Union List, State List, and Concurrent List (Seventh Schedule).

- Union has exclusive power over corporation tax, customs duties, income tax (except agricultural income), etc.
- States have power over agricultural income tax, land revenue, excise on alcohol, stamp duties, etc.
- GST is a concurrent tax with a unique sharing mechanism between Centre and States.

Article 265 states that no tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law.

## Direct Tax System in India

### Income Tax

Governed by the Income Tax Act, 1961. Income is classified under five heads:

1. Income from Salaries
2. Income from House Property
3. Profits and Gains of Business or Profession
4. Capital Gains
5. Income from Other Sources

Key concepts:

- **Progressive taxation**: Higher rates on higher slabs of income. This is intended to promote equity.
- **Tax Base**: The total income on which tax is calculated after deductions and exemptions.
- **Tax Incidence**: Who ultimately bears the economic burden.
- **Tax Buoyancy**: Responsiveness of tax revenue to changes in GDP. A buoyancy greater than 1 indicates that revenue grows faster than GDP.
- **Tax Elasticity**: Responsiveness after adjusting for discretionary changes in tax policy.

Corporate tax is levied on the profits of companies. Successive reforms have aimed at reducing rates while broadening the base and removing exemptions.

### Capital Gains Tax

Tax on the profit arising from the transfer of a capital asset. Distinction is made between short-term and long-term capital gains, with different rates and indexation benefits for long-term gains.

## Indirect Tax System – Goods and Services Tax (GST)

GST, introduced in 2017, is the most significant indirect tax reform since Independence. It subsumed a large number of Central and State taxes (excise duty, service tax, VAT, entry tax, etc.).

### Structure of GST

- **CGST**: Central Goods and Services Tax (collected by Centre).
- **SGST**: State Goods and Services Tax (collected by States).
- **IGST**: Integrated Goods and Services Tax (on inter-State supplies; collected by Centre and apportioned).
- **UTGST**: Union Territory Goods and Services Tax.

GST is a destination-based consumption tax. The tax accrues to the State where the goods or services are consumed, not where they are produced.

### Key Features

- Dual GST model reflecting India's federal structure.
- Input Tax Credit (ITC) mechanism to avoid cascading of taxes (tax on tax).
- Multiple rate structure (currently 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28% plus compensation cess on certain luxury and sin goods).
- Threshold exemptions for small businesses.
- Composition scheme for small taxpayers with simplified compliance.

### Advantages of GST

- Removal of cascading effect.
- Creation of a common national market.
- Improved tax compliance through the invoice-matching system.
- Higher revenue productivity over the medium term.
- Simplified tax structure compared to the earlier multiplicity of taxes.

### Challenges

- Complexity of multiple rates and frequent changes in rates or classifications.
- Compliance burden on small and medium enterprises, especially in the initial years.
- Issues related to refund of ITC, particularly for exporters.
- Coordination between Centre and States in the GST Council.

## Customs Duties

Levied on imports (and to a limited extent on exports). Serve both revenue and protective purposes. India has progressively reduced peak customs duties as part of trade liberalisation, while retaining higher duties on certain sensitive sectors.

## Principles of a Good Tax System

Adam Smith’s canons remain relevant:

1. **Equity**: Ability to pay (vertical and horizontal equity).
2. **Certainty**: Clear rules regarding amount, time, and manner of payment.
3. **Convenience**: Ease of payment for the taxpayer.
4. **Economy**: Low cost of collection relative to revenue raised.

Modern additions include efficiency (minimal distortion of economic decisions), flexibility, and simplicity.

## Tax Reforms Trajectory in India

- 1990s onwards: Reduction in peak rates, simplification, and broadening of base.
- Introduction of computerisation and Permanent Account Number (PAN).
- GST (2017) as the landmark indirect tax reform.
- Faceless assessment and appeals in direct taxes to reduce human interface and improve transparency.
- Ongoing efforts to simplify the Income Tax Act and improve taxpayer services.

## Conceptual Linkages for UPSC

- Tax policy is an instrument of fiscal policy (alongside expenditure and borrowing).
- Progressivity of direct taxes versus regressivity concerns in indirect taxes (though GST with multiple rates and exemptions attempts to address equity).
- Centre-State fiscal relations are deeply affected by the design and sharing of GST and other taxes.
- Tax-to-GDP ratio is a key indicator of the fiscal capacity of the government.

Understanding the structure, principles, and evolution of the Indian tax system is essential for analysing public finance, fiscal federalism, and economic policy.
