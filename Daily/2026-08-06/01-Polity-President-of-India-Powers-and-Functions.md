# President of India: Powers and Functions

## Introduction to the Office

The President of India is the constitutional head of the State. Article 52 of the Constitution states that there shall be a President of India. The President is the first citizen of India and occupies the highest office in the country. All executive actions of the Union Government are taken in the name of the President. The President is the formal head of the executive, the legislature, and the judiciary in a ceremonial capacity, while real executive power is exercised by the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister under Article 74.

The office of the President embodies the unity and integrity of the nation. The President represents the Republic both within the country and in international relations. The Constitution carefully balances the dignity of the office with the principles of parliamentary democracy and collective responsibility.

## Election of the President

The President is elected by an electoral college consisting of:
- Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha)
- Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States
- Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry

Nominated members of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies do not participate in the election. The election is held in accordance with the system of proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote. The voting is by secret ballot.

The value of votes of Members of Parliament and Members of Legislative Assemblies is calculated on the basis of the population of the states as per the 1971 Census (frozen by the 84th Amendment until the year after the first census taken after 2026). This ensures uniformity in the scale of representation.

A candidate must secure a majority of the total valid votes cast to be declared elected. If no candidate secures the required quota in the first count, the votes of the candidate with the least votes are transferred according to second preferences, and the process continues until one candidate reaches the quota.

## Qualifications and Conditions of Office

A person is eligible for election as President if he or she:
- Is a citizen of India
- Has completed the age of 35 years
- Is qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha
- Does not hold any office of profit under the Government of India or the Government of any State or under any local or other authority subject to the control of any of the said Governments (certain offices are exempted)

The President holds office for a term of five years from the date on which he enters upon his office. The President is eligible for re-election. The President may resign by writing under his hand addressed to the Vice-President. The President can be removed from office by impeachment for violation of the Constitution. The impeachment process can be initiated in either House of Parliament and requires a two-thirds majority of the total membership of each House.

## Executive Powers

All executive power of the Union is vested in the President and is exercised by him either directly or through officers subordinate to him in accordance with the Constitution. In practice, these powers are exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers.

Key executive powers include:
- Appointment of the Prime Minister and other Ministers
- Appointment of the Attorney General of India
- Appointment of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India
- Appointment of the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners
- Appointment of the Chairman and members of the Union Public Service Commission
- Appointment of Governors of States
- Appointment of Ambassadors and High Commissioners
- Appointment of the Chief Justice and other Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts
- Power to make treaties and agreements with foreign countries (subject to parliamentary approval where required)
- Power to declare war and conclude peace (subject to parliamentary control)
- Power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment, or to suspend, remit or commute the sentence of any person convicted of any offence (Article 72)

The President is the Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces of the Union. The President can declare a national emergency under Article 352, a state emergency under Article 356, and a financial emergency under Article 360, subject to the conditions laid down in the Constitution.

## Legislative Powers

The President is an integral part of the Parliament of India. The President summons, prorogues, and dissolves the Lok Sabha. The President addresses both Houses of Parliament assembled together at the commencement of the first session after each general election and at the commencement of the first session of each year.

The President nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha from among persons having special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art, and social service. The President also nominates two members to the Lok Sabha from the Anglo-Indian community if, in his opinion, that community is not adequately represented (this provision was discontinued by the 104th Amendment).

Every bill passed by Parliament requires the assent of the President to become law. The President may:
- Give assent
- Withhold assent
- Return the bill (if it is not a Money Bill) for reconsideration

If the bill is passed again by Parliament with or without amendments and presented to the President, the President shall not withhold assent. For Money Bills, the President cannot return them for reconsideration and must either give or withhold assent.

The President can promulgate ordinances under Article 123 when both Houses of Parliament are not in session, if he is satisfied that circumstances exist which render it necessary for him to take immediate action. An ordinance has the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament but must be laid before both Houses and ceases to operate at the expiration of six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament, or earlier if disapproved.

## Financial Powers

No Money Bill can be introduced in Parliament except on the recommendation of the President. The President causes the Annual Financial Statement (Union Budget) to be laid before both Houses of Parliament. Contingency Fund of India is placed at the disposal of the President to enable advances to be made for meeting unforeseen expenditure pending authorisation by Parliament.

## Judicial Powers

The President has the power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment under Article 72. This power extends to cases where the punishment or sentence is by a Court Martial, where the punishment or sentence is for an offence against any law relating to a matter to which the executive power of the Union extends, and in all cases where the sentence is a sentence of death.

The President appoints the Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts after consultation with such Judges of the Supreme Court and of the High Courts as he may deem necessary. The President can also seek the advisory opinion of the Supreme Court under Article 143 on any question of law or fact of public importance.

## Diplomatic and Military Powers

The President represents India in international forums and receives credentials of foreign ambassadors. All international treaties and agreements are negotiated and concluded in the name of the President. The President is the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces and has the power to declare war or conclude peace, subject to parliamentary control.

## Discretionary Powers

Although the President generally acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, there are certain situations where the President may exercise discretion:
- Appointment of the Prime Minister when no party has a clear majority
- Dismissal of a Ministry that has lost the confidence of the Lok Sabha but refuses to resign
- Dissolution of the Lok Sabha when the Council of Ministers advises it but the President is not satisfied that the Ministry still commands majority support
- Seeking information from the Prime Minister regarding administration of the Union and proposals for legislation

These discretionary areas are limited and have been interpreted cautiously by constitutional practice and judicial pronouncements to preserve the parliamentary character of the system.

## Position in the Constitutional Scheme

The President is the symbol of the nation. While the real executive authority rests with the Council of Ministers, the President provides continuity, dignity, and a check in exceptional circumstances. The office ensures that the democratic process operates within the framework of the Constitution. The relationship between the President and the Council of Ministers is one of mutual respect and constitutional propriety, guided by the principle that the President shall act on the advice of the Council of Ministers except in the narrowly defined discretionary areas.

Understanding the powers and functions of the President is essential for grasping the working of the Indian parliamentary system, the separation and balance of powers, and the federal structure of the Constitution.
