# World Geography Basics: Continents, Oceans, and Major Features

## The Earth as a Planet

The Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known planet that supports life. It has a slightly oblate spheroid shape, flattened at the poles and bulging at the equator. The equatorial diameter is approximately 12,756 km, while the polar diameter is about 12,714 km. The total surface area of the Earth is approximately 510 million square kilometres, of which about 71 per cent is covered by water and 29 per cent by land.

The Earth rotates on its axis from west to east, completing one rotation in about 24 hours, which causes day and night. It revolves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit, completing one revolution in approximately 365.25 days, which causes the seasons. The axis of the Earth is tilted at an angle of about 23.5 degrees with respect to the perpendicular to the plane of its orbit. This tilt is responsible for the variation in the length of day and night and the occurrence of seasons.

## Continents of the World

There are seven continents on Earth. In order of size (from largest to smallest by area):

### Asia
Asia is the largest continent, covering about 30 per cent of the Earth's land area. It is bounded by the Arctic Ocean in the north, the Pacific Ocean in the east, the Indian Ocean in the south, and Europe and Africa in the west. The Ural Mountains, the Caspian Sea, and the Caucasus Mountains are conventionally taken as the boundary between Asia and Europe. Asia contains the highest point on Earth, Mount Everest (8,848.86 m) in the Himalayas, and the lowest point on land, the Dead Sea shore. It is home to the largest population of any continent and includes diverse climatic zones ranging from arctic to equatorial.

### Africa
Africa is the second-largest continent. It is almost equally divided by the Equator and is the only continent that stretches from the northern temperate zone to the southern temperate zone. The Sahara Desert in the north is the largest hot desert in the world. Africa is bounded by the Mediterranean Sea in the north, the Atlantic Ocean in the west, the Indian Ocean in the east and south, and the Red Sea and the Suez Canal in the northeast. The continent is rich in mineral resources and has a wide variety of ecosystems, including tropical rainforests, savannas, and deserts.

### North America
North America is the third-largest continent. It is bounded by the Arctic Ocean in the north, the Atlantic Ocean in the east, the Pacific Ocean in the west, and South America in the south (connected by the Isthmus of Panama). The Rocky Mountains form a major mountain system in the west, while the Appalachian Mountains lie in the east. The Great Plains occupy the central part. North America includes the world's largest island, Greenland, and the Great Lakes, which form the largest group of freshwater lakes by area.

### South America
South America is the fourth-largest continent. It is connected to North America by the Isthmus of Panama and is bounded by the Caribbean Sea in the north, the Atlantic Ocean in the east, the Pacific Ocean in the west, and the Southern Ocean in the south. The Andes Mountains, the longest continental mountain range in the world, run along the western edge of the continent. The Amazon Basin contains the world's largest tropical rainforest and the Amazon River, the largest river by discharge volume.

### Antarctica
Antarctica is the fifth-largest continent and the southernmost. It is almost entirely covered by ice and is the coldest, driest, and windiest continent. It has no permanent human population. The continent is governed under the Antarctic Treaty System, which reserves it for peaceful and scientific purposes. The ice sheet of Antarctica contains about 70 per cent of the world's freshwater.

### Europe
Europe is the sixth-largest continent by area but has a high population density. It is bounded by the Arctic Ocean in the north, the Atlantic Ocean in the west, the Mediterranean Sea in the south, and Asia in the east. The continent has a highly indented coastline and numerous peninsulas. The Alps, the Carpathians, and the Scandinavian Mountains are major mountain systems. Europe has played a central role in world history, culture, and economic development.

### Australia
Australia is the smallest continent and the only continent that is also a single country. It is surrounded by the Indian and Pacific Oceans. The continent is relatively flat, with the Great Dividing Range in the east. A large part of the interior is desert or semi-arid. Australia is known for its unique flora and fauna, much of which is endemic due to long isolation.

Sometimes Oceania is referred to as a region that includes Australia, New Zealand, and the Pacific island groups (Melanesia, Micronesia, and Polynesia).

## Oceans of the World

There are five major oceans:

### Pacific Ocean
The Pacific is the largest and deepest ocean, covering more than one-third of the Earth's surface. It is bounded by Asia and Australia in the west and the Americas in the east. The Mariana Trench, located in the western Pacific, is the deepest known point on Earth (approximately 11,000 m). The Pacific is characterised by the Ring of Fire, a zone of frequent earthquakes and volcanic activity along its margins.

### Atlantic Ocean
The Atlantic is the second-largest ocean. It separates the Americas from Europe and Africa. It is relatively narrow compared to the Pacific and has a mid-ocean ridge running roughly north-south. The Atlantic has been the most important ocean for navigation and trade in modern history. It includes major seas such as the Mediterranean, the Caribbean, and the Gulf of Mexico.

### Indian Ocean
The Indian Ocean is the third-largest ocean and is largely bounded by land on three sides: Asia in the north, Africa in the west, and Australia in the east. It is warmer than the other oceans and has a unique monsoon circulation system. The Indian Ocean is strategically important for trade routes connecting Asia, Africa, and Europe.

### Southern Ocean
The Southern Ocean surrounds Antarctica and is defined by the Antarctic Convergence, where cold Antarctic waters meet the warmer waters of the Pacific, Atlantic, and Indian Oceans. It is characterised by strong westerly winds (the Roaring Forties, Furious Fifties, and Screaming Sixties) and the Antarctic Circumpolar Current, the largest ocean current in the world.

### Arctic Ocean
The Arctic Ocean is the smallest and shallowest of the five oceans. It is located around the North Pole and is largely covered by sea ice. It is bordered by the northern coasts of North America, Europe, and Asia. Climate change has led to significant reduction in the extent of Arctic sea ice, with implications for global climate and navigation.

## Major Latitudes and Longitudes

Important parallels of latitude include:
- Equator (0 degrees): Divides the Earth into Northern and Southern Hemispheres
- Tropic of Cancer (23.5 degrees North): Northern limit of the tropical zone
- Tropic of Capricorn (23.5 degrees South): Southern limit of the tropical zone
- Arctic Circle (66.5 degrees North): Southern limit of the Arctic region
- Antarctic Circle (66.5 degrees South): Northern limit of the Antarctic region

Important meridians include the Prime Meridian (0 degrees longitude) passing through Greenwich, and the International Date Line (approximately 180 degrees longitude).

## Major Mountain Ranges and Plateaus

Important mountain ranges of the world include the Himalayas, the Andes, the Rockies, the Alps, the Atlas, the Urals, and the Great Dividing Range. Major plateaus include the Tibetan Plateau (the highest and largest), the Deccan Plateau, the Brazilian Plateau, the African Plateau, and the Colorado Plateau.

## Significance for UPSC

A clear understanding of the basic physical framework of the Earth—continents, oceans, major landforms, and the grid of latitudes and longitudes—provides the foundation for studying climate, natural resources, human geography, international relations, and environmental issues. Map-based questions in Prelims frequently test knowledge of the location of these features and their interrelationships.
