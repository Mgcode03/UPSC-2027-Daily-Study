# Making of the Indian Constitution

## Background and Demand for a Constituent Assembly

The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward by M.N. Roy in 1934. The Indian National Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly in 1935. The British Government accepted the demand in principle in the August Offer of 1940, but the concrete proposal came with the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946.

The Cabinet Mission proposed a Constituent Assembly of 389 members. Of these, 292 were to be elected from the British Indian provinces, 93 from the Princely States, and 4 from the Chief Commissioners' Provinces. The members from the provinces were elected by the Provincial Legislative Assemblies on the basis of communal representation (General, Muslim, and Sikh seats) through the method of proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.

The elections to the Constituent Assembly were held in July-August 1946. The Congress secured an overwhelming majority of the general seats, while the Muslim League secured most of the Muslim seats. The Muslim League initially participated but later withdrew and demanded a separate Constituent Assembly for Pakistan.

## Composition of the Constituent Assembly

After the partition of India under the Mountbatten Plan, the membership of the Constituent Assembly was reduced to 299. The Assembly included representatives of the provinces that remained in India and the Princely States that acceded to India. The Assembly was a body of eminent leaders, jurists, scholars, and social reformers representing diverse regions, communities, and interests.

Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was the temporary President of the Assembly. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected as the permanent President. The Assembly had a distinguished membership that included Jawaharlal Nehru, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, and many others.

Women members such as Sarojini Naidu, Hansa Mehta, Durgabai Deshmukh, and Rajkumari Amrit Kaur also made significant contributions. The Assembly represented a broad spectrum of Indian society and political opinion.

## Working of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly held its first meeting on 9 December 1946. The Muslim League boycotted the meeting. On 13 December 1946, Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution, which laid down the fundamentals and philosophy of the constitutional structure. The Resolution was adopted on 22 January 1947.

The Objectives Resolution declared the Assembly's firm and solemn resolve to proclaim India as an Independent Sovereign Republic and to draw up for her future governance a Constitution wherein the territories that now comprise British India, the territories that now form the Indian States, and such other territories as are willing to be constituted into the Independent Sovereign India, shall be a Union of them all.

It further resolved that the Constitution would guarantee and secure to all the people of India justice (social, economic, and political), equality of status, of opportunity, and before the law, freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith, worship, vocation, association, and action, subject to law and public morality. Adequate safeguards were to be provided for minorities, backward and tribal areas, and depressed and other backward classes.

## Committees of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly appointed several committees to deal with different aspects of the Constitution. The most important was the Drafting Committee, chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar. Other major committees included:

- Union Powers Committee (Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru)
- Union Constitution Committee (Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru)
- Provincial Constitution Committee (Chairman: Sardar Patel)
- Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities, and Tribal and Excluded Areas (Chairman: Sardar Patel)
- Rules of Procedure Committee (Chairman: Dr. Rajendra Prasad)
- States Committee (Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru)
- Steering Committee (Chairman: Dr. Rajendra Prasad)

The Drafting Committee was appointed on 29 August 1947. It consisted of seven members: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman), N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadullah, B.L. Mitter (later replaced by N. Madhava Rau), and D.P. Khaitan (later replaced by T.T. Krishnamachari).

## Drafting and Debates

The Drafting Committee prepared the first draft of the Constitution, which was published in February 1948. The people of India were given eight months to discuss the draft and propose amendments. The Drafting Committee then prepared a second draft, which was introduced in the Assembly on 4 November 1948 by Dr. Ambedkar.

The Assembly discussed the draft clause by clause. The debates were thorough, informed, and often passionate. Members raised issues concerning fundamental rights, minority safeguards, federal structure, emergency provisions, language, and the form of government. The Assembly sat for 166 days spread over nearly three years.

Dr. Ambedkar played a pivotal role in explaining the provisions, defending the draft, and accepting useful amendments. His speeches in the Assembly remain a valuable source for understanding the philosophy and intention behind various constitutional provisions.

## Adoption and Enforcement

The Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 26 November 1949. Some provisions relating to citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament, temporary and transitional provisions came into force immediately. The remaining provisions came into force on 26 January 1950, which is celebrated as Republic Day.

The choice of 26 January was deliberate. It was on this day in 1930 that the Indian National Congress had observed Independence Day following the adoption of the Purna Swaraj resolution at the Lahore Session in December 1929.

## Salient Features Emerging from the Making Process

The making of the Constitution reflected several important characteristics:

- It was the product of a deliberative and representative process.
- It drew upon the best features of various constitutions of the world while adapting them to Indian conditions.
- It sought to reconcile the need for a strong Centre with the autonomy of the units.
- It placed fundamental rights at the core of the constitutional scheme while also incorporating Directive Principles to guide the State in establishing a just social order.
- It provided for a parliamentary form of government with a republican character.
- It included elaborate provisions for the protection of minorities and the upliftment of weaker sections.
- It created independent institutions such as the Election Commission, the Comptroller and Auditor General, and the Union Public Service Commission to safeguard democracy and accountability.

## Sources of the Constitution

The Constitution of India is a unique blend of various sources:
- Government of India Act, 1935: Federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary, public service commissions, emergency provisions, and administrative details
- British Constitution: Parliamentary form of government, rule of law, legislative procedure, single citizenship, cabinet system, prerogative writs, parliamentary privileges, and bicameralism
- American Constitution: Fundamental rights, independence of judiciary, judicial review, impeachment of the President, removal of judges, and the Preamble
- Irish Constitution: Directive Principles of State Policy, nomination of members to the Rajya Sabha, and method of election of the President
- Canadian Constitution: Federation with a strong Centre, residuary powers with the Centre, appointment of State Governors by the Centre, and advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court
- Australian Constitution: Concurrent List, freedom of trade and commerce, and joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament
- Weimar Constitution of Germany: Suspension of fundamental rights during emergency
- South African Constitution: Procedure for amendment of the Constitution and election of members of the Rajya Sabha
- French Constitution: Republic and the ideals of liberty, equality, and fraternity in the Preamble

The framers did not merely copy these provisions. They adapted them carefully to suit the Indian social, economic, and political context.

## Significance of the Constituent Assembly Debates

The debates of the Constituent Assembly remain an important aid to the interpretation of the Constitution. The Supreme Court has often referred to the debates to understand the original intention of the framers. The debates reveal the careful balancing of competing interests, the commitment to democracy and social justice, and the vision of a modern, inclusive India.

The making of the Indian Constitution is one of the most remarkable achievements of modern political history. It transformed a diverse, hierarchical, and colonially administered society into a sovereign democratic republic based on the principles of justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity.
