# Labour, Employment and Social Security in India

## Structure of the Indian Labour Force

The Indian labour force is characterised by a large informal sector, significant underemployment, and a high share of self-employment and casual labour. The Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) provides the main official data on employment and unemployment.

Key features include:

- Dominance of the informal sector (more than 80% of the workforce in many estimates).
- High proportion of workers in agriculture, though the share has been declining.
- Rising participation of women in certain segments, but overall female labour force participation remains relatively low.
- Large youth population entering the labour market every year, creating pressure for job creation.

## Types of Unemployment

Unemployment in India takes several forms:

- **Open unemployment**: Persons willing and able to work at the prevailing wage but unable to find work.
- **Disguised unemployment**: More people are engaged in an activity than are required; removal of some does not reduce output. Common in agriculture.
- **Seasonal unemployment**: Work is available only during certain seasons (agriculture, tourism, construction).
- **Underemployment**: Persons working fewer hours than they are willing and able to work, or working in jobs that do not fully utilise their skills.
- **Structural unemployment**: Mismatch between the skills of the workforce and the requirements of available jobs.
- **Frictional unemployment**: Temporary unemployment while moving between jobs.

## Labour Laws and Reforms

India historically had a large number of central and state labour laws. In recent years, 29 central labour laws have been consolidated into four Labour Codes:

1. **Code on Wages**: Seeks to ensure minimum wages and timely payment across all employments.
2. **Industrial Relations Code**: Deals with trade unions, conditions of employment, and industrial disputes.
3. **Code on Social Security**: Extends social security coverage to a wider range of workers, including gig and platform workers in principle.
4. **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code**: Consolidates laws relating to safety, health and working conditions.

The codes aim to simplify compliance, improve ease of doing business, and expand the coverage of social security while protecting workers’ rights. Implementation remains a shared responsibility of the Centre and the states.

## Employment Generation and Skill Development

Government interventions focus on both demand-side and supply-side measures.

- Public employment programmes such as the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme provide a legal guarantee of 100 days of wage employment in rural areas.
- Skill development initiatives aim to improve the employability of the workforce through short-term training, apprenticeships and industry-linked programmes.
- Promotion of labour-intensive manufacturing, micro, small and medium enterprises, and the services sector is seen as critical for absorbing the growing labour force.

## Social Security

Social security in India has traditionally been limited mainly to the organised sector. Key instruments include:

- Employees’ Provident Fund and Employees’ Pension Scheme.
- Employees’ State Insurance for medical and cash benefits.
- Various pension and insurance schemes for unorganised workers, including the Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maan-dhan and other recent initiatives.
- Health insurance schemes aimed at reducing out-of-pocket expenditure for the poor.

The expansion of social security to the unorganised and gig economy workforce is an ongoing policy challenge. Portability of benefits, digital delivery and contribution mechanisms suited to irregular incomes are important design issues.

## Key Challenges

- Creating sufficient quality employment for a large and young population.
- Raising female labour force participation.
- Bridging the skill mismatch between education outcomes and industry requirements.
- Extending social security and labour protections to informal workers without imposing excessive compliance burdens that discourage formalisation.
- Managing the transition associated with technological change, automation and the platform economy.

A balanced approach that combines faster economic growth, labour market flexibility with adequate protection, and continuous skill upgradation is central to addressing India’s employment challenge.
