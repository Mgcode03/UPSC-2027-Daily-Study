# CSAT: Geometry and Mensuration Basics

## Fundamental Geometric Terms

- **Point**: A location with no dimension.
- **Line**: Extends infinitely in both directions; has no thickness.
- **Line segment**: A part of a line with two endpoints.
- **Ray**: A part of a line with one endpoint, extending infinitely in one direction.
- **Angle**: Formed by two rays sharing a common endpoint (vertex). Measured in degrees.

Types of angles:
- Acute: less than 90°
- Right: exactly 90°
- Obtuse: greater than 90° but less than 180°
- Straight: exactly 180°
- Reflex: greater than 180° but less than 360°

## Triangles

Sum of interior angles of a triangle is always 180°.

### Classification by Sides
- Equilateral: all three sides equal; all angles 60°.
- Isosceles: two sides equal; base angles equal.
- Scalene: all sides different.

### Classification by Angles
- Acute-angled: all angles acute.
- Right-angled: one angle 90°.
- Obtuse-angled: one angle obtuse.

### Important Properties and Theorems

**Pythagoras Theorem**: In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. If a and b are the legs and c the hypotenuse, then a² + b² = c².

**Converse of Pythagoras**: If a² + b² = c², the triangle is right-angled at the angle opposite side c.

**Triangle Inequality**: The sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side.

**Exterior Angle Theorem**: An exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles.

### Congruence of Triangles

Two triangles are congruent if their corresponding sides and angles are equal. Criteria:
- SSS (three sides)
- SAS (two sides and included angle)
- ASA (two angles and included side)
- RHS (right angle, hypotenuse, and one side)

### Similarity of Triangles

Triangles are similar if corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are proportional. Criteria include AAA, SAS similarity, and SSS similarity. The ratio of corresponding sides is the scale factor. The ratio of areas is the square of the scale factor.

## Quadrilaterals

Sum of interior angles is 360°.

- **Parallelogram**: Opposite sides parallel and equal; opposite angles equal; diagonals bisect each other.
- **Rectangle**: Parallelogram with all angles 90°; diagonals equal.
- **Rhombus**: Parallelogram with all sides equal; diagonals bisect each other at right angles.
- **Square**: Rectangle with all sides equal (or rhombus with right angles).
- **Trapezium (Trapezoid)**: Exactly one pair of parallel sides (in some definitions).
- **Kite**: Two pairs of adjacent sides equal; diagonals perpendicular.

## Circles

- **Radius**: Distance from centre to any point on the circle.
- **Diameter**: Twice the radius; longest chord.
- **Chord**: Line segment joining two points on the circle.
- **Arc**: Part of the circumference.
- **Sector**: Region bounded by two radii and an arc.
- **Segment**: Region bounded by a chord and an arc.

Important theorems:
- The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
- Equal chords are equidistant from the centre.
- Angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle subtended at any point on the remaining circumference.
- Angle in a semicircle is a right angle.
- Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°.

## Mensuration: Areas and Perimeters

### Plane Figures

- **Rectangle**: Perimeter = 2(l + b); Area = l × b
- **Square**: Perimeter = 4a; Area = a²
- **Triangle**: Area = (1/2) × base × height. Also, by Heron’s formula if all three sides are known: √[s(s–a)(s–b)(s–c)] where s is the semi-perimeter.
- **Parallelogram**: Area = base × height
- **Rhombus**: Area = base × height, or (1/2) × product of diagonals
- **Trapezium**: Area = (1/2) × (sum of parallel sides) × height
- **Circle**: Circumference = 2πr or πd; Area = πr²
- **Sector**: Area = (θ/360) × πr²; Arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr

### Solid Figures

- **Cuboid**: Volume = l × b × h; Total surface area = 2(lb + bh + hl)
- **Cube**: Volume = a³; Total surface area = 6a²
- **Cylinder**: Volume = πr²h; Curved surface area = 2πrh; Total surface area = 2πr(h + r)
- **Cone**: Volume = (1/3)πr²h; Curved surface area = πrl (l = slant height); Total surface area = πr(l + r)
- **Sphere**: Volume = (4/3)πr³; Surface area = 4πr²
- **Hemisphere**: Volume = (2/3)πr³; Curved surface area = 2πr²; Total surface area = 3πr²

## Practical Approach for CSAT

CSAT questions typically test the ability to apply these formulas and properties under time pressure. Common patterns include:

- Finding a missing side or angle using Pythagoras or angle sum properties.
- Comparing areas or perimeters of different figures.
- Using similarity to find unknown lengths.
- Calculating volumes or surface areas of combined solids.
- Problems involving paths around gardens, borders, or frames (outer and inner dimensions).

When a figure is described but not drawn, sketching a rough diagram helps. Units must be consistent. For π, questions may ask for answers in terms of π or may specify the value (commonly 22/7 or 3.14).

## Key Formulas to Memorise

- Pythagoras: a² + b² = c²
- Area of triangle: (1/2)bh or Heron’s formula
- Area of circle: πr²
- Volume of cylinder: πr²h
- Volume of cone: (1/3)πr²h
- Volume of sphere: (4/3)πr³
- Surface area of sphere: 4πr²

Mastery of these basics enables quick and accurate solutions to the majority of geometry and mensuration questions appearing in the CSAT paper.
