# Polity: Prime Minister and Cabinet System

## Introduction to the Parliamentary Executive

India follows a parliamentary system of government modelled on the British Westminster system. In this system the real executive power is vested in the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister. The President is the formal head of the state, while the Prime Minister is the head of the government. This distinction between the nominal and real executive is a defining feature of parliamentary democracy.

The Constitution of India does not explicitly define the office of the Prime Minister in great detail, but Articles 74, 75, 77 and 78 together lay down the broad framework. Article 74 states that there shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President. The advice of the Council of Ministers is binding on the President after the 42nd and 44th Amendments.

## Appointment of the Prime Minister

The President appoints the Prime Minister. By convention, the leader of the majority party or coalition in the Lok Sabha is invited to form the government. If no single party has a clear majority, the President may exercise discretion and invite the leader who is most likely to command majority support. This discretion has been exercised on several occasions, including 1979, 1989, 1991, 1996, 1998 and 1999.

Once appointed, the Prime Minister must prove majority support on the floor of the Lok Sabha within a reasonable time. The Prime Minister holds office during the pleasure of the President, but this pleasure is exercised on the basis of majority support in the Lok Sabha. In practice, the Prime Minister remains in office as long as he or she enjoys the confidence of the lower house.

## Powers and Functions of the Prime Minister

The Prime Minister occupies a position of pre-eminence in the Indian political system. The powers can be broadly classified into several categories.

### 1. Head of the Council of Ministers
The Prime Minister selects the ministers and allocates portfolios. The President appoints ministers only on the advice of the Prime Minister. The Prime Minister can reshuffle the cabinet, drop ministers, or accept their resignations. All ministers are collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, but individual ministers are responsible to the Prime Minister for the efficient administration of their departments.

### 2. Leader of the Cabinet
The Prime Minister presides over cabinet meetings, decides the agenda, and guides the deliberations. The cabinet is the highest decision-making body of the government. Decisions are generally taken by consensus, but the Prime Minister’s views carry decisive weight. The Prime Minister can overrule a minister or even the majority of the cabinet in exceptional circumstances, though such instances are rare.

### 3. Link between President and Council of Ministers
Article 78 imposes three specific duties on the Prime Minister:
- To communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation.
- To furnish such information relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation as the President may call for.
- If the President so requires, to submit for the consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by a minister but which has not been considered by the Council.

These provisions ensure that the President remains informed and that individual ministers do not take decisions without collective scrutiny.

### 4. Leader of Parliament
The Prime Minister is the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha and, by extension, the leader of the House. He or she announces government policies, answers questions on major issues, and intervenes in important debates. The Prime Minister also advises the President on summoning and proroguing sessions of Parliament and on the dissolution of the Lok Sabha.

### 5. Chief Spokesperson of the Government
The Prime Minister is the principal communicator of government policy both inside and outside Parliament. Foreign policy statements, major economic announcements, and responses to national crises are usually made by the Prime Minister or under his or her direction.

### 6. Role in International Relations
As the head of government, the Prime Minister represents India in international forums, conducts high-level diplomacy, and signs treaties (subject to constitutional requirements). Summit-level diplomacy has become increasingly important, and the Prime Minister’s personal equations with foreign leaders often shape bilateral relations.

### 7. Crisis Manager
In times of national emergency, natural disasters, or political instability, the Prime Minister provides leadership and coordinates the response of the entire government machinery.

## The Council of Ministers and the Cabinet

The Council of Ministers is a large body that includes Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State (independent charge), Ministers of State, and Deputy Ministers. The Constitution does not specify the size of the Council, but the 91st Amendment Act, 2003, limited the total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister, to 15 per cent of the total strength of the Lok Sabha.

Within the Council of Ministers, the Cabinet is the smaller, more powerful body. It consists of senior ministers holding important portfolios. The Cabinet is the real policy-making organ of the government. It meets regularly, takes decisions on all major policy matters, and coordinates the work of different ministries.

The distinction between the Council of Ministers and the Cabinet is important:
- The Council of Ministers is the larger body; the Cabinet is a part of it.
- The Cabinet meets more frequently and takes major decisions; the full Council rarely meets as a body.
- Collective responsibility is shared by the entire Council, but the Cabinet is the effective decision-making unit.

## Collective Responsibility

Article 75(3) states that the Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People. This means:
- All ministers swim or sink together.
- A vote of no-confidence against the government leads to the resignation of the entire Council.
- Ministers must support cabinet decisions in public even if they disagreed internally. If a minister cannot accept a decision, the only honourable course is to resign.

Collective responsibility ensures unity of the government and prevents individual ministers from pursuing independent policies.

## Individual Responsibility

In addition to collective responsibility, ministers are individually responsible for the working of their departments. The Prime Minister can ask a minister to resign if he or she loses confidence in the minister’s ability or integrity. The President dismisses a minister only on the advice of the Prime Minister.

## Kitchen Cabinet or Inner Cabinet

In practice, the Prime Minister often relies on a small group of trusted colleagues and advisors known as the Kitchen Cabinet or Inner Cabinet. This informal body helps the Prime Minister in decision-making on sensitive or complex issues. While it is not a constitutional body, it plays an important role in modern governance.

## Relationship with the President

The relationship between the President and the Prime Minister is governed by the principle that the President acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers. After the 42nd Amendment (1976) and the 44th Amendment (1978), the advice is binding. The President can ask the Council to reconsider advice, but if the Council reiterates the advice, the President is bound to accept it.

In certain situations, such as hung Parliament or dissolution of the Lok Sabha, the President retains limited discretionary powers. However, once a government with majority support is in place, the Prime Minister becomes the real centre of power.

## Evolution of the Prime Minister’s Office (PMO)

The Prime Minister’s Office has grown significantly in importance over the decades. It is a staff agency that assists the Prime Minister in the performance of duties. The PMO coordinates the work of various ministries, monitors implementation of important decisions, and provides secretarial assistance. Under different Prime Ministers the PMO has assumed varying degrees of influence, sometimes functioning almost as a parallel decision-making centre.

## Key Constitutional Articles

- Article 74: Council of Ministers to aid and advise the President
- Article 75: Appointment, tenure, and responsibility of Ministers
- Article 77: Conduct of business of the Government of India
- Article 78: Duties of the Prime Minister regarding furnishing of information to the President

## Important Conventions and Practices

- The Prime Minister is always a member of either House of Parliament. If not a member at the time of appointment, he or she must become a member within six months.
- The Prime Minister usually sits in the Lok Sabha, though there have been exceptions.
- Resignation of the Prime Minister leads to the resignation of the entire Council of Ministers.
- The death of the Prime Minister also ends the tenure of the Council; a new Prime Minister is appointed.

## Comparison with Presidential System

In a presidential system (for example, the United States), the President is both head of state and head of government and is not accountable to the legislature on a day-to-day basis. In the Indian parliamentary system, the real executive is continuously accountable to the Lok Sabha. This continuous accountability is considered a major strength of the parliamentary model, though it can also lead to instability if majorities are fragile.

## Summary of Key Points for Memorisation

- Real executive power lies with the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.
- President is the formal head; Prime Minister is the real head of government.
- Appointment is by the President, but based on majority support in Lok Sabha.
- Collective responsibility to Lok Sabha is the cornerstone of the system.
- Prime Minister selects ministers, allocates portfolios, and can drop them.
- Article 78 defines the informational duties of the Prime Minister towards the President.
- Size of Council of Ministers limited to 15% of Lok Sabha strength (91st Amendment).
- Cabinet is the core decision-making body within the larger Council of Ministers.
- PMO has emerged as a powerful coordinating and monitoring agency.

The office of the Prime Minister is the fulcrum of the Indian political system. Understanding its powers, limitations, and relationship with other constitutional functionaries is essential for grasping the working of Indian democracy.
