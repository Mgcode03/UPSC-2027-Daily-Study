# History: Revolutionary and Socialist Streams in the Indian National Movement

## Background and Context

The Indian National Movement was not a monolithic struggle. Alongside the mainstream Congress politics of petitions, constitutional agitation, and later mass satyagraha, there existed powerful revolutionary and socialist currents. These streams believed that British rule could not be ended merely through peaceful methods and that fundamental social and economic transformation was necessary alongside political independence.

The revolutionary stream emerged in the early twentieth century, inspired by the failure of moderate politics, the partition of Bengal (1905), and the example of revolutionary movements in Europe and Ireland. The socialist stream gained strength in the 1920s and 1930s, influenced by the Russian Revolution of 1917 and the growing awareness of class exploitation within Indian society.

## Early Revolutionary Activities (1905–1918)

The Swadeshi Movement following the partition of Bengal provided the immediate background for revolutionary activity. Young nationalists who were impatient with the slow pace of constitutional methods formed secret societies.

### Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar
In Bengal, the Anushilan Samiti (founded around 1902) and the Jugantar group became the principal centres of revolutionary activity. Leaders such as Aurobindo Ghosh, Barindra Kumar Ghosh, and later Jatindranath Mukherjee (Bagha Jatin) organised physical training, ideological education, and plans for armed action. The Alipore Conspiracy Case (1908) involved Aurobindo and several others; although Aurobindo was acquitted, many were sentenced to long terms of imprisonment.

### Revolutionary Activities Outside Bengal
In Maharashtra, the Abhinav Bharat Society founded by Vinayak Damodar Savarkar and his brother Ganesh Savarkar carried out revolutionary propaganda and organised arms training. Savarkar’s book “The Indian War of Independence 1857” reinterpreted the 1857 revolt as a national war of independence and became an inspirational text for revolutionaries.

In Punjab, the Ghadar Movement emerged among Indian immigrants in North America. The Ghadar Party, formed in 1913 in San Francisco under leaders such as Lala Hardayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, and later Kartar Singh Sarabha, aimed at an armed uprising in India with the help of returning migrants. The attempt coincided with the First World War and was suppressed, but it left a lasting impact on Punjab’s revolutionary tradition.

### Individual Acts of Revolution
Several individual acts of violence marked this period. The assassination of British officials, such as the killing of Curzon Wyllie by Madanlal Dhingra in London (1909), and the attempt on the life of Lord Hardinge by Rash Behari Bose and others in 1912, demonstrated the determination of the revolutionaries. These acts were intended to inspire fear among the British and to awaken national consciousness among Indians.

## The Hindustan Republican Association and Hindustan Socialist Republican Association

After the First World War and the Non-Cooperation Movement, a new generation of revolutionaries emerged. In 1924, the Hindustan Republican Association (HRA) was founded by Ram Prasad Bismil, Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee, Sachindra Nath Sanyal, and others. Its aim was to establish a federal republic of the United States of India by an organised and armed revolution.

The Kakori Train Robbery (1925) was the most famous action of the HRA. Revolutionaries stopped a train near Kakori and looted government money to finance their activities. The subsequent trial led to the execution of Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri, and Roshan Singh. Chandrashekhar Azad escaped and continued underground activity.

In 1928, under the influence of socialist ideas, the HRA was reorganised as the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA). Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru became its leading figures. The HSRA explicitly adopted socialist goals and sought to combine the struggle for national independence with the struggle for social and economic equality.

### Major Actions of the HSRA
- The assassination of J.P. Saunders in Lahore (December 1928) in retaliation for the death of Lala Lajpat Rai, who had been injured in a police lathi-charge during the protest against the Simon Commission.
- The throwing of bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly by Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt (April 1929) to protest against repressive laws. They deliberately courted arrest to use the trial as a platform for propaganda.
- The attempt to blow up the Viceroy’s train and other acts of sabotage.

Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru were executed on 23 March 1931. Chandrashekhar Azad died in a police encounter in Alfred Park, Allahabad, in February 1931. Their martyrdom created a powerful emotional impact across the country and inspired a new generation of nationalists.

## Ideology of the Revolutionary Stream

The early revolutionaries were primarily motivated by intense patriotism and the desire to end foreign rule through force. Later revolutionaries, especially those associated with the HSRA, developed a more sophisticated ideology. Bhagat Singh’s writings, particularly “Why I Am an Atheist” and his letters from prison, reveal a commitment to rationalism, atheism, and socialism. He rejected both religious orthodoxy and blind nationalism, arguing that the struggle must aim at the establishment of a society free from exploitation.

The slogan “Inquilab Zindabad” (Long Live the Revolution), popularised by Bhagat Singh, captured the spirit of this phase. The revolutionaries sought not merely a change of rulers but a complete transformation of the social and economic order.

## The Socialist Stream within the Congress

While the revolutionaries operated largely outside the Congress, a socialist current developed within the Congress itself in the 1930s. The influence of the Russian Revolution, the Great Depression, and the growing strength of the working-class and peasant movements created the conditions for the rise of socialist ideas.

### Congress Socialist Party
In 1934, the Congress Socialist Party (CSP) was formed within the Indian National Congress by leaders such as Jayaprakash Narayan, Acharya Narendra Dev, Ram Manohar Lohia, Yusuf Meherally, and Minoo Masani. The CSP accepted the overall leadership of the Congress in the national struggle but sought to give it a socialist orientation. It advocated the abolition of zamindari, nationalisation of key industries, and the organisation of workers and peasants.

The CSP played an important role in radicalising the Congress and in organising mass movements among peasants and workers. It also provided a bridge between the nationalist movement and the emerging leftist forces.

### Jawaharlal Nehru and Socialism
Jawaharlal Nehru was deeply influenced by socialist ideas, especially after his visit to Europe and the Soviet Union in the late 1920s. In his presidential address at the Lucknow Congress (1936), he openly declared his belief in socialism. Although Nehru never formally joined the CSP, his speeches and writings gave legitimacy to socialist thinking within the Congress. He saw political independence and social and economic equality as inseparable goals.

### Other Leftist Groups
The Communist Party of India, though operating under difficult conditions and often banned, also contributed to the radicalisation of the national movement. Peasant organisations such as the All India Kisan Sabha (formed in 1936) and trade unions mobilised large sections of the rural and urban poor around demands for land reform, better wages, and an end to colonial exploitation.

## Relationship with the Mainstream National Movement

The relationship between the revolutionary and socialist streams and the mainstream Congress leadership was complex. Mahatma Gandhi consistently opposed violence and revolutionary methods, believing that they would not lead to true swaraj and would corrupt the moral character of the movement. At the same time, Gandhi and other Congress leaders often expressed admiration for the courage and sacrifice of the revolutionaries.

The Congress under Gandhi’s leadership absorbed some of the radical energy generated by the revolutionaries and socialists, especially during the Civil Disobedience Movement and the Quit India Movement. Many young people who were initially attracted to revolutionary methods later joined the mass movements led by the Congress.

## Legacy and Significance

The revolutionary and socialist streams made several lasting contributions:

1. They kept alive the spirit of resistance during periods when the mass movements were temporarily suspended.
2. They introduced the ideas of socialism, class struggle, and economic equality into the national discourse.
3. They created a powerful tradition of sacrifice and martyrdom that continued to inspire later generations.
4. They pressured the mainstream leadership to adopt more radical social and economic programmes.
5. After independence, many of the ideas associated with these streams influenced the framing of the Directive Principles of State Policy and the early economic policies of independent India.

## Key Personalities and Their Contributions

- **Bhagat Singh**: Symbol of revolutionary patriotism combined with socialist and rationalist thought. His writings remain important texts for understanding the ideological evolution of the movement.
- **Chandrashekhar Azad**: Organiser and military strategist of the HSRA; remained underground till his death.
- **Ram Prasad Bismil and Ashfaqulla Khan**: Exemplified Hindu-Muslim unity in the revolutionary movement through the Kakori action.
- **Surya Sen**: Leader of the Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930); organised a parallel government for a short period.
- **Jayaprakash Narayan and Acharya Narendra Dev**: Architects of the Congress Socialist Party and later important figures in Indian politics.
- **M.N. Roy**: Early communist thinker who later developed the philosophy of Radical Humanism.

## Points for Conceptual Clarity

- Revolutionary nationalism and socialism were not identical; early revolutionaries were primarily political nationalists, while later ones and the CSP added a clear social and economic dimension.
- The HSRA marked a transition from pure nationalism to socialist nationalism.
- The Congress Socialist Party worked within the Congress and sought to transform it from inside, whereas the Communist Party often operated independently or in opposition.
- Gandhi’s non-violence and the revolutionaries’ advocacy of violence represented two different methods, but both shared the goal of ending British rule.
- The martyrdom of the revolutionaries created a moral pressure that strengthened the mass movements of the 1930s and 1940s.

The revolutionary and socialist streams enriched the Indian national movement by adding ideological depth, a commitment to social justice, and an uncompromising spirit of resistance. Their contribution remains an essential part of the story of India’s freedom struggle.
