# Ecosystems: Structure and Functions

## Definition of Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a functional unit of nature where living organisms (biotic components) interact among themselves and with the non-living (abiotic) components of the environment, resulting in a flow of energy and cycling of materials.

The term was coined by A.G. Tansley in 1935.

## Components of an Ecosystem

### Abiotic Components

- Climatic factors: temperature, rainfall, light, wind, humidity
- Edaphic factors: soil texture, pH, nutrients, organic matter
- Topographic factors: altitude, slope, aspect
- Inorganic substances: carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, water, oxygen
- Organic substances: proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, humic substances

### Biotic Components

1. **Producers (Autotrophs)** – Green plants, algae, phytoplankton, chemosynthetic bacteria. Convert solar energy into chemical energy through photosynthesis.
2. **Consumers (Heterotrophs)**
   - Primary consumers (herbivores)
   - Secondary consumers (carnivores that eat herbivores)
   - Tertiary consumers (top carnivores)
   - Omnivores
3. **Decomposers (Saprotrophs)** – Bacteria, fungi, actinomycetes. Break down dead organic matter and release nutrients back into the soil and atmosphere.

## Structure of Ecosystem

### Trophic Levels

The sequence of eating and being eaten is represented by trophic levels:

- Trophic Level 1: Producers
- Trophic Level 2: Primary consumers
- Trophic Level 3: Secondary consumers
- Trophic Level 4: Tertiary consumers

### Food Chain

A linear sequence of organisms through which nutrients and energy pass.

Types:
- Grazing food chain (starts with producers)
- Detritus food chain (starts with dead organic matter)

### Food Web

Interconnected food chains forming a complex network. Provides stability; if one link is affected, alternatives exist.

### Ecological Pyramids

Graphical representation of the relationship between organisms at successive trophic levels.

1. **Pyramid of Numbers** – Number of individuals. Can be upright or inverted (e.g., one tree supporting many insects).
2. **Pyramid of Biomass** – Total dry weight. Usually upright in terrestrial ecosystems; can be inverted in aquatic (phytoplankton have less biomass but high productivity).
3. **Pyramid of Energy** – Always upright because energy is lost as heat at each transfer (Second Law of Thermodynamics). Only about 10% of energy is transferred to the next trophic level (Ten Percent Law – Lindeman).

## Functions of Ecosystem

1. **Energy Flow** – Unidirectional. Solar energy → Producers → Consumers → Decomposers → Heat loss. Governed by laws of thermodynamics.
2. **Nutrient Cycling (Biogeochemical Cycles)** – Cyclic movement of nutrients.
   - Gaseous cycles: Carbon, Nitrogen, Oxygen (reservoir in atmosphere)
   - Sedimentary cycles: Phosphorus, Sulphur (reservoir in earth’s crust)
3. **Primary Production** – Rate of conversion of solar energy into organic matter by producers. Gross Primary Productivity (GPP) minus respiration = Net Primary Productivity (NPP).
4. **Secondary Production** – Generation of biomass by consumers.
5. **Decomposition** – Breakdown of complex organic matter into simpler inorganic substances. Involves fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification and mineralisation.

## Types of Ecosystems

### Terrestrial

- Forest ecosystems (tropical rainforest, temperate, boreal/taiga, deciduous)
- Grassland ecosystems (tropical savanna, temperate prairie)
- Desert ecosystems (hot and cold)

### Aquatic

- Freshwater: Lotic (flowing – rivers, streams) and Lentic (standing – lakes, ponds)
- Marine: Oceans, seas, estuaries, coral reefs, mangroves

### Artificial / Man-made

- Agricultural ecosystems (cropland)
- Urban ecosystems
- Aquarium, dam reservoirs

## Ecological Succession

The gradual and predictable change in species composition of a given area over time.

- Primary succession: Starts on bare rock, newly formed land (e.g., after volcanic eruption or glacial retreat). Pioneer species are lichens and mosses.
- Secondary succession: Starts on previously occupied land that has been disturbed (e.g., abandoned farmland, forest fire). Faster because soil is already present.

Climax community: Relatively stable final community that is in equilibrium with the environment.

## Productivity of Ecosystems

- Most productive: Tropical rainforests, coral reefs, estuaries, swamps and marshes, alluvial plains.
- Least productive: Deserts, open ocean (despite large area), tundra.

## Ecosystem Services

Benefits that humans derive from ecosystems (Millennium Ecosystem Assessment classification):

1. Provisioning services – food, water, timber, fibre, genetic resources
2. Regulating services – climate regulation, flood control, water purification, pollination, disease control
3. Cultural services – recreation, spiritual, aesthetic, educational
4. Supporting services – nutrient cycling, soil formation, primary production (necessary for all other services)

## Key Concepts for UPSC

- Ten Percent Law of energy transfer
- Pyramid of Energy is always upright
- Difference between GPP and NPP
- Role of decomposers in nutrient cycling
- Primary vs Secondary succession
- Ecosystem services framework
- Hotspots of biodiversity are areas with high endemism and high threat

## Indian Examples

- Sundarbans mangrove ecosystem – unique biodiversity, cyclone protection, livelihood support
- Western Ghats – biodiversity hotspot, water regulation for peninsular rivers
- Himalayan ecosystems – source of major rivers, climate regulation
- Coral reefs of Andaman, Lakshadweep and Gulf of Mannar
- Chilika Lake – brackish water ecosystem, bird sanctuary, livelihood for fisherfolk

Understanding ecosystem structure and function is fundamental to environmental conservation, climate change mitigation, disaster risk reduction and sustainable development policies.
