# Multilateralism and India’s Role in International Organisations

## Meaning of Multilateralism

Multilateralism refers to the practice of coordinating national policies among three or more states through formal or informal institutions and rules. It is based on principles of inclusiveness, generalised principles of conduct, and diffuse reciprocity (benefits are expected over time rather than in every transaction).

It stands in contrast to unilateralism (acting alone) and bilateralism (relations between two states).

## Importance of Multilateralism

- Provides a platform for collective action on global challenges (climate change, pandemics, terrorism, nuclear proliferation, trade).
- Gives smaller and developing countries a voice and reduces the dominance of major powers.
- Creates predictable rules that reduce transaction costs and uncertainty in international relations.
- Helps in burden-sharing for global public goods.
- Legitimises actions through broad consent.

## Challenges to Multilateralism

In recent years, multilateralism has faced stresses from:

- Rise of economic nationalism and protectionism
- Great power rivalry and geopolitical competition
- Perceived inequities in the structure of institutions (e.g., UNSC permanent membership)
- Slow decision-making and lowest-common-denominator outcomes
- Withdrawal or reduced engagement by major powers from certain regimes
- Proliferation of plurilateral and minilateral groupings that sometimes bypass universal institutions

## United Nations System

The United Nations (founded 1945) remains the central multilateral organisation.

### Key Organs

- General Assembly – Universal membership; deliberative body
- Security Council – Primary responsibility for international peace and security; 5 permanent members with veto (P5) and 10 non-permanent members
- Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)
- International Court of Justice (ICJ)
- Secretariat headed by the Secretary-General

### India’s Role and Positions

- Founding member of the UN
- Strong advocate of UN reform, particularly expansion of the Security Council in both permanent and non-permanent categories to reflect contemporary realities
- Member of the G4 (with Brazil, Germany, Japan) seeking permanent membership
- Active contributor to UN Peacekeeping operations – one of the largest troop contributors historically
- Participates in almost all major UN specialised agencies and programmes
- Supports the principle of sovereign equality and non-interference while engaging constructively on global issues

## Bretton Woods Institutions

### International Monetary Fund (IMF)

- Provides financial assistance and policy advice to countries facing balance-of-payments problems
- Quota-based system; voting power linked to quotas
- India has called for greater voice and representation for emerging market and developing countries

### World Bank Group

- International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD), International Development Association (IDA), IFC, MIGA, ICSID
- Focus on development finance, poverty reduction and knowledge services
- India is both a borrower and a contributor; has graduated from IDA but remains engaged

## World Trade Organization (WTO)

- Established 1995, successor to GATT
- Rules-based multilateral trading system
- India has been a leading voice of developing countries on issues such as agricultural subsidies, special and differential treatment, food security (public stockholding), and resistance to new issues that may constrain policy space
- Participates actively in negotiations and dispute settlement

## Other Important Multilateral and Plurilateral Platforms

### G20

- Premier forum for international economic cooperation
- India hosted the G20 Summit in 2023 under the theme “Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam – One Earth, One Family, One Future”
- Brought development concerns, Global South priorities, and issues such as digital public infrastructure, climate finance and reformed multilateralism to the centre stage

### BRICS

- Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa (expanded later)
- Platform for cooperation among major emerging economies
- New Development Bank (NDB) headquartered in Shanghai; Contingent Reserve Arrangement
- Focus on reforming global governance, development finance and intra-BRICS cooperation

### Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)

- Security and economic cooperation focused on Eurasia
- India became a full member in 2017
- Useful for engagement on counter-terrorism, connectivity and regional stability

### Quad (Quadrilateral Security Dialogue)

- India, United States, Japan, Australia
- Informal strategic forum focused on a free, open and inclusive Indo-Pacific
- Cooperation in maritime security, critical and emerging technologies, climate, health, infrastructure

### Other Engagements

- Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA)
- Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation (BIMSTEC)
- ASEAN-led mechanisms (East Asia Summit, ARF, ADMM-Plus)
- International Solar Alliance (ISA) – Indian initiative
- Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI) – Indian initiative

## India’s Approach to Multilateralism

India’s approach can be characterised as:

- Support for a rules-based international order
- Emphasis on reformed multilateralism that is more representative and equitable
- Balancing engagement in universal institutions with participation in flexible plurilateral and minilateral groupings
- Advocacy for the interests of the Global South while pursuing its own strategic and economic interests
- Contribution of ideas and public goods (digital public infrastructure, vaccine diplomacy during COVID-19, disaster relief)
- Strategic autonomy – engagement without entanglement in rigid alliances

## Key Points for Memorisation

- Multilateralism vs unilateralism vs bilateralism
- UNSC reform and G4
- India’s contribution to UN Peacekeeping
- G20 Presidency 2023 theme and outcomes
- BRICS and New Development Bank
- ISA and CDRI as Indian-led initiatives
- Principle of Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam in foreign policy discourse
