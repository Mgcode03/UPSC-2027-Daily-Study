# Urbanisation and Related Issues in India

## Concept of Urbanisation

Urbanisation is the process by which an increasing proportion of a population lives in urban areas. It involves the growth of cities and towns both in terms of population and spatial expansion, and the associated social, economic and cultural transformations.

In India, an urban area is defined by the Census as a place with:
- A minimum population of 5,000
- At least 75% of the male working population engaged in non-agricultural pursuits
- A density of population of at least 400 persons per sq km

In addition, all statutory towns (notified under law) are treated as urban.

## Trends of Urbanisation in India

- 1901: about 11% urban
- 1951: 17.3%
- 1991: 25.7%
- 2011: 31.2% (377 million people)
- Projections suggest India will be around 40% urban by 2030 and may cross 50% in the coming decades.

Urban growth in India is characterised by:
- Rapid growth of large metropolitan cities and million-plus cities
- Emergence of census towns (not statutory but meeting demographic criteria)
- Uneven regional distribution – higher urbanisation in southern and western states, lower in Bihar, Odisha, Assam etc.
- Growth of urban agglomerations and peri-urban areas

## Drivers of Urbanisation

1. **Natural increase** in urban population
2. **Rural-to-urban migration** – push factors (poverty, unemployment, lack of facilities in rural areas) and pull factors (jobs, education, healthcare, lifestyle)
3. **Reclassification** of rural areas as urban when they meet the criteria
4. **Economic transformation** – shift from agriculture to industry and services

## Positive Aspects of Urbanisation

- Concentration of economic activities leading to higher productivity and innovation
- Better access to education, healthcare, and specialised services
- Greater social mobility and exposure to diverse ideas
- Economies of scale in infrastructure provision
- Contribution to national income (cities generate a disproportionate share of GDP)

## Challenges and Problems of Urbanisation

### 1. Housing and Slums

A significant portion of the urban population lives in informal settlements and slums characterised by overcrowding, lack of secure tenure, inadequate water and sanitation, and poor housing quality. The problem is acute in large metros.

### 2. Infrastructure Deficit

- Water supply – intermittent and inadequate in many cities
- Sanitation and sewerage – large gaps; open defecation still exists in some areas
- Solid waste management – collection, segregation and scientific disposal remain weak
- Urban transport – congestion, inadequate public transport, rising private vehicle ownership
- Power supply and drainage – frequent issues during monsoon

### 3. Environmental Degradation

- Air pollution (vehicular, industrial, construction, stubble burning impact)
- Water pollution of rivers and lakes
- Loss of green cover and water bodies due to encroachment
- Urban heat island effect
- Noise pollution

### 4. Social Issues

- Inequality and spatial segregation (gated communities vs informal settlements)
- Crime and safety concerns, especially for women
- Stress on health and education facilities
- Breakdown of traditional social support systems
- Issues of migrant workers – vulnerability, lack of social security, exclusion from city services

### 5. Governance Challenges

- Multiplicity of agencies and lack of coordination
- Weak financial position of Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)
- Limited capacity and professional expertise
- Political interference and short-termism
- Inadequate citizen participation

## Government Initiatives and Policies

- **74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992** – Strengthened Urban Local Bodies; provided for devolution of functions, funds and functionaries; District and Metropolitan Planning Committees.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Mission (JNNURM)** – Earlier flagship for infrastructure and governance reforms.
- **Smart Cities Mission** – Area-based development and pan-city solutions using technology.
- **Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation (AMRUT)** – Water supply, sewerage, green spaces, urban transport.
- **Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (Urban)** – Housing for all.
- **Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban)** – Sanitation and solid waste management.
- **National Urban Transport Policy** – Emphasis on public transport and non-motorised transport.
- **HRIDAY** – Heritage city development.
- **National Heritage City Development and Augmentation Yojana** and other city-specific programmes.

## Concepts Related to Urbanisation

- **Urban Sprawl** – Unplanned and uncontrolled expansion of urban areas into peripheral rural land.
- **Counter-urbanisation** – Movement of people from large cities to smaller towns or rural areas (limited in India so far).
- **Rurbanisation** – Development of rural areas with urban amenities while preserving rural character.
- **Census Towns vs Statutory Towns**
- **Million-plus cities and Metropolitan areas**
- **Satellite towns and New towns**

## Way Forward

Sustainable urbanisation requires:

1. Strengthening the financial and functional autonomy of Urban Local Bodies.
2. Integrated urban planning that includes peri-urban areas and considers regional linkages.
3. Focus on affordable housing and in-situ slum redevelopment with community participation.
4. Massive investment in public transport, walking and cycling infrastructure.
5. Circular economy approaches to waste and water.
6. Climate-resilient urban design and green-blue infrastructure.
7. Inclusive policies that address the needs of migrants, informal workers, women, children and persons with disabilities.
8. Use of technology for better service delivery and citizen engagement, while bridging the digital divide.
9. Capacity building of municipal cadres and professionalisation of urban management.

Urbanisation is inevitable and can be a powerful engine of development if managed well. The challenge is to make Indian cities liveable, inclusive, sustainable and resilient.
