# Constitutional Bodies: Election Commission, UPSC and CAG

## Introduction

The Indian Constitution establishes several independent bodies that are not part of the executive, legislature or judiciary in the conventional sense, yet perform vital constitutional functions. These are known as Constitutional Bodies because they derive their authority and existence directly from the Constitution. Among the most important are the Election Commission of India, the Union Public Service Commission, and the Comptroller and Auditor General of India. Their independence is protected through special provisions regarding appointment, tenure, removal and conditions of service.

These bodies act as institutional checks and balances. They ensure free and fair elections, merit-based recruitment to public services, and financial accountability of the government.

## Election Commission of India (Articles 324–329)

### Composition and Appointment

Article 324 vests the superintendence, direction and control of the preparation of electoral rolls and the conduct of all elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President in the Election Commission.

Originally a single-member body, it became a multi-member body in 1989 (temporarily) and permanently from 1993. It currently consists of the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) and two Election Commissioners (ECs).

- The President appoints the CEC and ECs.
- They hold office for a term of six years or until the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier.
- The conditions of service of the CEC are similar to those of a Supreme Court judge.
- The CEC can be removed only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge (impeachment by Parliament).
- Other Election Commissioners can be removed by the President on the recommendation of the CEC.

### Powers and Functions

- Preparation, maintenance and revision of electoral rolls.
- Notification of election schedules.
- Recognition of political parties and allotment of election symbols.
- Scrutiny of nomination papers.
- Conduct of the poll, counting of votes and declaration of results.
- Advisory jurisdiction in matters of post-election disqualification of sitting members of Parliament and State Legislatures (Articles 103 and 192).
- Power to cancel polls in case of booth capturing, violence or other irregularities and order repolls.
- Registration of political parties under Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.

### Independence and Challenges

The Constitution seeks to insulate the Election Commission from executive interference. The security of tenure of the CEC is a key safeguard. However, the appointment process has been a subject of debate. Until recently, the President appointed the CEC and ECs on the advice of the Council of Ministers. The Supreme Court in 2023 directed that appointments be made by a committee consisting of the Prime Minister, the Leader of the Opposition and the Chief Justice of India, pending legislation by Parliament. Subsequently, Parliament enacted a law providing for a selection committee of the Prime Minister, a Union Cabinet Minister and the Leader of the Opposition.

Other issues include the need for a permanent, independent secretariat, financial autonomy, and the power to deregister political parties.

## Union Public Service Commission (Articles 315–323)

### Composition and Appointment

The UPSC is the central recruiting agency for the All India Services and the Central Services. It consists of a Chairman and other members appointed by the President. The strength is determined by the President (usually 9–11 members including the Chairman).

- Members hold office for six years or until the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier.
- The Chairman and members can be removed by the President only on the grounds of misbehaviour after an inquiry by the Supreme Court, or on grounds of insolvency, infirmity of mind or body, or engagement in paid employment outside their duties.
- Conditions of service cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment.

### Functions

- Conduct of examinations for appointment to the services of the Union (Civil Services Examination, Engineering Services, etc.).
- Direct recruitment by selection through interviews.
- Appointment of officers on promotion, deputation and absorption.
- Framing and amendment of recruitment rules.
- Disciplinary matters affecting persons serving under the Government of India.
- Advising the government on any matter referred to it.
- Annual report to the President, which is laid before Parliament along with a memorandum explaining cases where the advice of the Commission was not accepted.

### Limitations

The UPSC is a recommendatory body. The government is not bound to accept its advice, though in practice it does so in the vast majority of cases. Certain posts are excluded from its purview (for example, some temporary posts, posts in the Secretariat of Parliament, etc.).

State Public Service Commissions perform analogous functions at the state level.

## Comptroller and Auditor General of India (Articles 148–151)

### Appointment and Independence

The CAG is the guardian of the public purse and heads the Indian Audit and Accounts Department. He is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal.

- Holds office for six years or until the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier.
- Can be removed only in like manner and on like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court.
- Salary and other conditions of service cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment.
- Not eligible for further office under the Government of India or any state after he has ceased to hold office.
- Administrative expenses of the office are charged upon the Consolidated Fund of India.

### Duties and Powers

The CAG audits:
- All receipts and expenditure of the Government of India and of each state and union territory having a Legislative Assembly.
- All bodies and authorities substantially financed from Union or State revenues.
- Government companies and corporations as prescribed by law.
- Accounts of stores and stock.

He ascertains and certifies the net proceeds of any tax or duty, and his certificate is final.

He submits audit reports relating to the accounts of the Centre to the President, who causes them to be laid before each House of Parliament. Reports relating to states are submitted to the Governor, who causes them to be laid before the state legislature.

The CAG also acts as a guide, friend and philosopher of the Public Accounts Committee of Parliament.

### Role and Significance

The CAG is one of the most important officers under the Constitution. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described the CAG as the most important functionary under the Constitution. Through his reports, the CAG ensures parliamentary control over public expenditure and promotes accountability and transparency in financial administration.

Over the years, the CAG has expanded the scope of audit from mere regularity and propriety audit to performance audit and environmental audit, examining economy, efficiency and effectiveness of government programmes.

## Comparative Perspective and Key Takeaways

All three bodies share common features of constitutional status, security of tenure, and protection against arbitrary removal. Their independence is essential for the healthy functioning of democracy.

- Election Commission safeguards the electoral process.
- UPSC upholds the principle of merit in public recruitment.
- CAG ensures financial accountability.

Challenges remain in further strengthening their autonomy, particularly with regard to appointment processes, financial independence, and the binding nature of their recommendations or findings. A clear understanding of their composition, powers, limitations and constitutional safeguards is indispensable for both Prelims and Mains.
