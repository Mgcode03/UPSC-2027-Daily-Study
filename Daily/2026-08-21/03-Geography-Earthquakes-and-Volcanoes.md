# Earthquakes and Volcanoes: Causes, Types, Distribution and Impact

Earthquakes and volcanoes are the two most dramatic expressions of the Earth’s internal energy. Both are closely related to the movement of tectonic plates and the release of heat and pressure from the Earth’s interior.

## Earthquakes

An earthquake is the sudden shaking of the ground caused by the passage of seismic waves through the Earth’s rocks. These waves are generated when accumulated stress along a fault is released.

### Causes

1. **Tectonic Earthquakes**  
   The majority of earthquakes occur due to the movement of lithospheric plates. Stress builds up along plate boundaries until it exceeds the strength of the rocks, resulting in rupture and the release of energy.

2. **Volcanic Earthquakes**  
   Associated with the movement of magma beneath volcanoes. These are generally of lower magnitude but can serve as precursors to eruptions.

3. **Collapse Earthquakes**  
   Caused by the sudden collapse of underground mines or caverns.

4. **Explosion Earthquakes**  
   Result from underground nuclear tests or large conventional explosions.

### Key Terminology

- **Focus (Hypocentre)**: The point within the Earth where the rupture begins.
- **Epicentre**: The point on the Earth’s surface directly above the focus.
- **Seismic Waves**:  
  - Body waves: P-waves (primary, compressional, fastest) and S-waves (secondary, shear, slower, cannot travel through liquids).  
  - Surface waves: Love waves and Rayleigh waves; these cause the most damage.
- **Magnitude**: Measured by the Richter scale (logarithmic) or the more modern Moment Magnitude Scale (Mw). Each whole-number increase represents approximately 31.6 times more energy release.
- **Intensity**: Measured by the Modified Mercalli Intensity Scale, which describes the observed effects on people, buildings and the environment (I to XII).

### Distribution of Earthquakes

Earthquakes are concentrated along plate boundaries:

- **Circum-Pacific Belt (Ring of Fire)**: Accounts for about 80 percent of the world’s shallow earthquakes and most of the deep ones. Includes the western coasts of North and South America, Japan, the Philippines, New Zealand and the Pacific islands.
- **Mid-Atlantic Ridge and other divergent boundaries**: Mostly shallow, moderate earthquakes.
- **Alpine-Himalayan Belt**: Extends from the Mediterranean through Turkey, Iran, the Himalayas to Southeast Asia. Highly active due to the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates.
- **Intra-plate Earthquakes**: Less frequent but can be destructive (for example, the 1811–12 New Madrid earthquakes in the United States or the 1993 Latur earthquake in India).

### Earthquake Zones of India

The Bureau of Indian Standards classifies India into four seismic zones:

- Zone II: Low intensity (parts of the Deccan plateau).
- Zone III: Moderate (large parts of peninsular India, Indo-Gangetic plain).
- Zone IV: High (parts of the Himalayas, Delhi region, northern Bihar).
- Zone V: Very high (entire northeastern India, parts of Jammu & Kashmir, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Rann of Kutch).

The Himalayan region is particularly vulnerable because of the ongoing northward movement of the Indian plate at approximately 4–5 cm per year.

## Volcanoes

A volcano is an opening in the Earth’s crust through which magma, gases and ash erupt. The accumulated material forms a volcanic mountain or landform.

### Types of Volcanoes Based on Activity

- **Active**: Erupted in recent historical times or shows signs of unrest (for example, Mount Etna, Stromboli, Barren Island in the Andaman Sea).
- **Dormant**: Has not erupted for a long time but is expected to erupt again (for example, Mount Fuji).
- **Extinct**: No expectation of future eruption (for example, many volcanoes in the Deccan Traps region).

### Types Based on Shape and Eruption Style

1. **Shield Volcanoes**  
   Broad, gently sloping, formed by low-viscosity basaltic lava. Example: Mauna Loa and Kilauea in Hawaii.

2. **Composite or Stratovolcanoes**  
   Steep-sided, built of alternating layers of lava and pyroclastic material. Highly explosive. Examples: Mount Fuji, Mount Vesuvius, Mount St. Helens, Mount Pinatubo.

3. **Cinder Cones**  
   Small, steep, formed mainly of pyroclastic fragments. Example: Paricutin in Mexico.

4. **Calderas**  
   Large depressions formed when the summit collapses after a massive eruption. Example: Crater Lake in Oregon, the Yellowstone caldera.

### Volcanic Materials

- Lava (molten rock that reaches the surface).
- Pyroclastic materials: ash, cinders, bombs, blocks.
- Gases: water vapour, carbon dioxide, sulphur dioxide, hydrogen sulphide.

### Distribution of Volcanoes

Most volcanoes occur along plate boundaries:

- **Convergent Boundaries**: Subduction zones produce explosive stratovolcanoes (Pacific Ring of Fire).
- **Divergent Boundaries**: Mid-ocean ridges produce basaltic eruptions (Iceland is a rare example of a mid-ocean ridge rising above sea level).
- **Hotspots**: Intra-plate volcanism caused by mantle plumes (Hawaii, Yellowstone, Reunion Island).

India has limited active volcanism. Barren Island in the Andaman Sea is the only active volcano in Indian territory. Narcondam is classified as dormant.

### Effects of Volcanic Activity

**Positive**  
- Fertile volcanic soils (for example, in Java, Italy, the Deccan).
- Geothermal energy.
- Creation of new land (Hawaii, Iceland).
- Mineral deposits.

**Negative**  
- Loss of life and property from lava flows, pyroclastic flows, ash falls and lahars (volcanic mudflows).
- Climatic effects: large eruptions inject sulphur dioxide into the stratosphere, forming aerosols that reflect sunlight and cause temporary global cooling (for example, Mount Pinatubo 1991, Tambora 1815 — the “year without a summer”).
- Disruption of air travel by ash clouds.

## Relationship Between Earthquakes and Volcanoes

Both phenomena are concentrated along plate boundaries. Earthquakes often precede or accompany volcanic eruptions because the movement of magma generates seismic activity. Monitoring of earthquake swarms is an important tool in volcano forecasting.

## Key Points for Retention

- P-waves travel through solids and liquids; S-waves only through solids.
- The Circum-Pacific Belt accounts for the majority of the world’s earthquakes and volcanoes.
- India’s highest seismic risk is in Zone V (Northeast and parts of the Himalayas).
- Barren Island is India’s only active volcano.
- Shield volcanoes are associated with low-viscosity basaltic lava; composite volcanoes with more viscous, explosive eruptions.
