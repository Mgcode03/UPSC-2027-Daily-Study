# Basic Economic Concepts for UPSC

Economy questions in Prelims test conceptual clarity and current application. Mains requires analytical understanding of growth, development, policy and challenges.

## What is Economics?

Economics studies how societies allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants. Microeconomics deals with individual units (consumers, firms). Macroeconomics deals with aggregates (national income, employment, inflation, growth).

## National Income Aggregates

Gross Domestic Product (GDP): Market value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic territory of a country in a year. Includes production by residents and non-residents within the territory.

Gross National Product (GNP): GDP + Net factor income from abroad (income earned by residents abroad minus income earned by non-residents domestically).

Net Domestic Product (NDP): GDP – Depreciation (consumption of fixed capital).

Net National Product (NNP): GNP – Depreciation. NNP at factor cost is National Income.

Market price vs Factor cost: Market price includes indirect taxes and excludes subsidies. Factor cost is the opposite. National Income is NNP at factor cost.

Real vs Nominal: Nominal is at current prices. Real is at constant prices (adjusted for inflation). Real GDP growth is the key indicator of economic growth.

GDP can be measured by production (value added), income (sum of factor incomes) and expenditure (C + I + G + (X–M)) methods. In principle they should give the same result.

Limitations of GDP: Does not account for distribution, non-market activities, environmental degradation, quality of life, informal sector fully, or happiness.

## Growth and Development

Economic growth: Increase in real GDP or per capita GDP. Quantitative.

Economic development: Broader – includes growth plus structural change, improvement in living standards, reduction in poverty and inequality, better health and education, environmental sustainability. Qualitative and multi-dimensional.

Human Development Index (HDI): UNDP measure based on life expectancy, education (mean and expected years of schooling), and GNI per capita. India has improved but ranks in medium human development category.

Inclusive growth: Growth that includes all sections, reduces inequality and poverty, and provides opportunities. Emphasis in Indian planning and recent policy.

## Sectors of the Economy

Primary: Agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining. Direct use of natural resources.

Secondary: Manufacturing, construction, electricity, gas, water. Processing of primary products.

Tertiary: Services – trade, transport, communication, banking, insurance, real estate, public administration, professional services. Dominant in India’s GDP share currently.

Structural transformation: Shift from primary to secondary and then tertiary as economy develops. In India, services have grown rapidly while agriculture still employs a large share of workforce (structural lag).

## Money and Banking

Money: Medium of exchange, store of value, unit of account, standard of deferred payment. From commodity money to fiat money and now digital forms.

Measures of money supply in India (RBI): M0 (reserve money – currency in circulation + bankers’ deposits with RBI + other deposits), M1 (narrow money – currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI), M2, M3 (broad money – M1 + time deposits), M4.

Central Bank (RBI): Issues currency, banker to government, banker to banks, controller of credit, manager of foreign exchange, supervisor of financial system. Monetary policy aims at price stability while supporting growth (flexible inflation targeting – 4% CPI with ±2% band).

Monetary policy instruments: Repo rate, reverse repo, bank rate, cash reserve ratio (CRR), statutory liquidity ratio (SLR), open market operations, marginal standing facility, qualitative measures.

Commercial banks: Accept deposits, give loans, create credit. Public sector, private sector, foreign, regional rural banks, small finance banks, payments banks (limited functions).

## Inflation

Sustained rise in general price level. Measured by Consumer Price Index (CPI – combined, rural, urban; used for targeting), Wholesale Price Index (WPI), GDP deflator.

Types: Demand-pull, cost-push, structural, imported. Creeping, walking, running, hyperinflation.

Effects: Redistributes income (hurts fixed income groups, savers; benefits debtors), creates uncertainty, can lead to wage-price spiral, affects competitiveness.

Control: Monetary tightening, fiscal measures, supply-side interventions, administrative measures.

Deflation and disinflation are different. Stagflation is inflation with stagnation.

## Fiscal Concepts

Budget: Annual financial statement of estimated receipts and expenditure. Article 112.

Receipts: Revenue (tax and non-tax) and Capital (recoveries, borrowings, other).

Expenditure: Revenue and Capital; Plan and Non-Plan (classification changed); developmental and non-developmental.

Deficit measures:

- Budget deficit: Total expenditure – total receipts (not commonly used now).
- Revenue deficit: Revenue expenditure – revenue receipts. Indicates borrowing for consumption.
- Effective revenue deficit: Revenue deficit – grants for creation of capital assets.
- Fiscal deficit: Total expenditure – (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts). Measures the total borrowing requirement. Key indicator of fiscal health.
- Primary deficit: Fiscal deficit – interest payments. Shows the deficit excluding past debt burden.

Fiscal policy: Use of government expenditure and taxation to influence economy. FRBM Act aims at fiscal discipline.

Taxation: Progressive, proportional, regressive. Direct (income tax, corporate tax) and indirect (GST, customs, excise). GST is a major reform – destination-based, multi-stage, comprehensive.

## Poverty and Unemployment

Poverty: Absolute (minimum calorie or consumption basket) and relative. Tendulkar, Rangarajan committees for estimation. Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) by NITI Aayog and UNDP/OHPI – health, education, living standards.

Unemployment: Types – frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal, disguised (especially in agriculture), underemployment. Measured by usual status, current weekly status, current daily status (NSSO/PLFS).

Labour force participation rate, worker population ratio, unemployment rate are key indicators. Female labour force participation in India has been a concern.

## Important Concepts for Prelims and Mains

- Difference between growth and development.
- GDP vs GNP vs NNP; real vs nominal.
- Fiscal deficit as the key borrowing indicator.
- Inflation targeting framework of RBI.
- Structural change in Indian economy – services-led growth with agricultural employment share remaining high.
- Inclusive growth and sustainable development goals.

Mastering these basics allows understanding of Budget documents, Economic Survey, monetary policy statements, and current economic debates.
