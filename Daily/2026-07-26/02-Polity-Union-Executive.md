# Union Executive – President, Vice-President, Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

The Union Executive consists of the President, the Vice-President, the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers, and the Attorney-General of India.

## The President of India

The President is the head of the Indian State and the first citizen of India. He is the nominal executive; the real executive is the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.

### Election of the President

The President is elected by an electoral college consisting of:

- Elected members of both Houses of Parliament
- Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States
- Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry

Nominated members of Parliament and State Legislatures do not participate. Members of Legislative Councils (in states that have them) also do not participate.

The election is held in accordance with the system of proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote. Voting is by secret ballot.

Value of vote of an MLA = (Population of the State as per 1971 Census) / (Number of elected MLAs × 1000)

Value of vote of an MP = Total value of votes of all MLAs of all States / Total number of elected MPs

This ensures parity between the Union and the States as a whole, and uniformity among the States inter se.

### Qualifications

- Citizen of India
- 35 years of age
- Qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha
- Must not hold any office of profit under the Government of India or any State Government or any local or other authority

The President, Vice-President, Governor and Ministers are not considered to hold office of profit for this purpose.

### Conditions of Office

- Must not be a member of Parliament or State Legislature (if he is, he is deemed to have vacated the seat on assuming office)
- Must not hold any other office of profit
- Entitled to official residence (Rashtrapati Bhavan), emoluments, allowances and privileges as determined by Parliament
- Emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during his term

### Term and Impeachment

Term is five years. Eligible for re-election any number of times. Can resign by writing to the Vice-President. Can be removed by impeachment for violation of the Constitution.

Impeachment process (Article 61):

- Charge can be preferred by either House of Parliament
- Resolution must be signed by at least one-fourth of the total members of that House
- 14 days’ notice to the President
- Resolution must be passed by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the total membership of that House
- The other House investigates or causes investigation
- If the other House also passes a resolution by two-thirds of total membership sustaining the charge, the President is removed from the date of the resolution

No President has so far been impeached.

### Powers of the President

Executive powers: All executive actions of the Government of India are taken in his name. He appoints the Prime Minister and on his advice the other Ministers, the Attorney-General, the Comptroller and Auditor-General, the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners, the Chairman and members of the Union Public Service Commission, Governors of States, and other key functionaries. He can seek information from the Prime Minister on administration and legislation.

Legislative powers: He is an integral part of Parliament. He can summon, prorogue and dissolve Lok Sabha. He addresses both Houses at the commencement of the first session after each general election and the first session of each year. He can send messages to either House. He nominates 12 members to Rajya Sabha (persons having special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service) and can nominate two members to Lok Sabha from the Anglo-Indian community (this power was discontinued by the 104th Amendment). Bills passed by Parliament require his assent. He can give assent, withhold assent, or return a bill (if it is not a Money Bill) for reconsideration. If Parliament passes it again, he must give assent. He can promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session (Article 123). Ordinances have the same force as an Act but must be laid before Parliament and cease to operate six weeks after reassembly unless approved earlier.

Financial powers: Money Bills can be introduced only on his recommendation. He causes the Annual Financial Statement (Budget) to be laid before Parliament. No demand for grant can be made except on his recommendation. He can make advances from the Contingency Fund. He constitutes a Finance Commission every five years.

Judicial powers: He appoints the Chief Justice and judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts. He can grant pardons, reprieves, respites and remissions of punishment or suspend, remit or commute the sentence of any person convicted of any offence (Article 72). This power is exercised on the advice of the Council of Ministers. The scope includes cases where punishment is by court martial, cases under Union law, and death sentences.

Diplomatic powers: International treaties and agreements are negotiated and concluded in his name. He represents India in international forums. He sends and receives ambassadors.

Military powers: He is the Supreme Commander of the defence forces. He appoints the chiefs of the Army, Navy and Air Force. He can declare war or conclude peace, subject to parliamentary approval or control.

Emergency powers: National Emergency (Article 352), President’s Rule (Article 356), and Financial Emergency (Article 360). These are exercised on the written advice of the Cabinet.

### Discretionary Powers

In most cases the President acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers (Article 74). However, there are situations of discretion:

- Appointment of Prime Minister when no party has a clear majority or when the incumbent dies in office and there is no clear successor
- Dismissal of the Council of Ministers when it loses confidence of the Lok Sabha but refuses to resign
- Dissolution of Lok Sabha when the Council of Ministers has lost majority and no alternative government is possible
- Seeking information from the Prime Minister under Article 78

## The Vice-President

The Vice-President is the second highest office. He is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

### Election

Elected by members of both Houses of Parliament assembled at a joint meeting, by proportional representation by means of single transferable vote, by secret ballot. State legislatures do not participate.

Qualifications: Citizen of India, 35 years of age, qualified for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha, and not holding any office of profit.

Term is five years. Can resign to the President. Can be removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all its then members and agreed to by the Lok Sabha. No ground needs to be stated for removal.

When the President’s office falls vacant due to death, resignation, removal or otherwise, the Vice-President acts as President until a new President is elected (maximum six months). He also discharges functions when the President is unable to do so due to absence, illness or any other cause.

While acting as President or discharging his functions, the Vice-President does not perform the duties of the Chairman of Rajya Sabha. Those are performed by the Deputy Chairman.

## Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

The real executive authority is vested in the Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head.

### Appointment of the Prime Minister

The President appoints the Prime Minister. By convention, the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha is appointed. In case of a hung Parliament, the President exercises discretion and may invite the leader of the largest party or a person who can command majority support.

Other Ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister. The Prime Minister allocates portfolios.

### Council of Ministers

Three categories: Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State (independent charge or attached to Cabinet Ministers), and Deputy Ministers.

The total number of Ministers, including the Prime Minister, shall not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha (91st Amendment).

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Article 75). Individual responsibility also exists; a Minister holds office during the pleasure of the President (practically the Prime Minister).

Cabinet is the smaller body of senior Ministers who take major policy decisions. It is the core of the Council of Ministers.

### Powers and Functions of the Prime Minister

- Head of the Council of Ministers and leader of the party in power
- Principal channel of communication between the President and the Council of Ministers
- Advises the President on appointment of Ministers, allocation of portfolios, and other key appointments
- Presides over Cabinet meetings and influences decisions
- Can recommend dissolution of Lok Sabha
- Leader of the Lok Sabha (if he is a member; otherwise the leader is nominated)
- Chief spokesperson of the government
- Plays a significant role in shaping foreign policy and national security

The Prime Minister is the keystone of the Cabinet arch. The position is one of first among equals, but in practice the PM exercises considerable authority.

## Attorney-General of India

The highest law officer. Appointed by the President. Must be qualified to be appointed a judge of the Supreme Court. Holds office during the pleasure of the President.

Duties: To advise the Government of India on legal matters, perform other legal duties assigned, and discharge functions conferred by the Constitution or any other law. He has the right of audience in all courts in India and can take part in parliamentary proceedings without the right to vote.

He is not a full-time counsel and can engage in private practice subject to restrictions.

## Key Points for Retention

- President is nominal executive; real executive is Council of Ministers.
- Electoral college for President excludes nominated members and Legislative Council members.
- Ordinance-making power under Article 123 is co-extensive with legislative power of Parliament but temporary.
- Collective responsibility of Council of Ministers to Lok Sabha is the cornerstone of parliamentary democracy.
- 15% limit on size of Council of Ministers.
- Vice-President is Chairman of Rajya Sabha and acts as President when required.

Understanding the distinction between nominal and real executive, the process of election, and the system of responsibility is crucial for both objective and descriptive questions.
