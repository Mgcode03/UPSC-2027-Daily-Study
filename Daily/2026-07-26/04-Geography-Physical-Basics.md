# Physical Geography Foundations – Earth, Landforms and Climate Basics

Physical geography is essential for Prelims (map-based and conceptual questions) and Mains GS Paper 1.

## The Earth

Shape: Geoid – approximately spherical but flattened at poles and bulging at equator (oblate spheroid).

Structure: Crust (continental thicker and less dense – sial; oceanic thinner and denser – sima), Mantle (upper and lower; asthenosphere is partially molten upper mantle allowing plate movement), Core (outer liquid, inner solid; mainly iron and nickel).

Earth’s interior knowledge comes from seismic waves – P waves (primary, longitudinal, pass through solids and liquids), S waves (secondary, transverse, pass only through solids), and surface waves.

## Rocks and Minerals

Igneous rocks: Formed from cooling of magma/lava. Intrusive (plutonic – granite) and extrusive (volcanic – basalt). Primary rocks.

Sedimentary rocks: Formed from deposition and lithification of sediments. Sandstone, limestone, shale. Often contain fossils. Cover large parts of Earth’s surface.

Metamorphic rocks: Formed from existing rocks under heat and pressure. Marble (from limestone), slate (from shale), gneiss, schist, quartzite.

Rock cycle: Continuous transformation among the three types.

## Plate Tectonics

Lithosphere is divided into plates that move over the asthenosphere. Driving force is convection currents in the mantle, slab pull and ridge push.

Types of plate boundaries:

- Divergent (constructive): Plates move apart. Mid-oceanic ridges, rift valleys (East African Rift). New crust formed.
- Convergent (destructive): Plates collide. Oceanic-continental (trench, volcanic arc – Andes), oceanic-oceanic (island arc – Japan, Philippines), continental-continental (fold mountains – Himalayas). Crust destroyed or deformed.
- Transform (conservative): Plates slide past each other. San Andreas Fault. Crust neither created nor destroyed. Earthquakes common.

Evidence for plate tectonics: Continental drift (Wegener – jigsaw fit, fossil match, geological match, palaeoclimatic evidence), sea-floor spreading (Hess – magnetic striping, age of ocean floor younger at ridges), distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes along plate boundaries.

## Landforms

Endogenic processes: Internal forces – diastrophism (folding, faulting) and volcanism. Build landforms.

Exogenic processes: External – weathering, erosion, deposition by rivers, wind, glaciers, waves. Modify landforms.

### Mountains

Fold mountains: Himalayas, Alps, Andes, Rockies – young, folded sedimentary rocks.

Block mountains: Formed by faulting – Vosges, Black Forest, Vindhyas (in part).

Volcanic mountains: Formed by accumulation of volcanic material – Mt. Kilimanjaro, Mt. Fujiyama, Mt. Vesuvius.

Residual mountains: Remnants after erosion – Aravallis, Nilgiris.

### Plateaus

Intermontane (Tibet), Piedmont (Patagonian), Continental (Deccan, African). Useful for minerals, agriculture in some cases, and as water divides.

### Plains

Structural, erosional and depositional. Great Northern Plain of India is depositional (alluvial). Important for agriculture and dense population.

## Geomorphic Agents

Rivers: Youth (V-shaped valleys, waterfalls, rapids), Maturity (meanders, flood plains), Old age (ox-bow lakes, deltas, levees). Erosion, transportation, deposition.

Glaciers: U-shaped valleys, cirques, aretes, horns, moraines, eskers, drumlins, fjords. Important in high mountains and polar regions.

Wind (Aeolian): In arid regions – deflation hollows, yardangs, sand dunes (barchan, seif), loess deposits.

Waves and currents: Coastal landforms – cliffs, caves, stacks, beaches, spits, bars, lagoons, coral reefs.

## Atmosphere and Climate Basics

Composition: Nitrogen 78%, Oxygen 21%, Argon, Carbon dioxide (increasing), water vapour (variable), ozone (stratosphere – protects from UV).

Structure: Troposphere (weather phenomena, temperature decreases with height – normal lapse rate 6.5°C/km), Stratosphere (ozone layer, temperature increases), Mesosphere (temperature decreases, meteors burn), Thermosphere (temperature increases, ionosphere – radio waves), Exosphere.

Heating of atmosphere: Insolation (incoming solar radiation). Factors affecting insolation – angle of sun’s rays, duration of day, transparency of atmosphere, distance from sun (seasons due to tilt and revolution).

Heat budget: Balance between incoming and outgoing radiation. Earth maintains average temperature through this balance. Greenhouse effect is natural and necessary; enhanced greenhouse effect due to human activity causes global warming.

Pressure and winds: Pressure decreases with altitude. Horizontal distribution creates pressure belts – Equatorial low, Subtropical high, Subpolar low, Polar high. Shifting with seasons (ITCZ movement).

Planetary winds: Trade winds, Westerlies, Polar easterlies. Local winds: Land and sea breeze, mountain and valley breeze, loo, chinook, mistral, fohn, harmattan etc.

Monsoon: Seasonal reversal of winds. Indian monsoon is a complex system involving differential heating of land and sea, shift of ITCZ, presence of Himalayas, Tibetan plateau heating, El Nino-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), Indian Ocean Dipole, jet streams (tropical easterly jet, subtropical westerly jet).

## Indian Monsoon – Key Points

Onset: Normal date 1 June at Kerala. Advances northwards. Withdrawal begins from northwest in September.

Features: Seasonal, concentrated rainfall in few months, orographic influence (Western Ghats, Himalayas), breaks in monsoon, spatial and temporal variability.

Factors: Differential heating, Coriolis force, jet streams, Tibetan high, Mascarene high, Somali jet, ENSO (El Nino associated with weak monsoon often), IOD.

Importance: Agriculture, water resources, economy, culture. Variability leads to floods and droughts.

## Cyclones

Tropical cyclones: Form over warm oceans (above 27°C), Coriolis force, low pressure, pre-existing disturbance. Energy from latent heat of condensation. Eye, eye wall, spiral bands. Indian Ocean – Bay of Bengal more prone than Arabian Sea. Naming by WMO panels.

Temperate cyclones: Frontal, mid-latitude, associated with western disturbances in India (winter rainfall in northwest, snowfall in mountains).

## Key Memorisation Points

- Plate boundaries and associated landforms and hazards.
- Difference between weather and climate.
- Factors controlling temperature and pressure distribution.
- Mechanism of Indian monsoon and factors affecting its variability.
- Major geomorphic agents and characteristic landforms.

These concepts form the base for understanding Indian physical geography, climate, natural hazards and resource distribution.
