# Special Provisions for States and Union Territories

## Constitutional Asymmetry in Indian Federalism

The Indian Constitution establishes a federal system with a strong Union. At the same time, it recognises that certain states and regions have unique historical, cultural, social or security circumstances that require special treatment. These special provisions create an element of asymmetry within the broader federal framework. The relevant articles are mainly found in Part XXI of the Constitution under “Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions.”

## Special Provisions for Certain States

### Article 370 and the Former State of Jammu and Kashmir

Article 370 (as it originally stood) conferred a special status on the State of Jammu and Kashmir. The State had its own Constitution and a dual citizenship arrangement. The legislative power of Parliament over the State was limited. In 2019, the special status was effectively ended through a presidential order and the reorganisation of the State into two Union Territories — Jammu and Kashmir, and Ladakh. The constitutional and legal processes involved, and the subsequent judicial scrutiny, form an important chapter in the evolution of Indian federalism.

### Article 371 and Related Provisions

Article 371 and its subsequent clauses (371A to 371J) contain special provisions for several states:

- Article 371: Special responsibility of the Governor of Maharashtra and Gujarat for certain areas (Vidarbha, Marathwada, Saurashtra, Kutch) with respect to developmental expenditure and equitable allocation of funds.
- Article 371A: Special provisions for Nagaland relating to religious or social practices, customary law and procedure, administration of civil and criminal justice involving customary law, and ownership and transfer of land and its resources. No Act of Parliament on these matters applies unless the Legislative Assembly of Nagaland so decides.
- Article 371B: Special provision for Assam regarding the constitution and functions of a committee of the Legislative Assembly consisting of members from the tribal areas.
- Article 371C: Similar special provision for Manipur with respect to a committee of members from the Hill Areas.
- Article 371D and 371E: Special provisions for Andhra Pradesh and Telangana concerning equitable opportunities in education and public employment, and the establishment of a Central University in Andhra Pradesh.
- Article 371F: Special provisions for Sikkim after its merger with India, protecting the rights and interests of different sections of the population and restricting the application of certain laws.
- Article 371G: Special provisions for Mizoram similar in spirit to those for Nagaland, protecting religious and social practices, customary law, and land ownership.
- Article 371H: Special responsibility of the Governor of Arunachal Pradesh with respect to law and order.
- Article 371I: Provision for a minimum number of seats in the Goa Legislative Assembly.
- Article 371J: Special provisions for the Hyderabad-Karnataka region (now Kalyana Karnataka) regarding education, public employment and development.

These provisions reflect the recognition that a uniform application of all constitutional arrangements may not be suitable for every region, especially those with distinct tribal, customary or historical identities.

## Union Territories

Union Territories are administered by the Union Government. Their constitutional status is governed mainly by Articles 239 to 241.

### Administration

Every Union Territory is administered by the President acting through an Administrator appointed by him. The Administrator may be designated as Lieutenant Governor in some territories. Parliament has the power to create a legislature and a Council of Ministers for certain Union Territories.

### Union Territories with Legislatures

Currently, Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir have legislative assemblies. The National Capital Territory of Delhi has a special constitutional arrangement under Article 239AA (inserted by the 69th Amendment). It has a Legislative Assembly and a Council of Ministers responsible to the Assembly. However, the Union Government retains control over public order, police and land. The relationship between the elected government and the Lieutenant Governor has been the subject of several judicial pronouncements clarifying the scope of executive power.

Puducherry also has an elected legislature and Council of Ministers under a parliamentary law. The remaining Union Territories are administered directly by the Administrator without an elected legislative body.

### Significance of Union Territories

Union Territories serve several purposes: administration of small or strategically sensitive areas, management of islands, and provision of a neutral administrative framework for the national capital. The creation and reorganisation of Union Territories is within the power of Parliament.

## Special Provisions and the Federal Balance

The existence of special provisions demonstrates that Indian federalism is not rigidly symmetrical. Asymmetry has been used as a tool of nation-building, allowing the integration of diverse regions while accommodating their distinctiveness. At the same time, special provisions can become sources of political contention, especially when they touch upon resource control, representation or the application of national laws.

The judiciary has generally upheld the constitutional validity of special provisions while insisting that they must operate within the broader framework of fundamental rights and the basic structure of the Constitution.

## Key Points for Retention

- Indian federalism contains deliberate asymmetries through special provisions.
- Article 371 series provides tailored arrangements for several states, especially in the North-East and certain other regions.
- Protection of customary law, land rights and social practices is a common theme in provisions for Nagaland, Mizoram and similar states.
- Union Territories are directly administered by the Union; some have legislatures.
- Article 239AA gives Delhi a unique status with an elected government but reserved subjects for the Union.
- Special provisions balance unity with diversity and have been important for national integration.
- Judicial review ensures that special arrangements remain consistent with the basic features of the Constitution.

Understanding these provisions is essential for a nuanced grasp of Indian federalism beyond the standard division of powers between the Union and the States.
