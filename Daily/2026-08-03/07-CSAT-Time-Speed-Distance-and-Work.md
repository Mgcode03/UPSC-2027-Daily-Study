# CSAT Foundations: Time, Speed, Distance and Work

## Basic Relationships

The fundamental relationship connecting distance, speed and time is:

Distance = Speed × Time

From this, Speed = Distance / Time and Time = Distance / Speed.

Units must be consistent. If distance is in kilometres and time in hours, speed is in km/h. If distance is in metres and time in seconds, speed is in m/s. The conversion factor is:

1 m/s = 18/5 km/h
1 km/h = 5/18 m/s

## Average Speed

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. It is not the arithmetic mean of speeds unless the time intervals are equal.

When a journey is performed at two different speeds for equal distances, the average speed is the harmonic mean:

Average speed = 2ab / (a + b)

where a and b are the two speeds.

When speeds are maintained for equal times, the average speed is the arithmetic mean.

## Relative Speed

When two objects move in the same direction, relative speed = difference of their speeds.
When they move in opposite directions, relative speed = sum of their speeds.

Relative speed is used in problems involving meeting, overtaking, and crossing.

## Problems on Trains

Important relations:

- Time taken by a train to cross a stationary object (pole, person) = Length of train / Speed of train.
- Time to cross a platform or bridge = (Length of train + Length of platform) / Speed of train.
- Time for two trains to cross each other = (Sum of lengths) / Relative speed.

Direction matters for relative speed as noted above.

## Boats and Streams

Let the speed of the boat in still water be b and the speed of the stream be s.

- Downstream speed = b + s
- Upstream speed = b − s

Therefore:

b = (Downstream + Upstream) / 2
s = (Downstream − Upstream) / 2

Time taken to go downstream and upstream for the same distance can be used to find these values.

## Circular Motion and Clocks

In problems involving circular tracks, relative speed determines the time to meet when moving in the same or opposite directions. The length of the track divided by relative speed gives the time for the first meeting.

Clock problems are a special case of circular motion. The minute hand moves 12 times faster than the hour hand. The relative speed is 5.5 degrees per minute. This is used to find times when hands are together, opposite, or at right angles.

## Time and Work

Work is usually treated as a constant quantity. If a person can complete a work in n days, that person’s one-day work is 1/n.

If A can do a work in a days and B in b days, then together they can do (1/a + 1/b) of the work in one day. Time taken together = ab / (a + b).

### Key Variants

- If A is twice as efficient as B, A takes half the time B takes.
- Work and wages are distributed in the ratio of the work done (or efficiency).
- Pipes and cisterns are analogous to work problems. Inlet pipes do positive work; outlet pipes do negative work.

### Efficiency Method

Sometimes it is convenient to assume the total work as the LCM of the individual times. Then efficiency of each person is total work divided by the number of days that person takes. Combined efficiency determines the joint time.

## Negative Work and Opposite Effects

When one agent undoes the work of another (for example, a leak in a tank), the net work rate is the difference of the rates. Care must be taken with signs.

## Useful Approaches for CSAT

- Always check units before calculating.
- Draw a simple diagram for relative motion problems.
- For two-person work problems, the formula ab/(a+b) is quick.
- For equal distance–different speeds, use harmonic mean for average speed.
- In mixed problems (work + time + distance), define variables clearly and write equations step by step.

## Illustrative Conceptual Examples

1. A person covers a distance at 40 km/h and returns at 60 km/h. Average speed for the entire journey is 2×40×60/(40+60) = 48 km/h.

2. Two trains 120 m and 150 m long are running in opposite directions at 40 km/h and 50 km/h. Relative speed = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Time to cross = (270)/25 = 10.8 seconds.

3. A can finish a work in 12 days, B in 18 days. Together one day’s work = 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36. Time together = 36/5 = 7.2 days.

4. A boat takes 4 hours downstream and 6 hours upstream for the same distance. Ratio of downstream to upstream speed = 6:4 = 3:2. Then b/s can be found from the relations above.

## Key Points for Retention

- Distance = Speed × Time; keep units consistent.
- Average speed = total distance / total time; harmonic mean for equal distances.
- Relative speed: sum for opposite, difference for same direction.
- Train crossing time depends on total length and relative speed.
- Boat: downstream = b+s, upstream = b−s.
- Work: one-day work = 1/n; combined rate is sum of individual rates.
- Efficiency method using LCM simplifies many problems.
- Pipes and cisterns follow the same logic as work with positive and negative rates.

Mastery of these elementary relations and the ability to translate word problems into equations are sufficient for the large majority of CSAT questions in this area. Regular practice with timed sets builds both accuracy and speed.
