# Accountability and Transparency in Public Administration

## Concept of Accountability

Accountability is the obligation of an individual or organisation to account for its activities, accept responsibility for them, and disclose the results in a transparent manner. In public administration, accountability means that public officials and institutions are answerable for the exercise of authority and the use of public resources.

Accountability has several dimensions:

- Hierarchical or administrative accountability: Answerability of subordinates to superiors within the organisation.
- Legal accountability: Answerability to courts and quasi-judicial bodies for the legality of actions.
- Political accountability: Answerability of the executive to the legislature and, ultimately, to the electorate.
- Professional accountability: Adherence to professional standards and ethics.
- Social or public accountability: Answerability directly to citizens and civil society.

Effective accountability requires clear assignment of responsibility, availability of information, mechanisms for questioning and explanation, and consequences for performance — both positive and negative.

## Transparency

Transparency refers to the openness of government processes, decisions and information to public scrutiny. It is the opposite of secrecy and opacity. Transparency enables citizens to understand how decisions are made, who makes them, and on what basis. It is a precondition for meaningful accountability.

Transparency does not mean that every piece of information must be disclosed. Legitimate exceptions exist for national security, privacy, commercial confidentiality and the integrity of decision-making processes. The challenge is to define these exceptions narrowly and subject them to independent oversight.

## Relationship between Accountability and Transparency

Transparency is an instrument of accountability. Without information, citizens and oversight bodies cannot hold officials to account. At the same time, accountability mechanisms create incentives for greater transparency. The two concepts reinforce each other. Together they form the foundation of ethical governance and public trust.

## Mechanisms of Accountability in India

### Legislative Accountability

The executive is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. Instruments include questions, discussions, motions, committees (Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings), and the budget process. Legislative accountability is the primary political check on the executive.

### Judicial Accountability

Judicial review ensures that executive and legislative actions conform to the Constitution and the law. Writ jurisdiction, public interest litigation, and the power to punish for contempt strengthen judicial oversight. Judicial accountability itself is maintained through internal mechanisms, the collegium system for appointments, and parliamentary removal procedures for higher judiciary.

### Administrative and Financial Accountability

- Internal hierarchy and reporting systems.
- Audit by the Comptroller and Auditor General.
- Vigilance bodies such as the Central Vigilance Commission.
- Lokpal and Lokayuktas for corruption-related accountability.
- Performance appraisal and departmental inquiries.

### Social Accountability

Social accountability tools empower citizens to engage directly with public services. These include:

- Right to Information.
- Public hearings and social audits (especially in programmes such as MGNREGA).
- Citizen report cards and community scorecards.
- Participatory planning and budgeting at local levels.
- Media and civil society oversight.

## Right to Information as a Pillar of Transparency

The Right to Information Act provides a statutory framework for citizens to access information held by public authorities. It has transformed the culture of secrecy inherited from the colonial period. Key features include proactive disclosure obligations, time-bound response to requests, independent information commissions, and penalties for non-compliance.

RTI has been used extensively to expose corruption, improve service delivery, and empower marginalised groups. Challenges include inadequate proactive disclosure, capacity constraints in public authorities, threats to RTI users, and occasional attempts to dilute the law.

## Ethical Foundations of Accountability and Transparency

Accountability and transparency are not merely technical or legal requirements; they rest on ethical values:

- Stewardship: Public office is a trust, not a personal possession.
- Honesty: Truthful reporting of facts and performance.
- Fairness: Equal treatment and absence of arbitrary exercise of power.
- Responsiveness: Willingness to explain and to correct mistakes.
- Humility: Recognition that power is temporary and derived from the people.

When these values are internalised, formal mechanisms become more effective. When they are absent, even well-designed institutions can be subverted.

## Challenges to Accountability and Transparency

- Complexity of modern administration makes it difficult to assign clear responsibility.
- Fragmentation of authority across multiple agencies and levels of government.
- Information asymmetry between officials and citizens.
- Political interference and the use of accountability mechanisms for partisan purposes.
- Capacity and resource constraints in oversight institutions.
- Cultural tolerance of opacity and the persistence of a secrecy mindset in some quarters.
- Digitalisation creates new opportunities for transparency but also new risks of surveillance and exclusion.

## Strengthening Accountability and Transparency

Measures that strengthen the ethical and institutional framework include:

- Clear performance standards and measurable outcomes.
- Protection for whistle-blowers.
- Independent and adequately resourced oversight bodies.
- Regular training in ethics and public service values.
- Use of technology for open data and real-time monitoring while safeguarding privacy.
- Encouragement of a culture of explanation rather than evasion.
- Active citizenship and a free, responsible media.

## Key Points for Retention

- Accountability means answerability, responsibility and enforceability.
- Dimensions: hierarchical, legal, political, professional, social.
- Transparency is openness that enables accountability.
- Major mechanisms: legislature, judiciary, audit, vigilance, RTI, social audits.
- RTI is a transformative tool but requires continuous defence and improvement.
- Ethical values of stewardship, honesty and responsiveness underpin formal systems.
- Challenges include complexity, asymmetry, capacity and culture.
- Strengthening requires institutional design, technology, training and active citizenship.

Accountability and transparency convert the abstract idea of democratic government into concrete practices that citizens can observe and influence. They are indispensable for ethical public administration and for sustaining public trust.
