# India–ASEAN Relations and the Act East Policy

## Evolution of India’s Engagement with Southeast Asia

India’s contemporary engagement with Southeast Asia is rooted in historical, cultural and civilisational linkages that date back centuries, including the spread of Buddhism, Hindu cultural influences, and maritime trade. In the post-independence period, however, engagement remained limited for several decades due to Cold War alignments, economic priorities and domestic preoccupations.

The end of the Cold War and India’s economic liberalisation in the early 1990s created the conditions for a renewed outreach. The Look East Policy, formally articulated in the early 1990s, marked a strategic shift towards greater economic and political engagement with Southeast Asia and East Asia. In 2014, the policy was upgraded and rebranded as the Act East Policy, signalling a more proactive, action-oriented and multi-dimensional approach that extends beyond ASEAN to include Japan, South Korea, Australia and the broader Indo-Pacific.

## ASEAN as a Central Pillar

The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) comprises ten countries: Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Brunei, Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar and Cambodia. ASEAN is central to India’s Act East Policy for several reasons:
- Geographical proximity and maritime connectivity across the Bay of Bengal and the Andaman Sea
- Economic complementarity and growing trade and investment linkages
- Shared interest in regional stability, freedom of navigation and a rules-based order
- ASEAN’s role as the institutional core of regional architecture in the Indo-Pacific (ASEAN-led forums)

India became a Sectoral Dialogue Partner of ASEAN in 1992, a Full Dialogue Partner in 1996, and a Summit-level Partner in 2002. The relationship was elevated to a Strategic Partnership in 2012 and further strengthened through comprehensive engagement across political, security, economic and socio-cultural pillars.

## Economic Dimensions

Economic cooperation has been a major driver of the relationship. The ASEAN–India Free Trade Agreement in goods came into effect in phases beginning in 2010, followed by agreements on services and investment. Trade has grown substantially, although the balance has often been in favour of ASEAN, and utilisation of the FTA has faced challenges related to non-tariff barriers, rules of origin and awareness among businesses.

India’s economic engagement focuses on:
- Trade in goods and services
- Investment flows in both directions
- Connectivity projects, including the India–Myanmar–Thailand Trilateral Highway and the Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project
- Cooperation in digital economy, start-ups, agriculture, blue economy and sustainable development
- Integration with regional value chains

Physical, digital and people-to-people connectivity are prioritised to reduce the costs of engagement and to link India’s North-Eastern states more closely with Southeast Asia.

## Strategic and Security Cooperation

The strategic dimension of the relationship has gained prominence with the changing geopolitics of the Indo-Pacific. India and ASEAN share concerns about freedom of navigation, respect for international law (including UNCLOS), and the maintenance of peace and stability in the South China Sea and the broader region.

Cooperation mechanisms include:
- ASEAN–India Summit and related ministerial meetings
- ASEAN Regional Forum (ARF)
- East Asia Summit (EAS)
- ASEAN Defence Ministers’ Meeting Plus (ADMM-Plus)
- Expanded maritime cooperation, including coordinated patrols, naval exercises and information sharing
- Cooperation on counter-terrorism, cyber security and transnational crime

India supports ASEAN centrality in the regional architecture and participates actively in ASEAN-led processes. The relationship is not directed against any country but seeks to strengthen a multipolar, inclusive and rules-based regional order.

## Socio-Cultural and People-to-People Ties

Historical and cultural affinities provide a strong foundation. Initiatives include:
- Student exchanges, scholarships and educational cooperation
- Cultural festivals, Buddhist heritage circuits and tourism promotion
- Diaspora linkages, particularly in Singapore, Malaysia and other countries
- Track 1.5 and Track 2 dialogues

These soft power dimensions complement the economic and strategic pillars and help sustain long-term goodwill.

## Act East Policy: Key Features and Objectives

The Act East Policy builds on Look East but is distinguished by:
- Greater emphasis on action and implementation rather than merely diplomatic articulation
- Extension of the geographical scope to the wider Indo-Pacific, including Japan, Korea, Australia and the Pacific Island countries
- Stronger security and defence cooperation component
- Focus on connectivity—physical, digital and people-to-people—especially linking the North-East of India with Southeast Asia
- Alignment with India’s broader Indo-Pacific vision of a free, open and inclusive region

The policy seeks to leverage India’s growing economic and military capabilities to deepen partnerships and to contribute to regional public goods such as maritime security, disaster relief and sustainable development.

## Challenges and Opportunities

Challenges include the need to improve trade balance and FTA utilisation, accelerate connectivity projects, manage domestic capacity constraints, and navigate the complex great-power dynamics in the region. Opportunities lie in the expanding digital and green economy, infrastructure partnerships, defence industry cooperation, and the shared interest in diversified and resilient supply chains.

## Key Points for Retention

- Look East Policy (early 1990s) upgraded to Act East Policy (2014)
- ASEAN: 10 members; India is Strategic Partner; ASEAN centrality supported
- ASEAN–India FTA (goods, services, investment)
- Key connectivity projects: Trilateral Highway, Kaladan
- Participation in ARF, EAS, ADMM-Plus
- Focus on maritime security, freedom of navigation, rules-based order
- Linkage with India’s North-East development and Indo-Pacific vision
- Multi-pillar engagement: political-security, economic, socio-cultural

India–ASEAN relations and the Act East Policy constitute a central element of India’s foreign policy orientation towards its eastern neighbourhood and the wider Indo-Pacific, combining economic opportunity with strategic necessity.
