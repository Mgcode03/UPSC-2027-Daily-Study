# International Relations: India's Neighbourhood First Policy and Regional Engagement

## Strategic Importance of the Neighbourhood

India shares land or maritime boundaries with several countries: Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, and the Maldives. Afghanistan is often included in the broader neighbourhood conception. The stability, prosperity, and orientation of these countries directly affect India's security, economic interests, connectivity, and role in the wider Indo-Pacific and global order.

A stable and cooperative neighbourhood reduces the space for external powers to gain strategic footholds that could be used against Indian interests. Conversely, instability, conflict, or hostile alignments in the neighbourhood impose costs in terms of security expenditure, refugee flows, disrupted trade, and diplomatic energy.

## Evolution of India's Neighbourhood Approach

Post-independence India initially pursued a policy shaped by anti-colonial solidarity, non-alignment, and a desire for friendly relations. The realities of Partition, the 1962 war with China, the 1965 and 1971 wars with Pakistan, and subsequent developments introduced enduring security concerns.

In the 21st century, successive governments have articulated a "Neighbourhood First" approach. The core idea is to prioritise relations with immediate neighbours, offer unilateral concessions where appropriate, accelerate connectivity, and treat the neighbourhood as a continuum rather than a series of bilateral problems.

Key elements typically include:

- Priority attention to neighbours in diplomatic engagement.
- Asymmetric responsibilities — India, as the larger economy and power, is prepared to give more than it receives in the short term.
- Connectivity — physical (roads, rail, ports, energy grids), digital, and people-to-people.
- Regional institutions and sub-regional cooperation (SAARC, BIMSTEC, BBIN, etc.).
- Non-reciprocal gestures on trade and market access where feasible.

## Major Neighbours: Conceptual Overview

### Pakistan

Relations are dominated by the unresolved issues arising from Partition, the status of Jammu and Kashmir, cross-border terrorism, and mutual distrust. Dialogue has been intermittent and often derailed by terrorist attacks or political changes. The challenge is to manage a difficult relationship so that it does not escalate while keeping the door open for eventual normalisation based on the cessation of terrorism and mutual respect for sovereignty.

### China

India-China relations combine cooperation (trade, multilateral forums such as BRICS and SCO) with deep strategic competition and border tensions (including the 2020 Galwan clash and ongoing friction along the LAC). The relationship requires careful management of the border question, economic interdependence, and competition for influence in the wider region and the Indo-Pacific.

### Bangladesh

Relations have improved significantly since 2009–10, with progress on the land boundary agreement, maritime boundary settlement, energy cooperation, connectivity, and security coordination. Bangladesh is a key partner in India's eastern strategy and in sub-regional connectivity. Outstanding issues include water sharing (Teesta), trade balance, and the treatment of minorities.

### Nepal

Historical, cultural, and open-border ties are extensive. Periodic tensions arise over border issues (Kalapani, Susta), perceptions of Indian interference, and the balancing of relations with China. India remains a major economic and transit partner. Managing the relationship requires sensitivity to Nepalese nationalism and consistent delivery on development and connectivity projects.

### Bhutan

India-Bhutan relations are among the closest. India is Bhutan's largest development and security partner. Hydropower cooperation is a cornerstone. The relationship is guided by the 2007 Treaty of Friendship (revising the 1949 treaty). Bhutan's careful diplomacy and India's respect for its sovereignty have sustained a unique partnership.

### Sri Lanka

Ties are shaped by geography, the Tamil question, economic engagement, and competition with China for influence (including the Hambantota port experience). India has provided substantial economic support during Sri Lanka's recent financial crisis. The challenge is to balance support for Sri Lanka's stability and sovereignty with the legitimate concerns of the Tamil population and strategic interests in the Indian Ocean.

### Myanmar

Myanmar is critical for India's Act East Policy and for the security of the Northeast. Connectivity projects (Kaladan, trilateral highway), energy, and counter-insurgency cooperation are important. Political instability and the military coup have complicated engagement. India has to balance principles of democracy with pragmatic security and connectivity interests.

### Maldives

The Maldives sits astride key Indian Ocean sea lanes. India has traditionally been a first responder in crises (e.g., 1988 coup, water crisis, COVID assistance). Recent political shifts have introduced greater competition with China and occasional anti-India rhetoric. Sustained engagement, economic partnership, and respect for Maldivian sovereignty remain the preferred approach.

## Regional Institutions

- **SAARC**: South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation. Vision of regional cooperation has been largely stymied by India-Pakistan tensions. Summit-level activity has been limited.
- **BIMSTEC**: Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation. Includes Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and Thailand. Seen as a more promising vehicle for eastern and Bay of Bengal cooperation.
- **BBIN**: Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal — sub-regional initiative for connectivity and resource management.
- **Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA)** and other maritime forums: Broader Indian Ocean engagement.

## Challenges and Constraints

1. **Asymmetry**: India's size generates both influence and anxiety among smaller neighbours.
2. **Domestic politics in neighbour countries**: Nationalism, identity politics, and regime changes can rapidly alter the climate of relations.
3. **Chinese presence**: Economic and strategic engagement by China offers neighbours alternative partnerships and can reduce India's leverage.
4. **Delivery deficit**: Announced projects sometimes face delays due to bureaucratic, financial, or political obstacles on both sides.
5. **Non-traditional security**: Terrorism, radicalisation, climate change, pandemics, and disaster response require cooperative frameworks that are still evolving.

## Key Principles for Effective Neighbourhood Policy

- Consistency and patience rather than transactional swings.
- Respect for the sovereignty and domestic political processes of neighbours.
- Focus on delivery of tangible benefits — connectivity, energy, health, education, and disaster assistance.
- People-to-people ties and cultural diplomacy as long-term stabilisers.
- Coordination between security, economic, and diplomatic instruments.
- Willingness to accommodate asymmetry in favour of smaller partners where strategic interests allow.

## Key Points for Memorisation

- Neighbourhood First: priority, asymmetry, connectivity, regional institutions.
- SAARC limited by India-Pakistan; BIMSTEC and BBIN as alternative/complementary platforms.
- Major bilateral relationships each have unique historical and strategic features.
- China is both a neighbour and a systemic competitor whose regional engagement affects India's options.
- Success depends on delivery, sensitivity to local politics, and long-term consistency.

## Conceptual Understanding

India's neighbourhood is not a peripheral concern but a core determinant of its security and its ability to act as a regional and global power. A successful Neighbourhood First policy converts geography from a source of vulnerability into a source of strength by building interdependent and resilient relationships. This requires strategic patience, institutional capacity for project delivery, and a diplomatic style that combines firmness on core interests with generosity and respect toward smaller partners.
