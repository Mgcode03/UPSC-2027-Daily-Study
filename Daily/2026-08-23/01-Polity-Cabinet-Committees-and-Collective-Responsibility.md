# Cabinet Committees and Collective Responsibility

## Concept of Collective Responsibility

Collective responsibility is a foundational principle of the parliamentary system of government in India. It means that the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. This principle is explicitly stated in Article 75(3) of the Constitution of India.

Under this doctrine, the entire Council of Ministers stands or falls together. If a no-confidence motion is passed against the Council of Ministers, the entire Ministry must resign. Individual ministers cannot continue in office if the Council as a whole loses the confidence of the House.

### Key Features of Collective Responsibility

- All ministers are jointly responsible for the policies and decisions of the government.
- Ministers must support cabinet decisions in public, even if they privately disagreed during internal discussions.
- A minister who is unable to defend a cabinet decision must resign.
- The Prime Minister is the leader of the Council of Ministers and plays a central role in maintaining collective responsibility.
- Collective responsibility ensures unity of the executive and prevents conflicting statements from different ministers.

### Individual Responsibility

Alongside collective responsibility, there exists individual responsibility. A minister is individually responsible for the working of the department under his or her charge. The President can remove a minister on the advice of the Prime Minister. Individual responsibility allows the government to drop a particular minister without the entire Council having to resign.

### Distinction between Collective and Individual Responsibility

Collective responsibility is to the Lok Sabha as a whole. Individual responsibility is to the President (acting on the advice of the Prime Minister). Collective responsibility is a political principle that maintains the unity of the executive. Individual responsibility is both a political and administrative principle that ensures accountability of each minister for departmental performance.

## Cabinet System and Its Working

The Cabinet is the core of the Council of Ministers. It consists of senior ministers who hold important portfolios. The Cabinet is the highest decision-making body in the executive.

### Functions of the Cabinet

- Policy formulation on major national issues.
- Coordination of the work of different ministries.
- Approval of major administrative appointments and important bills.
- Crisis management and handling of emergency situations.
- Supervision of the implementation of government policies.

The Cabinet meets regularly under the chairmanship of the Prime Minister. Decisions are taken by consensus or majority, and once taken, they bind all members under the principle of collective responsibility.

## Cabinet Committees

To manage the heavy workload and ensure specialised attention to complex issues, the Cabinet works through a system of Cabinet Committees. These committees are extra-constitutional bodies. They are not mentioned in the Constitution but have become an established part of the parliamentary system.

### Nature and Purpose of Cabinet Committees

Cabinet Committees are appointed by the Prime Minister. Their composition and terms of reference can be changed by the Prime Minister as required. They help in reducing the burden on the full Cabinet by examining issues in detail and making recommendations. Some committees have decision-making powers, while others are primarily deliberative.

### Standing Cabinet Committees

There are several standing Cabinet Committees. The important ones include:

**Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs (CCPA)**  
This is one of the most powerful committees. It deals with all major policy matters, including economic and political issues of national importance. It is often described as a super-cabinet because of the range of subjects it handles.

**Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)**  
This committee reviews economic trends, deals with prices, industrial licensing, investment, and other economic matters of national importance. It plays a key role in economic policy coordination.

**Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS)**  
This committee deals with issues relating to defence, internal security, and external security. It takes decisions on defence expenditure, security-related appointments, and major security policy matters.

**Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs**  
This committee looks after the progress of government business in Parliament. It decides the legislative programme and coordinates the work of the government in both Houses.

**Cabinet Committee on Appointments**  
This committee makes recommendations for all major appointments, including those of the chiefs of the armed forces, governors, and other key positions.

**Cabinet Committee on Accommodation**  
This deals with the allotment of government accommodation to ministers and other high functionaries.

**Cabinet Committee on Investment and Growth**  
This focuses on promoting investment, removing bottlenecks, and facilitating ease of doing business.

**Cabinet Committee on Employment and Skill Development**  
This addresses issues related to employment generation and skill development programmes.

### Ad-hoc Committees

In addition to standing committees, the Prime Minister can constitute ad-hoc committees to examine specific issues that require detailed consideration. These committees are temporary and are dissolved once their work is completed.

### Composition and Functioning

Cabinet Committees usually consist of senior Cabinet ministers. The Prime Minister is the chairperson of most important committees. The Cabinet Secretariat provides secretarial assistance to these committees. Decisions of the Cabinet Committees are generally treated as decisions of the Cabinet, unless the matter is specifically referred to the full Cabinet for further consideration.

## Importance of the Cabinet Committee System

The system of Cabinet Committees serves several important purposes:

- It allows specialised and detailed examination of complex policy issues.
- It reduces the burden on the full Cabinet, enabling it to focus on the most critical matters.
- It promotes coordination among ministries dealing with inter-related subjects.
- It facilitates quicker decision-making on routine and specialised matters.
- It strengthens the principle of collective responsibility by ensuring that major decisions are taken after collective deliberation in smaller groups of senior ministers.

## Relationship with Collective Responsibility

Cabinet Committees operate within the framework of collective responsibility. Decisions taken by a Cabinet Committee bind the entire Council of Ministers. A minister who is not a member of a particular committee is still bound by its decisions. This reinforces the unity of the executive and prevents individual ministers from publicly opposing government policy.

## Challenges and Observations

Over time, concerns have been raised about the concentration of decision-making power in a few key committees, particularly the Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs and the Cabinet Committee on Security. There have also been discussions about the need for greater transparency in the functioning of these committees. However, the system remains an essential feature of the working of the Union executive.

## Key Points for Memorisation

- Collective responsibility is provided under Article 75(3).
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
- Cabinet Committees are extra-constitutional bodies.
- The Prime Minister constitutes and reconstitutes Cabinet Committees.
- Major standing committees include CCPA, CCEA, CCS, and the Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs.
- Decisions of Cabinet Committees generally have the same force as Cabinet decisions.
- Collective responsibility ensures that the entire Ministry stands or falls together.

## Conceptual Clarity Notes

Collective responsibility is both a constitutional principle and a political convention. It ensures that the government presents a united face to Parliament and the public. The Cabinet Committee system is a practical mechanism that makes collective decision-making manageable in a large and complex government. Understanding the interplay between the full Cabinet, Cabinet Committees, and the principle of collective responsibility is essential for grasping how executive power is exercised at the Union level in India.
