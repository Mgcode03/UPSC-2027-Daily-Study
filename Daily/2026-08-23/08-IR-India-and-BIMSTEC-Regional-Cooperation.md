# India and BIMSTEC: Regional Cooperation

## Introduction to BIMSTEC

BIMSTEC stands for the Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation. It is a regional organisation that brings together countries from South Asia and Southeast Asia around the Bay of Bengal.

The member countries of BIMSTEC are Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and Thailand. The organisation was established in 1997 (initially as BIST-EC) and later expanded and renamed.

BIMSTEC is unique because it connects South Asia and Southeast Asia and focuses on the Bay of Bengal region, which is of strategic and economic importance to all its members.

## Objectives of BIMSTEC

The main objectives of BIMSTEC include:

- Creating an enabling environment for rapid economic development through identification and implementation of specific cooperation projects.
- Accelerating the economic growth and social progress in the region.
- Promoting active collaboration and mutual assistance in economic, social, technical, and scientific fields.
- Providing assistance to each other in the form of training and research facilities.
- Cooperating more effectively in the areas of trade, investment, industry, technology, human resources development, tourism, agriculture, energy, transport and communication, and other areas of mutual interest.
- Maintaining peace and stability in the region through close collaboration.

## Sectors of Cooperation

BIMSTEC has identified several priority sectors of cooperation. These have evolved over time. Key sectors include:

- Trade and investment
- Transport and communication
- Energy
- Tourism
- Technology
- Fisheries
- Agriculture
- Public health
- Poverty alleviation
- Counter-terrorism and transnational crime
- Environment and disaster management
- Culture
- People-to-people contact
- Climate change

Each sector is led by a member country that acts as the lead country for that sector.

## Institutional Structure

BIMSTEC has a relatively lean institutional structure:

- The Summit is the highest decision-making body and is held at the level of Heads of State or Government.
- The Ministerial Meeting is held at the level of Foreign Ministers.
- The Senior Officials’ Meeting prepares the ground for ministerial and summit meetings.
- The BIMSTEC Secretariat is located in Dhaka, Bangladesh, and provides administrative and technical support.
- Various working groups and expert groups operate at the sectoral level.

## India’s Interests and Role in BIMSTEC

India has a strong interest in the success of BIMSTEC for several reasons:

- **Neighbourhood and regional connectivity**: BIMSTEC includes India’s eastern neighbours and provides a platform for engagement with both South Asian and Southeast Asian countries.
- **Act East Policy**: BIMSTEC is an important vehicle for India’s Act East Policy, which seeks to deepen engagement with Southeast Asia.
- **Bay of Bengal as a strategic space**: The Bay of Bengal is central to India’s maritime interests. Cooperation on maritime security, connectivity, and blue economy is significant.
- **Alternative to SAARC**: With SAARC facing challenges due to regional political differences, BIMSTEC has gained greater importance as a platform for regional cooperation in India’s neighbourhood.
- **Development cooperation**: India has supported various BIMSTEC initiatives and has offered cooperation in capacity building, connectivity, and other areas.

## Connectivity and Economic Cooperation

Connectivity is a major focus of BIMSTEC. Improved physical connectivity (roads, ports, inland waterways, and air links) and digital connectivity can enhance trade and people-to-people contact.

The BIMSTEC Free Trade Area negotiations have been ongoing. Progress on the free trade agreement has been slower than expected, but the commitment to expand economic cooperation remains.

Energy cooperation, including the potential for a BIMSTEC energy grid, is another important area. The region has significant energy resources and complementary energy needs.

## Security Cooperation

BIMSTEC has mechanisms for cooperation on counter-terrorism, transnational crime, and maritime security. Given the shared challenges of terrorism, trafficking, and other transnational threats, security cooperation is an important dimension of the organisation’s work.

## Challenges Facing BIMSTEC

Despite its potential, BIMSTEC faces several challenges:

- Slow progress on key initiatives such as the free trade agreement.
- Limited institutional capacity and resources of the Secretariat.
- Diversity of member countries in terms of size, economic development, and political systems.
- Overlapping memberships with other regional organisations (such as SAARC, ASEAN, and others), which can dilute focus.
- The need for greater political commitment and regular summit-level engagement.
- Infrastructure and connectivity gaps that require substantial investment.

## Recent Developments and Way Forward

BIMSTEC has seen renewed attention in recent years. Summits have been held, and there have been efforts to strengthen the institutional framework and prioritise key projects. India has emphasised the importance of BIMSTEC in its regional outreach.

For BIMSTEC to realise its potential, greater focus on implementable projects, improved connectivity, stronger institutional mechanisms, and sustained political commitment from all members will be necessary. The organisation has the advantage of bringing together countries that share the Bay of Bengal and have complementary interests in trade, connectivity, and security.

## Key Points for Memorisation

- BIMSTEC members: Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Thailand.
- Established in 1997; Secretariat in Dhaka.
- Connects South Asia and Southeast Asia around the Bay of Bengal.
- Important for India’s Act East Policy and neighbourhood engagement.
- Priority sectors include trade, connectivity, energy, counter-terrorism, and others.
- Free Trade Area negotiations are ongoing.
- Challenges include slow progress, institutional capacity, and resource constraints.
- BIMSTEC has gained importance as SAARC has faced difficulties.

## Conceptual Notes

BIMSTEC represents a pragmatic approach to regional cooperation based on shared geography and complementary interests. For India, it is a key platform for implementing the Act East Policy and for deepening engagement with the eastern neighbourhood and Southeast Asia. The success of BIMSTEC depends on the ability of member states to move from declarations to concrete projects, particularly in connectivity, trade, and security. Understanding BIMSTEC is important for analysing India’s regional diplomacy, maritime strategy in the Bay of Bengal, and the evolving architecture of cooperation in the Indo-Pacific region.
