# CSAT: Time and Work – Advanced Concepts

## Basic Framework of Time and Work

In time and work problems, the fundamental relationship is:

Work = Rate × Time

If a person can complete a work in n days, then the person’s one-day work is 1/n of the total work. Conversely, if a person’s one-day work is 1/n, then the person will complete the work in n days.

When two or more persons work together, their combined one-day work is the sum of their individual one-day works (assuming they work at constant rates and do not interfere with each other).

## Combined Work and Efficiency

If A can do a work in a days and B can do the same work in b days, then:

- A’s one-day work = 1/a
- B’s one-day work = 1/b
- (A + B)’s one-day work = 1/a + 1/b = (a + b)/(ab)
- Time taken by A and B together = ab/(a + b) days

This formula can be extended to three or more persons.

### Efficiency Concept

Efficiency is often expressed as a percentage or as a ratio. If A is twice as efficient as B, then A does twice the work that B does in the same time. Equivalently, A takes half the time that B takes to do the same work.

If the efficiency ratio of A to B is m : n, then the time ratio of A to B is n : m (inverse relationship).

## Work and Wages

When persons work together and earn a certain amount, the wages are usually divided in the ratio of the work done by each person. Since work done is proportional to the product of efficiency and time (or simply to the rate if time is the same), wages are divided in the ratio of their efficiencies if they work for the same number of days, or in the ratio of the product of efficiency and days if the number of days differs.

## Pipes and Cisterns

Pipes and cisterns problems are a variation of time and work problems. An inlet pipe fills a tank, and an outlet pipe empties it.

If a pipe can fill a tank in m hours, its one-hour work is +1/m (positive for filling).  
If a pipe can empty a tank in n hours, its one-hour work is –1/n (negative for emptying).

When both operate together, the net one-hour work is 1/m – 1/n.  
The time taken to fill the tank when both are open is 1 / (1/m – 1/n) = mn/(n – m) hours (assuming m < n so that the tank eventually fills).

## Alternative Approach: LCM Method

A useful method for many time and work problems is to assume the total work as the LCM of the individual times. This avoids fractions.

Example: A completes work in 12 days, B in 18 days.  
LCM of 12 and 18 = 36.  
Assume total work = 36 units.  
A’s one-day work = 36/12 = 3 units.  
B’s one-day work = 36/18 = 2 units.  
Together = 5 units per day.  
Time together = 36/5 days.

This method is especially helpful when dealing with multiple workers or when work is left incomplete.

## Work Done in Parts and Remaining Work

Often a problem states that A works for some days, then B joins or A leaves, and so on. In such cases:

1. Calculate the work done in each phase.
2. Find the remaining work.
3. Calculate the time required to complete the remaining work at the new rate.

## Men, Women, and Children

Problems involving men, women, and children often give relative efficiencies. For example:  
“2 men and 3 women can complete a work in 10 days, while 3 men and 2 women can complete it in 8 days.”  
Such problems can be solved by forming equations based on the total work done.

Let one man’s one-day work be m and one woman’s one-day work be w.  
Then:  
2m + 3w = 1/10  
3m + 2w = 1/8  
Solving these equations gives the values of m and w, from which further questions can be answered.

## Negative Work and Damage

Sometimes a problem involves a person or a pipe that does negative work (for example, a person who undoes work or a leak in a tank). Treat such work as negative and proceed with the algebraic sum of rates.

## Variable Efficiency and Changing Rates

In some advanced problems, efficiency may change after a certain period, or different groups may work on different days. Break the problem into intervals of constant rate and sum the work done in each interval.

## Key Formulas Summary

- One person’s one-day work = 1/(number of days)
- Time taken by A and B together = (a × b)/(a + b)
- If A is m times as efficient as B, A takes 1/m of the time B takes.
- Wages are divided in the ratio of work done.
- Net rate when filling and emptying = rate of filling – rate of emptying.
- Total work can be taken as LCM of individual times for ease of calculation.

## Illustrative Reasoning Patterns

1. **Two persons, different times**: Use the formula or LCM method to find combined time.
2. **One leaves, another joins**: Calculate work done before the change, remaining work, and new combined rate.
3. **Pipes with leak**: Net rate = filling rate – emptying rate.
4. **Work and wages**: Ratio of wages = ratio of (efficiency × time).
5. **Men and women**: Form linear equations and solve for individual rates.

## Conceptual Notes for CSAT

Time and work problems test the ability to translate verbal information into quantitative relationships and to manipulate rates and proportions. The key is to work consistently either in terms of fractions of work per unit time or in terms of units of work (using the LCM method). Avoid mixing the two approaches in the same solution. Always check whether the rates are additive (as when people work together without interference) and whether any negative work is involved. Practice with a variety of problem types builds the speed and accuracy needed for the CSAT paper.
