# Mineral and Energy Resources of India

## Importance of Mineral and Energy Resources

Minerals and energy resources form the foundation of industrial development and economic growth. They provide raw materials for manufacturing, fuel for transportation and power generation, and inputs for agriculture (fertilisers). India’s mineral endowment is substantial but unevenly distributed, creating both opportunities and challenges of regional development and inter-state resource sharing.

Minerals are broadly classified into metallic, non-metallic and energy minerals. Metallic minerals include ferrous (iron ore, manganese, chromite) and non-ferrous (bauxite, copper, lead, zinc). Non-metallic minerals include limestone, mica, gypsum, and precious stones. Energy minerals comprise coal, petroleum, natural gas and nuclear minerals (uranium, thorium).

## Distribution of Major Minerals

### Iron Ore

India has large reserves of high-grade haematite and magnetite. Major belts are:

- Odisha-Jharkhand belt: High-grade haematite in Badampahar, Mayurbhanj, Keonjhar, Singhbhum.
- Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur belt: Bailadila (Chhattisgarh) produces very high-grade ore exported through Visakhapatnam.
- Bellary-Chitradurga-Chikmagalur-Tumkur belt in Karnataka: Kudremukh deposits of magnetite.
- Maharashtra-Goa belt: Lower grade ores.

India is among the leading producers of iron ore. Most production is used by the domestic steel industry; a significant quantity is also exported.

### Manganese

Manganese is essential for steel making (as a hardening agent) and for batteries and chemicals. Major producing states are Odisha, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh and Karnataka. Odisha alone accounts for a large share of production.

### Bauxite

Bauxite is the ore of aluminium. Major deposits are in Odisha (Kalahandi, Koraput, Bolangir), Gujarat, Jharkhand, Maharashtra and Chhattisgarh. The Amarkantak plateau and the Panchpatmali deposits in Odisha are particularly important. Aluminium smelters are located near sources of cheap power because the process is energy-intensive.

### Copper

Copper deposits are relatively limited. Major mines are in Singhbhum (Jharkhand), Balaghat (Madhya Pradesh) and Jhunjhunu-Alwar belt (Rajasthan). India remains a net importer of copper.

### Mica

India was once the leading producer of mica, used in electrical and electronic industries. Major belts are in Jharkhand (Kodarma-Gaya-Hazaribagh), Andhra Pradesh and Rajasthan. Production has declined with the rise of synthetic substitutes.

### Limestone

Limestone is crucial for cement and iron and steel industries. Almost every state has limestone deposits, but major producing areas are in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Chhattisgarh and Tamil Nadu.

## Energy Resources

### Coal

Coal is the most important source of commercial energy in India, accounting for a large share of electricity generation. Gondwana coal (bituminous) of the Damodar, Mahanadi, Godavari and Wardha valleys is the main type. Tertiary coal (lignite) occurs in Tamil Nadu (Neyveli), Rajasthan and Gujarat.

Major coalfields:
- Jharkhand: Jharia, Bokaro, Giridih.
- Odisha: Talcher, Ib Valley.
- Chhattisgarh: Korba, Mand-Raigarh.
- West Bengal: Raniganj.
- Madhya Pradesh: Singrauli, Pench-Kanhan.
- Maharashtra: Wardha Valley.

India has large coal reserves but faces challenges of high ash content, environmental impact of mining, and the need to balance domestic production with imports of coking coal for steel plants.

### Petroleum and Natural Gas

Petroleum deposits are found in sedimentary basins. Major producing areas:
- Mumbai High (offshore, Arabian Sea) – the largest producer.
- Gujarat: Ankleshwar, Cambay, Kalol.
- Assam: Digboi, Naharkatiya, Moran.
- Rajasthan: Barmer basin (Mangala, Bhagyam).
- Eastern offshore: Krishna-Godavari and Cauvery basins.

Natural gas is associated with oil fields and also occurs independently (for example, in the KG basin). India imports a large share of its crude oil requirements, making energy security a critical policy concern.

### Nuclear Minerals

Uranium deposits occur in Jharkhand (Jaduguda), Andhra Pradesh, Meghalaya and Rajasthan. Thorium is abundant in the monazite sands of the Kerala and Tamil Nadu coasts. India’s three-stage nuclear power programme is designed to utilise the large thorium reserves after developing uranium-based and plutonium-based reactors.

### Renewable Energy Resources

Although not minerals, renewable sources are increasingly important:
- Hydroelectric potential is concentrated in the Himalayan region and the Western Ghats.
- Solar energy potential is high across most of the country, especially in Rajasthan, Gujarat and the Deccan.
- Wind energy is significant in Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra and Karnataka.
- Biomass and small hydro also contribute.

India has set ambitious targets for renewable capacity addition to reduce dependence on fossil fuels and to meet climate commitments.

## Challenges and Conservation

Several issues affect the mineral and energy sector:

- Uneven distribution leads to inter-regional disparities and transport costs.
- Environmental degradation from open-cast mining, deforestation and pollution of water bodies.
- Displacement of tribal communities in mineral-rich areas of central and eastern India.
- Exhaustible nature of non-renewable resources requires scientific mining and recycling.
- Need for value addition within the country rather than export of raw ores.
- Energy security concerns arising from import dependence on oil and gas.

Policy responses include the National Mineral Policy, emphasis on sustainable mining, auctions of mineral blocks, promotion of renewable energy, and strategic petroleum reserves.

## Key Points

- Major iron ore belts: Odisha-Jharkhand, Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur, Bellary-Chitradurga.
- Bauxite: Odisha is the leading producer.
- Coal: Gondwana coalfields of Damodar, Mahanadi, Godavari valleys.
- Petroleum: Mumbai High is the largest producer.
- Thorium: Monazite sands of Kerala-Tamil Nadu coast.
- India is self-sufficient in coal but import-dependent for oil and coking coal.

Understanding the spatial distribution, economic significance and environmental implications of mineral and energy resources is fundamental to the geography of economic development in India.
