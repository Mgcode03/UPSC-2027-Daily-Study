# Indian Environmental Policies and Institutions

## Evolution of Environmental Concern in India

Environmental awareness in India has deep cultural roots, but modern environmental policy began to take shape after independence and accelerated after the 1970s. The Stockholm Conference of 1972, in which India participated actively, provided an international stimulus. The Bhopal gas tragedy of 1984 highlighted the need for stricter industrial safety and environmental regulation. The Rio Earth Summit of 1992 further shaped India’s approach to sustainable development.

The Constitution of India, through the 42nd Amendment (1976), introduced Article 48A (Directive Principle) directing the State to protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife, and Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty) requiring every citizen to protect and improve the natural environment.

## Key Environmental Legislations

### The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972

This Act provides for the protection of wild animals, birds and plants. It creates a network of protected areas – National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Conservation Reserves and Community Reserves. It regulates hunting, trade in wildlife products and establishes the National Board for Wildlife and State Boards. Schedules of the Act list species according to the degree of protection required.

### The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974

The Act aims to prevent and control water pollution and maintain or restore the wholesomeness of water. It established the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs). These bodies set standards, monitor compliance, and can issue directions including closure of polluting units.

### The Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981

Modelled on the Water Act, it addresses air pollution. CPCB and SPCBs are the implementing agencies. The Act was amended to include noise as an air pollutant.

### The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986

Enacted in the aftermath of Bhopal, this is an umbrella legislation. It empowers the central government to take all necessary measures for protecting and improving environmental quality, setting standards, regulating industrial locations, managing hazardous substances, and conducting environmental impact assessments. Most subsequent rules (Hazardous Waste Rules, EIA Notification, Coastal Regulation Zone Notification, etc.) have been issued under this Act.

### The Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980

This Act restricts the de-reservation of forests or use of forest land for non-forest purposes without prior approval of the central government. It has been instrumental in reducing the rate of diversion of forest land.

### The Biological Diversity Act, 2002

The Act gives effect to the Convention on Biological Diversity. It provides for conservation of biological diversity, sustainable use of its components, and fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge. It establishes the National Biodiversity Authority, State Biodiversity Boards and Biodiversity Management Committees at the local level.

### The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010

The NGT was established for effective and expeditious disposal of cases relating to environmental protection and conservation of forests and other natural resources. It has jurisdiction over civil cases involving substantial questions relating to the environment and has powers to order relief and compensation.

## Institutional Framework

- **Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)**: The nodal ministry at the centre.
- **Central Pollution Control Board and State Pollution Control Boards**: Regulatory and monitoring bodies for air and water pollution.
- **Forest Departments** at the centre and in states: Manage reserved and protected forests.
- **National Biodiversity Authority**: Regulates access to biological resources.
- **National Green Tribunal**: Quasi-judicial body for environmental disputes.
- **Wildlife Crime Control Bureau**: Combats organised wildlife crime.

## Major Policy Initiatives and Programmes

- National Forest Policy, 1988: Emphasises ecological security and participatory forest management (Joint Forest Management).
- National Environment Policy, 2006: Seeks to mainstream environmental concerns into development.
- National Action Plan on Climate Change (2008): Eight national missions covering solar energy, enhanced energy efficiency, sustainable habitat, water, sustaining Himalayan ecosystem, Green India, sustainable agriculture, and strategic knowledge on climate change.
- Namami Gange Programme: Comprehensive initiative for cleaning and rejuvenating the Ganga.
- National Clean Air Programme: Targets reduction of particulate pollution in cities.
- Project Tiger and Project Elephant: Species-specific conservation programmes that also protect habitats.
- Coastal Regulation Zone Notifications: Regulate development along the coast to protect sensitive ecosystems.

## Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

The EIA Notification (under the Environment Protection Act) makes prior environmental clearance mandatory for specified categories of projects. The process involves screening, scoping, public consultation and appraisal. Categories A and B determine whether clearance is at the central or state level. Public hearings are an important component intended to incorporate local concerns.

## Challenges in Implementation

- Capacity constraints in regulatory agencies.
- Coordination problems among multiple institutions.
- Balancing development needs with environmental protection, especially in infrastructure and mining projects.
- Enforcement gaps and delayed justice.
- Inadequate monitoring data and scientific capacity in some areas.
- Pressures of urbanisation, industrialisation and changing consumption patterns.

## Key Points

- Constitutional provisions: Article 48A and 51A(g).
- Umbrella legislation: Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.
- Pollution control: Water Act 1974, Air Act 1981, CPCB/SPCBs.
- Wildlife Protection Act 1972 and network of protected areas.
- Forest (Conservation) Act 1980.
- Biological Diversity Act 2002.
- National Green Tribunal, 2010.
- National Action Plan on Climate Change and its eight missions.

Indian environmental policy has evolved from a focus on pollution control and wildlife protection to a broader concern with sustainable development, climate change and biodiversity. Effective implementation depends on institutional capacity, public participation and the integration of environmental considerations into all sectors of development planning.
