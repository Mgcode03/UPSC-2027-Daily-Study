# All-India Services and Public Service Commissions

## Introduction to Civil Services in India

The civil services form the permanent executive wing of the government in India. They provide continuity, expertise and administrative machinery for implementing policies framed by the political executive. The Constitution of India envisages a strong, independent and efficient civil service as essential for good governance in a democratic polity.

Articles 308 to 323 in Part XIV of the Constitution deal with services under the Union and the States. The framework ensures a balance between national integration and state autonomy while maintaining high standards of recruitment and service conditions.

## Classification of Services

Services under the Union and States are broadly classified as:

1. **All-India Services**: Common to both the Union and the States. Officers of these services serve in both central and state governments during their careers.
2. **Central Services**: Serve exclusively under the Union Government.
3. **State Services**: Serve exclusively under the respective State Governments.

The three All-India Services currently in existence are:
- Indian Administrative Service (IAS)
- Indian Police Service (IPS)
- Indian Forest Service (IFoS)

These were created under Article 312. The Constitution originally provided for IAS and IPS. The Indian Forest Service was constituted later in 1966 under the All-India Services Act, 1951.

### Constitutional Basis of All-India Services

Article 312 empowers Parliament to create new All-India Services if the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting, declaring that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest to do so. Once created, the conditions of service, recruitment and disciplinary matters of All-India Services are regulated by the All-India Services Act, 1951 and rules framed under it.

Key features of All-India Services:
- Officers are recruited by the Union Public Service Commission through a common competitive examination.
- They are allotted to different state cadres but remain under the ultimate control of the central government.
- Cadre controlling authority for IAS is the Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT), for IPS the Ministry of Home Affairs, and for IFoS the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.
- Officers can be deputed to the Centre under the Central Staffing Scheme.
- They enjoy protection against arbitrary dismissal or reduction in rank; such action requires consultation with the UPSC in many cases.

### Significance of All-India Services

All-India Services promote national unity and integrity by creating a common administrative culture across states. Officers develop an all-India perspective while gaining deep knowledge of a particular state through cadre allocation. They act as a bridge between the Centre and the States, facilitating cooperative federalism. In times of emergency or when state administration breaks down, these officers provide a reliable instrument of central intervention.

## Public Service Commissions

Articles 315 to 323 provide for Public Service Commissions for the Union and for the States.

### Union Public Service Commission (UPSC)

The UPSC is an independent constitutional body. It consists of a Chairman and other members appointed by the President. The strength is determined by the President. At least half the members must have held office for at least ten years under the Government of India or a State Government.

Tenure of members is six years or until the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier. Members can be removed by the President only on the grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity after an inquiry by the Supreme Court. They can also be removed for insolvency, paid employment outside duties, or infirmity of mind or body.

Functions of UPSC (Article 320):
- Conduct examinations for appointments to All-India Services and Central Services.
- Advise the President on matters relating to methods of recruitment, principles of appointment, promotions and transfers.
- Be consulted on disciplinary matters affecting persons serving under the Union.
- Advise on claims for reimbursement of legal expenses incurred by a civil servant in defending legal proceedings.
- Advise on claims for award of pension for injuries sustained while on duty.
- Any other matter referred by the President.

The UPSC is not consulted on certain matters such as reservations, temporary appointments for less than one year, and appointments from among persons already in service in certain cases. The advice of UPSC is not binding, but the government is required to explain to Parliament if it rejects the advice.

### State Public Service Commissions (SPSC)

Each state has a Public Service Commission. The Governor appoints the Chairman and members. Tenure is six years or until 62 years of age. Removal is by the President on the same grounds as for UPSC members, after inquiry by the Supreme Court.

Functions are analogous to those of UPSC but limited to state services. A Joint Public Service Commission can be created for two or more states by an Act of Parliament if those states request it. The Chairman and members of a Joint Commission are appointed by the President.

### Independence of Public Service Commissions

Several provisions safeguard independence:
- Security of tenure.
- Conditions of service determined by the President/Governor and cannot be varied to the disadvantage of a member after appointment.
- Expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund of India/State.
- Chairman and members are ineligible for further employment under the government after ceasing to hold office (with limited exceptions for UPSC Chairman becoming eligible for SPSC Chairmanship under certain conditions).

These safeguards ensure that recruitment remains free from political interference and maintains high standards of integrity and competence.

## Recruitment and Training

Recruitment to All-India Services and Group A Central Services is through the Civil Services Examination conducted by UPSC. The examination has three stages: Preliminary, Main and Personality Test (Interview). The Preliminary examination consists of two papers of objective type (General Studies and CSAT). The Main examination is descriptive and tests depth of knowledge, analytical ability and expression. The Personality Test assesses suitability for public service.

After selection, officers undergo foundation training at the Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA), Mussoorie, followed by professional training in their respective academies (for example, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel National Police Academy for IPS).

## Challenges and Reforms

The civil services face several challenges:
- Political interference in postings and transfers.
- Over-centralisation and hierarchical culture that discourages initiative.
- Inadequate domain expertise in specialised areas.
- Issues of accountability and performance appraisal.
- Cadre management problems, including large inter-cadre disparities.

Reform efforts have included the introduction of the Central Staffing Scheme, mid-career training programmes, the Performance Related Incentive Scheme (though limited in application), and recommendations of various committees such as the Second Administrative Reforms Commission. Lateral entry of domain experts at senior levels has been introduced on a limited scale to bring specialised knowledge into the higher bureaucracy.

The concept of a permanent civil service remains central to Indian administration. Its strength lies in institutional memory, political neutrality (in principle) and the ability to provide continuity across changing political regimes. Strengthening its professionalism, domain competence and accountability while preserving its independence continues to be a key governance priority.

## Key Points for Memorisation

- Article 312: Creation of All-India Services.
- Existing All-India Services: IAS, IPS, IFoS.
- UPSC composition and tenure: Chairman + members, 6 years or 65 years.
- Removal of UPSC members: Only by President after Supreme Court inquiry on grounds of misbehaviour/incapacity.
- Article 315: Public Service Commissions for Union and States.
- Functions of UPSC under Article 320 are mainly advisory.
- Expenses of UPSC charged on Consolidated Fund of India.
- Joint Public Service Commission possible for two or more states.

The All-India Services and the independent Public Service Commissions together form the backbone of India's permanent executive. Understanding their constitutional position, functions and the balance they maintain between national unity and federal diversity is essential for grasping the working of Indian administration.
