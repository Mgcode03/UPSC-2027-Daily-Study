# Natural Resources of India: Minerals, Energy, and Water Resources

## Introduction

Natural resources form the material basis of economic development. India is endowed with a wide variety of mineral, energy, and water resources, though their distribution is uneven and their exploitation raises issues of sustainability, environmental impact, and inter-generational equity. Understanding the location, characteristics, and significance of these resources is essential for both Prelims and Mains.

## Mineral Resources

India has significant reserves of metallic and non-metallic minerals. The distribution is closely linked to the geological structure – the peninsular plateau is particularly rich in minerals.

### Iron Ore
India is among the leading producers of iron ore. The major types are haematite and magnetite. Haematite is of higher grade and is found mainly in the Dharwar and Cuddapah rock systems.

Major belts:
- Odisha-Jharkhand belt: High-grade haematite in the Badampahar mines (Mayurbhanj, Keonjhar, Sundargarh in Odisha) and in Singhbhum (Jharkhand). Odisha is the largest producer.
- Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur belt: Bailadila range in Chhattisgarh (high-grade ore exported through Visakhapatnam), and Chandrapur in Maharashtra.
- Bellary-Chitradurga-Chikmagalur-Tumkur belt in Karnataka: Kudremukh mines (magnetite, largely exported as slurry).
- Maharashtra-Goa belt: Lower grade ores.

Iron ore is the foundation of the iron and steel industry. India exports a significant quantity, though domestic demand has grown with the expansion of steel capacity.

### Manganese
Manganese is essential for steel making (as a hardening agent) and for batteries and chemicals. India has substantial reserves. Major producing states are Odisha, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Karnataka. The ores are associated with the Dharwar system.

### Bauxite
Bauxite is the ore of aluminium. It forms under tropical weathering conditions. Major deposits are in Odisha (Kalahandi, Koraput – Panchpatmali deposits are among the largest), Gujarat, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Tamil Nadu, and Madhya Pradesh. Odisha is the leading producer. Aluminium is a strategic metal used in transport, construction, packaging, and electrical industries.

### Copper
Copper reserves are relatively limited. Major deposits are in the Singhbhum belt (Jharkhand), Balaghat (Madhya Pradesh), and Jhunjhunu and Alwar (Rajasthan). India imports a large part of its copper requirements. Copper is vital for electrical industries.

### Mica
India was once the leading producer of mica. It is used in electrical and electronic industries because of its insulating properties. Major producing areas are in Jharkhand (Kodarma-Gaya-Hazaribagh belt), Andhra Pradesh, and Rajasthan. Production has declined with the rise of synthetic substitutes.

### Other Minerals
- Lead and zinc: Rajasthan (Zawar mines), Andhra Pradesh.
- Gold: Kolar and Hutti in Karnataka (Kolar is largely exhausted); some production from other areas.
- Silver: Associated with lead-zinc ores.
- Limestone: Widely distributed; essential for cement industry. Major producing states include Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, and Chhattisgarh.
- Dolomite, gypsum, and other non-metallic minerals support various industries.

### Mineral Policy and Issues
Mining has environmental and social costs – deforestation, displacement of tribal communities, pollution of water and air, and land degradation. The National Mineral Policy and subsequent reforms aim to attract investment, ensure sustainable mining, and share benefits with local communities (through District Mineral Foundations). Illegal mining remains a concern in some areas.

## Energy Resources

Energy is critical for industrialisation, transport, and household needs. India’s energy mix is dominated by coal, with growing shares of oil, gas, hydro, nuclear, and renewables.

### Coal
Coal is the most important source of commercial energy in India. It accounts for a large share of electricity generation. Coal is of Gondwana and Tertiary origin. Gondwana coal (about 98% of reserves) is found in river valleys of the peninsular plateau.

Major coalfields:
- Jharkhand: Jharia, Bokaro, Giridih, Karanpura – high-grade metallurgical coal (Jharia is the most important for coking coal).
- Odisha: Talcher, IB valley.
- Chhattisgarh: Korba, Hasdeo-Arand.
- West Bengal: Raniganj (oldest coalfield).
- Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra: Central Indian coalfields (Singrauli, Pench-Kanhan, etc.).
- Andhra Pradesh and Telangana: Godavari valley (Singareni).

Tertiary coal is found in Assam, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh, and Jammu & Kashmir (lignite and sub-bituminous). Lignite is important in Tamil Nadu (Neyveli), Gujarat, and Rajasthan.

India has large coal reserves but faces challenges of quality (high ash content), transportation, environmental impact of mining, and the need to reduce dependence on coal for climate reasons. Coal India Limited is the dominant public sector company.

### Petroleum and Natural Gas
Oil and gas are crucial for transport and industry. Domestic production meets only a fraction of demand; India is a major importer.

Major oil-producing areas:
- Mumbai High (offshore, western coast) – the largest producing field.
- Gujarat (Ankleshwar, Cambay, etc.).
- Assam (Digboi – one of the oldest, Naharkatiya, Moran).
- Rajasthan (Barmer – recent discoveries).
- Krishna-Godavari and Cauvery basins (offshore and onshore).

Natural gas is found in association with oil and in independent fields (KG basin, Assam, Gujarat, Rajasthan). The KG-D6 field was a major discovery. Gas is used for power, fertiliser, and as CNG in transport. The pipeline network (HBJ – Hazira-Bijaipur-Jagdishpur and others) has expanded.

Strategic petroleum reserves have been created for energy security.

### Nuclear Energy
India has modest reserves of uranium (Jharkhand, Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Meghalaya) and large reserves of thorium in monazite sands along the coasts of Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Odisha, and Andhra Pradesh. The three-stage nuclear programme aims to utilise thorium. Nuclear power plants are located at Tarapur, Rawatbhata, Kalpakkam, Narora, Kakrapar, Kaiga, Kudankulam, and others. Nuclear energy provides a low-carbon baseload option, though safety, waste disposal, and public acceptance remain concerns.

### Hydropower
Hydropower is a renewable and clean source. Potential is high in the Himalayan region (Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Arunachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir) and in the Western Ghats and north-eastern states. Major projects include Bhakra-Nangal, Tehri, Hirakud, and numerous projects in the north-east and south. Issues include displacement, environmental impact, seismic risks in the Himalayas, and interstate water disputes. Run-of-the-river projects are preferred in sensitive areas.

### Renewable Energy
Solar and wind energy have grown rapidly. India has high solar insolation and significant wind potential (Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Rajasthan). The National Solar Mission and other policies have driven capacity addition. Biomass, small hydro, and emerging sources like green hydrogen are part of the energy transition. The goal of high non-fossil capacity by 2030 reflects the shift towards cleaner energy.

## Water Resources

Water is a critical but stressed resource. India has about 4% of the world’s freshwater resources and 18% of the world’s population.

### Surface Water
The major river systems are the Himalayan rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra – perennial, snow-fed) and the Peninsular rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, Narmada, Tapi, Mahanadi – largely rain-fed). Utilisable surface water is limited by topography, interstate disputes, and seasonal variation. Multipurpose projects provide irrigation, power, flood control, and water supply.

### Groundwater
Groundwater is the main source of irrigation and drinking water in many regions. Over-extraction has led to declining water tables, especially in Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, and parts of hard-rock peninsular India. Fluoride, arsenic, and salinity contamination affect water quality in several areas.

### Irrigation
Irrigation is essential because of the seasonal and uncertain nature of rainfall. Sources include canals, tanks, wells, and tube-wells. The Green Revolution was supported by expansion of irrigation. Challenges include inefficient use (flood irrigation), waterlogging and salinity in canal-irrigated areas, and inequitable access.

### Water Management Issues
- Interstate river water disputes (Cauvery, Krishna, Ravi-Beas, etc.).
- Interlinking of rivers – proposed to transfer water from surplus to deficit basins; involves ecological, social, and federal complexities.
- Urban water supply and sanitation deficits.
- Climate change impacts on monsoon and glacier-fed rivers.
- Need for watershed management, rainwater harvesting, micro-irrigation (drip and sprinkler), and demand-side management.

The National Water Policy emphasises integrated water resources management, prioritisation of drinking water, and participatory approaches.

## Conservation and Sustainable Use

Natural resources are finite in the economic sense. Sustainable development requires efficient use, recycling, substitution where possible, and minimisation of environmental damage. Community participation, technological innovation, and sound policy are essential. The concept of the resource curse warns that resource wealth does not automatically translate into development without good governance.

## Key Points for Retention

- Iron ore: Odisha largest producer; Bailadila, Kudremukh important.
- Bauxite: Odisha (Panchpatmali); aluminium industry.
- Coal: Gondwana coalfields – Jharia (coking), Raniganj, Talcher, Korba, Singareni.
- Oil: Mumbai High, Assam, Gujarat, Barmer, KG basin.
- Thorium: monazite sands on southern and eastern coasts.
- Water stress: high population pressure on limited utilisable water; groundwater over-exploitation.
- Energy transition: growing share of solar and wind alongside continued reliance on coal in the near term.

A clear understanding of the spatial distribution, economic significance, and sustainability challenges of natural resources is fundamental to Indian geography and economic geography.
