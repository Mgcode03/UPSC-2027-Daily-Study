# The United Nations System and India’s Engagement

## Origins and Purpose of the United Nations

The United Nations (UN) was established in 1945 in the aftermath of the Second World War with the primary objectives of maintaining international peace and security, developing friendly relations among nations, achieving international cooperation in solving economic, social, cultural, and humanitarian problems, and promoting respect for human rights. The UN Charter is its foundational document.

The UN was designed to improve upon the League of Nations by including the major powers (with special responsibilities and rights) and by creating a more robust institutional structure.

## Principal Organs

### General Assembly

The main deliberative body in which all member states have equal representation (one vote each). It discusses and makes recommendations on a wide range of issues. Special sessions and emergency special sessions can be convened. The Assembly elects non-permanent members of the Security Council, members of the Economic and Social Council, and, jointly with the Security Council, judges of the International Court of Justice. It also approves the budget.

### Security Council

Primary responsibility for the maintenance of international peace and security. It has 15 members: five permanent members (China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom, and the United States) with the veto power, and ten non-permanent members elected for two-year terms. The Council can impose sanctions, authorise the use of force, and establish peacekeeping operations. Its decisions are binding on member states.

### Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)

Coordinates the economic and social work of the UN and its specialised agencies. It serves as a forum for discussion of international economic and social issues.

### International Court of Justice (ICJ)

The principal judicial organ. It settles legal disputes between states and gives advisory opinions. Its jurisdiction depends on the consent of states.

### Secretariat

Headed by the Secretary-General, it carries out the day-to-day work of the Organisation. The Secretary-General is a key diplomatic actor and can bring matters to the attention of the Security Council.

### Trusteeship Council

Established to supervise the administration of trust territories; its work is largely complete.

## Specialised Agencies and Related Bodies

The UN system includes specialised agencies (WHO, UNESCO, ILO, FAO, IMF, World Bank Group, etc.), funds and programmes (UNDP, UNICEF, UNHCR, UNEP, etc.), and related organisations. These bodies address functional areas of international cooperation.

## India’s Engagement with the United Nations

India was a founding member of the United Nations and has been a consistent participant in its activities.

### Peacekeeping

India has been one of the largest contributors of troops to UN peacekeeping operations over the decades. Indian peacekeepers have served in numerous missions across continents. India has also suffered casualties in the line of duty and has advocated for greater involvement of troop-contributing countries in decision-making related to peacekeeping.

### Decolonisation and Development

In the early decades, India played an active role in the decolonisation process and in advocating for the interests of developing countries. It has been a strong voice for development, poverty eradication, and a more equitable international economic order.

### Disarmament and Non-Proliferation

India has participated in disarmament discussions while maintaining its independent nuclear policy. It has supported complete nuclear disarmament within a time-bound framework and has been critical of discriminatory non-proliferation regimes that do not address existing nuclear arsenals of the recognised nuclear-weapon states.

### Security Council Reform

India is a leading advocate of the reform of the Security Council to make it more representative of contemporary geopolitical realities. It seeks permanent membership, arguing on the basis of its population, democratic credentials, economic weight, contributions to the UN, and role in international affairs. India works with the G4 (Brazil, Germany, Japan, and India) and other reform-oriented groupings.

### Climate Change, Sustainable Development, and Global Public Goods

India engages actively in negotiations on climate change (UNFCCC), sustainable development goals, health, and other global issues, emphasising equity, common but differentiated responsibilities, and the right to development.

### Human Rights

India participates in the Human Rights Council and related mechanisms while emphasising the importance of dialogue, national ownership, and the avoidance of politicisation of human rights issues.

## Challenges Facing the United Nations

- The veto power and the difficulty of reforming the Security Council.
- Resource constraints and dependence on a few major contributors.
- Questions about effectiveness in preventing or resolving major conflicts.
- Coordination among the vast array of agencies and programmes.
- Balancing the principles of sovereignty and non-interference with the responsibility to protect and address global challenges that transcend borders.

## Conceptual Significance for India

The United Nations remains the central, if imperfect, forum for multilateral diplomacy. India’s engagement reflects its commitment to multilateralism, its aspiration for a greater role in global governance, and its desire to shape norms and outcomes on issues of peace, development, and equity. Understanding the structure of the UN system and the main themes of India’s participation is essential for analysing contemporary international relations and India’s foreign policy.
