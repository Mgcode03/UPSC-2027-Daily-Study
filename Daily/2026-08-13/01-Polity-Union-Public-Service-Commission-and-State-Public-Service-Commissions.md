# Union Public Service Commission and State Public Service Commissions

## Constitutional Status and Independence

The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) and the State Public Service Commissions (SPSCs) are constitutional bodies designed to ensure merit-based recruitment to the civil services and to protect the services from political interference in matters of appointment, promotion, and disciplinary action.

### Relevant Articles

- Article 315: Public Service Commissions for the Union and for the States.
- Article 316: Appointment and term of office of members.
- Article 317: Removal and suspension of a member.
- Article 318: Power to make regulations regarding conditions of service.
- Article 319: Prohibition on holding office after ceasing to be a member (with limited exceptions).
- Article 320: Functions of Public Service Commissions.
- Article 321: Power to extend functions.
- Article 322: Expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund.
- Article 323: Reports of Public Service Commissions.

The Constitution deliberately places these bodies outside the direct control of the executive in key respects so that recruitment and certain service matters remain insulated from partisan influence.

## Composition and Appointment

### UPSC

- Chairman and other members appointed by the President.
- Number of members is determined by the President (no fixed constitutional number).
- At least half the members should be persons who have held office under the Government of India or of a State for at least ten years.
- Term: six years or until the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier.
- Members are ineligible for further employment under the Government of India or a State (with limited exceptions for the Chairman becoming a member of a State Commission or vice versa under specific conditions).

### State Public Service Commissions

- Chairman and members appointed by the Governor.
- Term: six years or until 62 years of age.
- Similar safeguards regarding removal and post-retirement employment.

## Removal and Safeguards

A member of the UPSC can be removed by the President only on the grounds of misbehaviour after an inquiry by the Supreme Court, or on grounds of insolvency, paid employment outside the duties of office, or infirmity of mind or body. The same high threshold applies, with necessary modifications, to State Commission members (removal by the President on similar grounds).

These provisions give the Commissions a high degree of security of tenure, comparable in spirit to that of judges of the higher judiciary.

## Functions of the UPSC (Article 320)

The UPSC is consulted on:

1. Matters relating to methods of recruitment to civil services and civil posts.
2. Principles to be followed in making appointments, promotions, and transfers.
3. Suitability of candidates for appointments, promotions, and transfers.
4. Disciplinary matters affecting a person serving under the Government of India in a civil capacity (including memorials or petitions relating to such matters).
5. Claims for reimbursement of legal expenses incurred by a civil servant in defending legal proceedings.
6. Claims for award of pension in respect of injuries sustained while serving in a civil capacity.
7. Any other matter specifically referred by the President.

The Commission conducts examinations for recruitment to the All India Services and the Central Services. It also advises on the framing of recruitment rules and on certain service matters.

### Limitations on Consultation

The President may make regulations specifying matters in which it shall not be necessary to consult the Commission. In practice, certain posts and matters are excluded. The advice of the Commission is not binding, but rejection of advice is rare and must be explained in the annual report.

## Annual Report and Accountability

The UPSC submits an annual report to the President, who causes it to be laid before Parliament. The report includes instances where the Commission’s advice was not accepted. This provides a measure of transparency and parliamentary oversight.

State Commissions submit reports to the Governor, who places them before the State Legislature.

## Role in Maintaining Merit and Neutrality

The existence of independent Public Service Commissions is a key institutional arrangement for:

- Ensuring that recruitment is based on merit rather than patronage.
- Protecting civil servants from arbitrary disciplinary action motivated by political considerations.
- Providing a degree of continuity and professionalism in the higher civil services.

The UPSC’s examination system (Civil Services Examination and others) is one of the most visible and competitive merit-based selection processes in the country.

## Challenges and Contemporary Issues

- Increasing volume of recruitment and the need to continuously update examination patterns and syllabi.
- Balancing objectivity with the need to assess broader competencies (ethics, decision-making, domain knowledge).
- Delays in the recruitment cycle and their impact on cadre management.
- Questions regarding the exclusion of certain posts from the purview of the Commission.
- Coordination between UPSC and State Commissions, and the role of the Commission in All India Services that serve both Centre and States.

## Conceptual Significance

The Public Service Commissions embody the constitutional commitment to a professional, merit-based, and relatively insulated civil service. Their independence, functions, and reporting obligations form an important part of the institutional design of Indian democracy and administration. Understanding their constitutional position, powers, and limitations is essential for analysing issues of governance, administrative reform, and the relationship between political executive and permanent executive.
